# How Can I Reduce AI Cheating in My Course?

**The strongest direction in the knowledge base is to rely less on detection and more on structural assessment design, explicit expectations, learning verification, and [[ai-literacy]].** The [[academic-integrity|Academic Integrity]] synthesis reports substantial limitations in AI-text detection and argues that academic integrity in the generative-AI era is increasingly an assessment-design problem rather than simply a detection problem.

## Structural approaches

The [[reducing-ai-misuse|Reducing AI Misuse]] page gives highest priority to structural approaches:

- Use guardrailed AI that favors hints over answers where possible.
- Include meaningful unassisted or authenticated evidence of learning.
- Require drafts, reasoning traces, or other process artifacts when appropriate.
- Create explicit AI-free portions when independent capability is genuinely the construct being assessed.

It also recommends **"think first, AI second, reflect third,"** AI-literacy instruction, and task-specific AI-use declarations that clarify which forms of assistance are acceptable.

## Learning verification

A complementary approach is **learning verification**: occasionally ask students to explain or apply what they submitted rather than trying to prove how it was produced. [[best-response-student-ai-dialog-2026|The Best Response to Student AI Use Is Not Detection, It Is Dialog]] describes short verification conversations, early drafts, reflections, and student videos as possible mechanisms.

That source is a practitioner account, and its institution-wide implementation is not yet equivalent to a controlled efficacy study, so it is best treated as a promising practice rather than definitive causal evidence.
