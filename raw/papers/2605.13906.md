Modeling AI-TPACK in Practice: Insights from Teachers' Multi-Agent Workflow Design

Authors: Yimeng Sun, Haiyang Xin, Shuang Li, Qiannan Niu, Ching Sing Chai, Lingyun Huang, Gaowei Chen
Submitted: 13 May 2026
Category: cs.CY (Computers and Society)
License: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0

Abstract:
This study investigates teachers' design behaviors and cognitive underpinnings when designing multi-agent instructional workflows. Analyzing behavioral logs (N=61), cluster and Markov analyses identified three archetypes: Systematic Optimizers iteratively refining complex architectures; Prolific Creators rapidly prototyping pragmatic tools via scaffolding; and Passive Observers exhibiting polarized expert-novice profiles. Subsequent artifact (n=15) and interview (n=12) analyses reveal AI-TPACK integration emerges from a dynamic interplay of systems thinking, pedagogical beliefs, and self-efficacy, not merely from the possession of discrete knowledge. These findings call for differentiated scaffolding responsive to teachers' cognitive-behavioral diversity.

Three Teacher Archetypes in Multi-Agent Workflow Design:
- Systematic Optimizers: Iteratively refine complex architectures; methodical, architectural focus.
- Prolific Creators: Rapidly prototype pragmatic tools via scaffolding; efficiency-oriented.
- Passive Observers: Display polarized expert-novice profiles; minimal proactive modification.

AI-TPACK Integration Goes Beyond Discrete Knowledge:
The study shows that effective AI-TPACK (Technological, Pedagogical, and Content Knowledge) integration does not arise solely from possessing separate knowledge domains. It emerges from a dynamic interplay of:
- Systems thinking: Ability to conceptualize interactions among multiple AI agents and instructional goals.
- Pedagogical beliefs: Underlying assumptions about teaching and learning that shape design choices.
- Self-efficacy: Confidence in one's ability to orchestrate complex AI-enhanced workflows.

Methodology:
- Behavioral logs from 61 in-service teachers designing multi-agent workflows
- Artifact analysis of 15 workflow designs
- In-depth interviews with 12 participants
- Cluster analysis + Markov chain analysis + qualitative coding

Practical Implications:
- Differentiated scaffolding for distinct cognitive-behavioral profiles
- Move beyond tool-centric training; integrate systems thinking, pedagogical reflection, and self-efficacy development into AI-education courses
