# Explainable Artificial Intelligence in education

**Authors:** Hassan Khosravi, Simon Buckingham Shum, Guanliang Chen, Cristina Conati, Yi-Shan Tsai, Judy Kay, Simon Knight, Roberto Martinez-Maldonado, Shazia Sadiq, Dragan Gašević
**Published:** 2022
**Venue:** Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, 3, 100074
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.caeai.2022.100074
**License:** CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (Open Access)
**Institutions:** The University of Queensland, University of Technology Sydney, Monash University, University of British Columbia, University of Sydney

## Abstract

There are emerging concerns about the Fairness, Accountability, Transparency, and Ethics (FATE) of educational interventions supported by the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) algorithms. One of the emerging methods for increasing trust in AI systems is to use eXplainable AI (XAI), which promotes the use of methods that produce transparent explanations and reasons for decisions AI systems make. Considering the existing literature on XAI, this paper argues that XAI in education has commonalities with the broader use of AI but also has distinctive needs. Accordingly, we first present a framework, referred to as XAI-ED, that considers six key aspects in relation to explainability for studying, designing and developing educational AI tools. These key aspects focus on the stakeholders, benefits, approaches for presenting explanations, widely used classes of AI models, human-centred designs of the AI interfaces and potential pitfalls of providing explanations within education. We then present four comprehensive case studies that illustrate the application of XAI-ED in four different educational AI tools. The paper concludes by discussing opportunities, challenges and future research needs for the effective incorporation of XAI in education.

## Key Sections

### Six Aspects of the XAI-ED Framework

1. **Stakeholders** — Learners, teachers, administrators, parents, researchers, developers, and policymakers each have different explainability needs
2. **Benefits** — Trust, fairness, debugging, usability, learning, regulatory compliance, and adoption
3. **Approaches for Presenting Explanations** — Visual, textual, example-based, counterfactual, feature importance, rule extraction, model simplification
4. **Classes of AI Models** — White-box (decision trees, linear models, rule-based), black-box (neural networks, ensemble methods), and glass-box approaches
5. **Human-Centred Design** — Interfaces must be designed for the specific stakeholder, not just technical accuracy
6. **Potential Pitfalls** — Explanation overload, misleading explanations, confirmation bias, over-trust, and gaming the system

### Four Case Studies

1. **OnTask** — Learning analytics platform; XAI provides instructors with interpretable student risk indicators and rule explanations
2. **OATutor** — Open-source adaptive tutoring system; XAI explains mastery predictions and hint selections
3. **SRES** — Student-facing recommender; XAI explains why certain learning resources are recommended
4. **Connected Learning Analytics (CLA)** — Multimodal collaboration analytics; XAI surfaces group dynamics and individual contributions

### Key Arguments

- Education has *distinctive* XAI needs beyond general AI — learning data is noisy at many levels, and explanations can directly support metacognition
- XAI in education must support learner agency and self-regulated learning, not just system transparency
- The GDPR right to explanation creates a regulatory imperative for XAI in educational contexts
- Explanations can serve as boundary objects between different stakeholders (teachers, students, developers)
