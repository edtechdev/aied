Presents a large-scale descriptive analysis of an AI learning assistant (Syntea) using objective log data from 77,543 higher-education students, characterizing real usage patterns, adoption, and [[student-engagement|engagement]] at scale. The work connects to broader debates about how [[generative-ai]] systems reshape [[student-experience]] and the conditions under which AI support [[scaffolding|scaffolds]] rather than undermines learning. It has direct implications for [[pedagogy-ai-mistakes]] and the risk of [[cognitive-offloading|Over-Reliance]] when assistants absorb too much of the cognitive load. Findings also bear on [[ai-literacy]] and [[self-regulated-learning]], and on how institutions should govern [[student-experience]] and [[academic-integrity]]. Practitioners in [[higher-ed]] and [[teacher-role|teachers]] can use the evidence to calibrate when to deploy [[llm]]-based help and how to pair it with [[feedback]] that preserves [[learning-gains|learning gains]].
