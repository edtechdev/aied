> **Synthesis:** Ocak and Caskurlu (2026) use a multiple case study to explore how in-service K-12 teachers' technical knowledge of AI relates to their trust in using AI in education, and how this trust is shaped by ethical perceptions. Framed within the Ethics dimension of the intelligent-TPACK framework (Celik, 2023), the study examines four ethical constructs — transparency, fairness, accountability, and inclusiveness — as indicators of ethical AI, and analyzes how teachers' trust plays out relative to these constructs. Drawing on written reflections from seven in-service teachers following a two-week, AI-focused online learning module, the findings suggest that without solid foundational technical knowledge, teachers struggle to recognize how human decisions shape AI systems — a prerequisite for trusting and evaluating them.

## Key Findings

**Technical knowledge underpins ethical recognition.** Teachers without solid foundational AI technical knowledge often struggle to recognize how human decisions shape AI systems, which undermines their ability to evaluate and trust AI in educational contexts.

**Four ethical constructs.** The study operationalizes the Ethics dimension of intelligent-TPACK through transparency, fairness, accountability, and inclusiveness, and examines how trust interacts with each.

**Multiple case study design.** Data from seven in-service K-12 teachers' written reflections (after a two-week AI-focused online module in a graduate-level computer science/instructional technology course) illuminate how trust and distrust emerge in practice.

**Trust as a relational outcome.** Teachers' trust in AI is shown to be shaped by their ethical perceptions as much as by technical proficiency — pointing to the interdependence of knowledge and values in [[ai-literacy]].

**Implication for professional development.** The findings argue that AI professional development must build both technical knowledge and ethical fluency so teachers can trust — and appropriately distrust — AI systems, a central aim of the wiki's [[teacher-education]] concept.
