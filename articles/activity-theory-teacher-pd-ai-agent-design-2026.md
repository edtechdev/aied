> **Synthesis:** Xin et al. (2026) report a two-cycle [[formative-assessment|formative]] intervention study addressing the "low [[student-engagement|engagement]] paradox" in teacher [[educational-development|professional development]] (TPD) for [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] AI agent design — why teachers disengage from AI agent creation soon after training. Cycle 1 (N = 218) found that despite completing comprehensive TPD, **87% of teachers stopped creating agents within three weeks**; behavioral tracking and interviews identified systemic contradictions — not capacity deficits — as the source of psychological need frustration. Cycle 2 (N = 26) implemented a Cultural-Historical Activity Theory (CHAT) and [[self-determination-theory|Self-Determination Theory]] (SDT)-driven redesign directly targeting the diagnosed contradictions, achieving synchronized enhancement of both capacity and willingness. The authors reframe implementation failure as a rational response to need-thwarting systems and offer a replicable CHAT-SDT diagnostic framework.

## Key Findings

- **The low engagement paradox.** 87% of teachers who completed comprehensive TPD stopped creating pedagogical AI agents within three weeks — a disengagement that points to systemic, not individual, causes.
- **Systemic contradictions, not capacity deficits.** Using CHAT, the study diagnosed the disengagement as arising from activity-system contradictions (primary/secondary/tertiary types) rather than a lack of teacher capability or training.
- **CHAT + SDT redesign.** Cycle 2 redesigned the TPD activity system to directly target diagnosed contradictions, using Self-Determination Theory to address psychological need frustration. The redesign synchronized gains in both teacher capacity (ability) and willingness (motivation) for sustained AI agent creation.
- **A replicable CHAT-SDT diagnostic framework.** The study offers a diagnostic framework for transformative professional development that treats implementation failure as a rational response to need-thwarting systems.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Diagnose the activity system before blaming capacity: all 218 teachers in Cycle 1 completed the two-day workshop and 87% still stopped creating agents within three weeks, which CHAT traced to tool-subject, rule-object, and subject-community contradictions rather than skill deficits.
- **Instructors.** Remove the contradictions rather than restating the expectation: mandatory collaboration produced autonomy frustration and post-workshop isolation produced relatedness frustration, and Cycle 2's redesign fixed the structure instead of asking teachers to try harder.
- **Faculty developers.** Design need support into the workshop — autonomy through choice architecture (individual or collaborative modes, topic selection), competence through progressive scaffolding, an embedded AI Q&A assistant and discipline-specific templates, and relatedness through peer consultation and community sharing — plus low-barrier peer-sharing spaces, since sequential "train, then motivate" designs decouple capacity from willingness.
- **Administrators.** Protect creation time, for example dedicated weekly periods for agent iteration, and grant tool-selection agency, because autonomy deficits are what drove the 87% disengagement after training.
- **Researchers.** Trace why a specific [[agentic-ai|pedagogical agent]] adoption succeeded or failed by mapping contradictions onto psychological needs, rather than attributing generic barriers.

## Limitations

- Participants came from one coastal city in Southern China with well-developed educational infrastructure and voluntarily enrolled in district-wide PD, so the diagnosed contradictions belong to one district's activity system.
- Cycle 2 (N = 26) was a separate cohort rather than a follow-up of Cycle 1's 218 teachers; the authors state the smaller number reflects the separate-cohort design, so the group whose disengagement was diagnosed is not the group whose capacity and willingness improved.
- Capacity and willingness are self-report 7-point Likert measures (AI-TPACK, IPACK, Attitude toward AI; α = 0.88–0.94) administered at the start of workshop Day 1 and the end of Day 2, capturing immediate change with no classroom implementation or student outcome measured.
- The Basic Needs Satisfaction Survey was post-only, leaving no baseline need-frustration measure, and the researchers designed and facilitated both workshop cycles themselves; interview coding reached Cohen's κ = 0.78.
