> **Synthesis:** Nourashrafi, Alavinia and Darvishi (2026) use a sequential-explanatory mixed-methods design to compare the pedagogical and content quality of AI-generated versus human-developed assessment tasks in English language teaching. Twenty experienced EFL teachers rated 52 tasks from six human and six equivalent AI-generated lesson plans using a TPCK-aligned rubric, followed by interviews. Quantitative results show no statistically significant differences across eight quality criteria; teachers preferred AI for grammar and vocabulary tasks but favoured human development for reading, listening, writing and speaking. Qualitative analysis highlights AI's speed, range and rule-based competence while stressing the need for [[human-ai-collaboration|teacher mediation]] for personalization and contextual relevance. AI tools are positioned as useful initial resources rather than complete replacements.

## Key Findings

**No significant overall difference.** Across eight quality criteria, ratings showed no statistically significant difference between AI-generated and human-developed assessment tasks — AI is already near-parity for routine task generation in EFL.

**Skill-specific preferences.** Teachers preferred AI-generated grammar and vocabulary tasks but favoured human-developed reading, listening, writing and speaking tasks — a division of labour suggesting AI excels at rule-based content.

**TPCK-aligned evaluation.** The TPCK rubric foregrounds pedagogical and content knowledge in task quality, providing a structured lens on [[tpack]] in assessment design.

**AI strengths.** Qualitative analysis found AI's advantages lie in speed of generation, diversity/range of tasks, and managing rule-based content — efficiency gains relevant to [[automated-question-generation]] and [[automated-assessment]].

**Teacher mediation essential.** Personalization and contextual relevance require human intervention, positioning AI as an initial resource rather than a full replacement — a [[human-ai-collaboration|human-AI collaboration]] model for EFL assessment design.
