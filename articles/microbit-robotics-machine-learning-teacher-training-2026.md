> **Synthesis:** Sáez-López, García-Jiménez, and de Lara García-Cervigón (2026) assess the design and implementation of innovative educational robotics activities in higher education with 105 Primary Education degree students from two Spanish universities. The activities involved simple tasks, visual block programming, machine learning, and the construction of sensors and actuators, fostering computational concepts and practical tasks. Using a quasi-experimental design with a control group of 39 students and the Coding, Robotics, and Machine Learning Test (CRMT) plus three scales, the experimental group improved their performance and results with the practical intervention. The authors conclude there are training gaps in programming in higher education, making coding essential in initial teacher training.

## Key Findings

**Quasi-experimental gains.** The experimental group using micro:bit + Maqueen/Nezha educational robotics improved performance and results compared to a control group.

**Broad activities.** Activities spanned simple tasks, visual block programming, machine learning, and construction of sensors and actuators, covering computational concepts and practical implementation.

**Teacher-training gap.** The study identifies training gaps in programming in higher education, arguing that coding is essential in initial teacher training.

**Measures.** The CRMT test and three scales provided descriptive and inferential statistics (t-test, Mann-Whitney U).

**Implication.** Educational robotics and machine-learning activities should be embedded in [[teacher-education]] so future teachers can teach computational thinking and programming, linking to [[programming-education]] and [[educational-robotics]].
