## A Fragmented Field

Generative LLMs are widely used by students, yet research on their role in learning remains conceptually fragmented. Existing studies describe student interactions using heterogeneous and often incompatible categories, ranging from speculative use cases to fine-grained dialogue acts, making it difficult to compare findings across studies and to understand when and how LLM use supports learning.

## Method and Variation

This rapid scoping review examines prior work on student-LLM interactions with a focus on how interaction types are defined and constructed. Across 46 categorizations drawn from 33 studies, the authors identify substantial variation in data sources, category-construction approaches, and units of analysis, reflecting divergent theoretical commitments and research purposes.

## Toward a Shared Taxonomy

The lack of shared terminology motivates a convergent taxonomy of learning-oriented use. The review situates these interaction categories within [[self-regulated-learning]] and [[learning-analytics]], supporting [[research-methods-aied]] for synthesizing evidence on [[ai-feedback-quality]] and the conditions under which student-LLM engagement produces learning rather than mere completion.
