> **Synthesis:** This study investigated different collaboration modes and how they interact with competition to influence [[computational-thinking|computational thinking]] learning, group metacognition and in-game behaviours. In a quasi-experimental 3×2 factorial design with 148 seventh-grade students, results revealed significant main effects of collaboration mode and an interaction effect between collaboration and competition. The cooperative group with competition achieved the highest CT outcomes, while competition undermined the collaborative turn-taking mode.

## Key Findings

- **Collaboration mode matters:** Different collaboration modes (cooperative vs. turn-taking) significantly affected computational thinking outcomes, group metacognition, and in-game behaviour.
- **Competition interacts with collaboration:** Competition boosted the cooperative group's CT outcomes (the highest overall) but undermined the collaborative turn-taking mode — showing that not all collaboration benefits from competition.
- The findings inform how [[collaborative-learning|collaboration]] and competition should be designed together in [[computational-thinking|computational thinking]] games for [[k-12|seventh-grade]] [[ai-education|learners]].
