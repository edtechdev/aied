> **Synthesis:** Korchak, Costley, and Fanguy (2026) explore how university students use generative AI (Gen-AI) during the preparation of a group writing assignment. Semi-structured interviews with 10 postgraduate students majoring in AI research, analyzed using Braun and Clarke's thematic analysis with two independent coders, revealed three themes: (1) group strategy — how students establish and implement strategies for Gen-AI use, (2) the division of labor between students and Gen-AI, and (3) the interplay of collaboration and AI use. The findings illuminate the diverse ways groups appropriate Gen-AI within collaborative writing, from AI-first to student-led approaches.

## Key Findings

**Three emergent themes.** Thematic analysis revealed three themes: group strategy for Gen-AI use, division of labor between students and AI, and the interplay of collaboration and AI use.

**Group appropriation of Gen-AI.** Students adopted varied strategies for using Gen-AI in collaborative writing, reflecting different balances of AI and human effort ("AI writes, we collaborate" vs. "we write, AI supports").

**Collaborative learning context.** The study links Gen-AI use to collaborative learning benefits (reduced preparation time, enhanced product quality, accelerated learning) in group writing assignments.

**Student-Gen-AI interaction.** Rich qualitative accounts detail how postgraduate students negotiate roles with Gen-AI during collaborative writing.

**Implication.** Instructors should understand the diverse group strategies for Gen-AI to design collaborative writing assignments that balance AI support with genuine [[collaborative-learning]] and student authorship — relevant to [[writing-education]] and [[agency]].
