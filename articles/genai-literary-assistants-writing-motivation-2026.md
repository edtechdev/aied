> **Synthesis:** Wang, Wang, and Liu (2026) use a mixed-methods design to examine the multifaceted effects of integrating generative AI (GAI) literary assistants into ninth-grade writing instruction, focusing on students' writing motivation, flow experiences, and overall writing performance. While quantitative measures of general writing motivation showed no statistically significant increase, qualitative data revealed a marked surge in task-specific engagement and situational motivation — particularly during the revision phase — and demonstrably improved writing quality. The authors also critically examine the risks of over-reliance on AI, which could diminish engagement in areas requiring creative expression and critical thinking.

## Key Findings

**Improved writing quality.** Integrating GAI literary assistants demonstrably improved students' writing quality, particularly in revision.

**Situational vs. general motivation.** While general writing motivation did not rise significantly, task-specific engagement and situational motivation surged, especially during revision — pointing to the context-dependence of AI's motivational effects.

**Flow experiences.** GAI supported students' flow experiences during writing, enhancing task engagement and immersion.

**Over-reliance risk.** The authors caution that over-reliance on AI could diminish student engagement, particularly in areas requiring creative expression and critical thinking.

**Pedagogical recommendation.** The study concludes with nuanced pedagogical recommendations for integrating GAI writing assistants while preserving students' creative and critical capacities — relevant to the wiki's [[writing-education]] and [[over-reliance]] concerns.
