# How motivation and roles influence metacognitive [[student-engagement|engagement]] in student-GenAI interaction

> **Synthesis:** This qualitative study analyzes 120 authentic [[student-ai-interaction|student-GenAI interaction]] logs and retrospective interviews from 24 Chinese undergraduates to examine how [[motivation|motivational orientations]] and perceived GenAI roles shape [[metacognition|metacognitive engagement]]. Using [[network-analysis|epistemic network]] and lag sequential analysis, the authors find that intrinsically motivated students predominantly positioned GenAI as an [[teacher-role|instructor]] or collaborator, while extrinsically motivated students treated it as a replacement tool. Intrinsic motivation and collaborator framing were associated with higher-order metacognitive engagement (evaluation, elaboration) and a complete metacognitive chain, whereas replacement-tool use produced no higher-order metacognitive connections. The authors conclude that the metacognitive consequences of [[generative-ai|GenAI]] use are conditioned by motivation and role perception rather than inherent to the technology.

## Core Finding

The quality and structure of metacognitive engagement during GenAI interaction is systematically conditioned by students' motivational orientation and the role they assign to the tool. [[metacognition|Metacognitive]] effects of GenAI are **not uniform or inherent** — they depend on whether students are intrinsically or extrinsically motivated and whether they frame GenAI as an instructor, enhancement tool, replacement tool, or collaborator.

## Motivation and GenAI Role (RQ1)

Drawing on [[self-determination-theory|self-determination theory]], the study adopts a binary intrinsic–extrinsic contrast. Intrinsically motivated students predominantly positioned GenAI as an instructor or collaborator; extrinsically motivated students overwhelmingly treated it as a replacement tool. This difference is not merely attitudinal but unfolds turn-by-turn in how students structure authentic interactions. Extrinsically motivated students were more likely to use GenAI for task completion and cognitive offloading.

## Metacognitive Engagement by Motivation (RQ2)

Intrinsic motivation was associated with significantly higher engagement in higher-order metacognitive dimensions — [[critical-thinking|evaluation]] and elaboration — forming reflective-iterative co-occurrence networks. Extrinsic motivation produced task-completion-oriented patterns concentrated in lower-order processing (orientation, planning, monitoring), where students submitted prompts and monitored outputs for usability but rarely interrogated, critiqued, or extended them. This suggests the [[ai-misuse-learning-harm|cognitive offloading]] risk is more pronounced among extrinsically motivated students.

## GenAI Roles Map to Metacognitive Profiles (RQ3)

- **Collaborator:** the only role with a complete sequential chain spanning all six metacognitive categories (Orientation→Elaboration) plus an Elaboration self-loop, reflecting iterative knowledge construction. Present in only 7.5% of logs.
- **Enhancement Tool:** a partial higher-order chain (M4→M5→M6); the student retains cognitive ownership while using GenAI as a supplement.
- **Instructor:** a receptive comprehension-checking pattern (Orientation-Monitoring, Execution-Monitoring) with near-absent evaluation and elaboration.
- **Replacement Tool:** only self-loops with no higher-order connections and entirely absent Evaluation/Elaboration — cognitive closure.

The augmentation–automation distinction is supported: retaining [[agency]] over one's thinking yields substantially richer metacognitive engagement than full delegation.
