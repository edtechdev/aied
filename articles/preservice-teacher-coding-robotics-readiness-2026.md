> **Synthesis:** Govender (2026) investigates pre-service teachers' readiness to teach Coding and Robotics in South Africa through their behavioural intention to teach the subject. Analysing survey data from 235 pre-service teachers via Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) within the UTAUT framework, the study examines Performance Expectancy (PE), Effort Expectancy (EE) and Social Influence (SI) as predictors of Behavioural Intention (BI). Results show Effort Expectancy has a moderate effect on behavioural intention, while Performance Expectancy and Social Influence have weak effects. The misalignment between curriculum reform and teacher-education policy raises concerns about pre-service teachers' readiness to implement Coding and Robotics effectively, contributing to the growing literature on [[educational-robotics]] and teacher preparedness.

## Key Findings

**UTAUT predictors.** Within the UTAUT framework, Effort Expectancy (EE) moderately predicts pre-service teachers' behavioural intention to teach Coding and Robotics, while Performance Expectancy (PE) and Social Influence (SI) show only weak effects.

**Method.** A structured 5-point Likert survey of 235 pre-service teachers analysed with Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM), a robust approach for an emerging educational context.

**Policy misalignment.** The lack of alignment between curriculum reform and teacher-education policies raises concerns about readiness to implement Coding and Robotics effectively — a systemic issue for [[cs-education]] and [[educational-robotics]] rollout.

**Teacher readiness focus.** The study foregrounds the role of pre-service teachers' preparedness and the factors affecting their readiness to teach the subject, relevant to [[teacher-role]] and [[tpack]].

**Contribution.** It extends research on educational robotics and coding by connecting behavioural-intention modelling to teacher-education readiness in the South African context.
