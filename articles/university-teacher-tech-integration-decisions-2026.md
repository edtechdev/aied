> **Synthesis:** Nguyen et al. (2026) use a multiple case study design to explore how individual and contextual aspects shape university teachers' decisions on [[edtech-platform|technology integration]] into teaching. Interviewing ten teachers across nine courses at a large Dutch research university, they adopt an ecological systems perspective and find that integration decisions are influenced by a wide range of contextual forces beyond specific technology affordances, negotiated across nested systems. Disciplinary culture — exemplified by mathematics — shapes multiple systems of decision-making, while the chronosystem of the post-pandemic era actively configures those nested systems. The study extends technology-integration research by systematically foregrounding context in the university setting.

## Key Findings

**Contexts beyond affordances.** University teachers weigh a range of contextual influences — social influence, institutional conditions, facilities — that extend well beyond what any specific technology affords, countering purely tool-centric accounts of adoption.

**Ecological systems lens.** Decisions are embedded within and negotiated across the nested systems described by ecological systems theory: micro-, meso-, exo-, macro- and chronosystem influences all bear on integration choices.

**Disciplinary culture as pivotal.** Disciplinary norms, exemplified by mathematics, informed multiple ecological systems and shaped technology-related decisions — pointing to the importance of [[discipline-specific-aied]] and subject-context in integration.

**Chronosystem in the post-pandemic era.** Historical/temporal context actively reconfigured the nested systems within which technology integration decisions are made, reflecting the lasting imprint of pandemic-era shifts on [[teacher-role|teacher practice]].

**Method.** A heterogeneous multiple case study of ten teachers and nine courses (first semester 2022–2023) at a multidisciplinary Dutch research university, yielding rich, contextualised accounts of decision-making.
