> **Synthesis:** Wang et al. (2026) address the risk of "performance without learning" in generative AI use by proposing the IDEA framework — Intent, Deconstruction, Expression, and Adaptation — a theory-informed [[metacognition|metacognitive]] scaffold that embeds prompting within a cycle of planning, monitoring and regulation. In an exploratory quasi-experimental pilot with 42 undergraduates, IDEA-trained students produced higher-quality prompts and final AI outputs than peers given structured prompt-engineering instruction, with interaction logs showing observable enactment of the framework's core activities. On unaided tasks five days later the IDEA group retained advantages on selected tasks. The framework reframes GenAI use from passive content outsourcing into deliberate, evaluative, learner-regulated interaction.

## Key Findings

**The IDEA scaffold.** The framework operationalises metacognitively regulated GenAI use through four moves: articulate goals and constraints (Intent), decompose the task into essential processes (Deconstruction), communicate requirements through structured prompts (Expression), and critically evaluate and iteratively refine AI outputs (Adaptation). Prompting is thereby nested inside a wider self-regulatory cycle, extending [[self-regulated-learning]] into the GenAI era.

**Design.** A quasi-experimental pilot compared IDEA-based instruction (n = 21) with structured prompt-engineering instruction (n = 21) across immediate GenAI-assisted academic tasks, plus unaided tasks administered five days later. Outcomes included prompt quality, final output quality, and interaction-record evidence of framework enactment.

**Results.** IDEA-trained students produced higher-quality prompts and final AI-generated outputs on immediate tasks, and their interaction records showed observable enactment of the framework's core activities. On the unaided five-day tasks, the IDEA group demonstrated advantages on selected tasks, suggesting some transfer beyond the scaffolded setting.

**Significance.** The paper positions IDEA as a practical antidote to [[cognitive-offloading]] and over-reliance: by requiring learners to plan, monitor and regulate their AI use, it aims to preserve agency and deep learning while capturing GenAI's efficiency. It complements [[ai-literacy]] interventions by offering a teachable metacognitive procedure rather than a content checklist.

**Limits.** The pilot is small, exploratory and single-site; the authors call for replication across disciplinary contexts and for longer-term learning-outcome measurement.
