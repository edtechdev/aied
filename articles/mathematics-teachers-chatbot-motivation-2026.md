> **Synthesis:** Weinhandl, Andic, Wijaya, Bleckenwegner, Riegler, Baldinger, Mayrhofer, and Helm (2026) investigate what motivates mathematics teachers to use chatbots for teaching purposes. Using a digital questionnaire study of 448 teachers and structural equation modeling, they found that Performance Expectancy, Effort Expectancy, and Social Influence are key drivers of mathematics teachers' motivation to adopt chatbots — consistent with the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT). The study extends technology-acceptance research to the specific domain of AI chatbots in mathematics teaching.

## Key Findings

**UTAUT drivers.** Performance Expectancy, Effort Expectancy, and Social Influence significantly shape mathematics teachers' motivation to use chatbots for teaching.

**Domain-specific acceptance.** The study applies the UTAUT framework to AI chatbots specifically in mathematics education, extending general technology-acceptance findings to a domain where conversational AI for problem-solving is novel.

**Large sample.** A survey of 448 mathematics teachers provided robust evidence on chatbot adoption motivation.

**Implication for teacher education.** Understanding what motivates teachers to adopt AI chatbots is essential for designing [[teacher-education]] programs and support that encourage effective integration of conversational AI in [[math-education]].
