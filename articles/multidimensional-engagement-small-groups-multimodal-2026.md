> **Synthesis:** Zou et al. (2026) develop and validate a human-centered framework for measuring multidimensional small-group engagement in a game-based Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (CSCL) environment. Grounded in sociocultural perspectives, the framework integrates multimodal data — discourse, interaction logs, and linguistic indicators — to assess behavioural, collaborative and disciplinary engagement through theory-informed human interpretation. Human-annotated ratings serve as ground truth for evaluating whether supervised machine learning models can approximate multimodal interpretive judgements. Results show average collaborative and disciplinary engagement positively predict group performance, whereas behavioural engagement alone does not. The study advances [[multimodal]] [[learning-analytics]] by proposing a generalizable, human-centered workflow that pairs human interpretation with model-assisted scalability.

## Key Findings

**Multidimensional engagement.** The framework distinguishes behavioural, collaborative and disciplinary engagement — not a single aggregate — enabling finer-grained analysis of small-group interaction in game-based CSCL.

**Human-centered multimodal measurement.** Discourse, interaction logs and linguistic indicators are integrated through theory-informed human interpretation, positioning [[human-in-the-loop-ai|humans in the loop]] as essential to valid, contextually grounded engagement assessment.

**Model-assisted scalability.** Human-annotated ratings ground the evaluation of three supervised learning algorithms, establishing a pathway toward scalable multimodal interpretive judgement while preserving interpretability.

**Predictive validity.** Multiple regression shows average collaborative and disciplinary engagement positively predict group performance, while behavioural engagement alone does not significantly relate to learning outcomes — a key finding for [[engagement-metrics]] design.

**Generalizable workflow.** The paper proposes a replicable workflow integrating multimodal interpretation with model-assisted scalability, with qualitative analysis of group responses illustrating the associations between engagement dimensions and outcomes.
