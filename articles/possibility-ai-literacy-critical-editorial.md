> **Synthesis:** Pangrazio (2026) offers a critical editorial examining whether AI literacy is a meaningful or even achievable goal. Tracing the history of literacy from its elite origins through mass institutionalization, the article argues that AI literacy has been positioned as a "cure-all" — a solutionist, normative response to the complex and evolving phenomenon of AI. The analysis questions what literacy actually means when applied to opaque, rapidly changing AI systems, and whether current AI literacy discourse can move beyond simplified skill-based framings to address the epistemological and political dimensions of AI.

## Key Findings

- Critiques the positioning of AI literacy as a solutionist "cure-all" for the societal challenges of AI
- Traces the history of literacy to argue that literacy is never neutral — it is tied to power, access, and cultural authority
- Questions whether conventional literacy framings can accommodate the opacity, fluidity, and unpredictability of AI systems
- Argues for rethinking AI literacy beyond normative skill checklists toward critical engagement with the politics and epistemology of AI
