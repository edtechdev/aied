> **Synthesis:** Ikram, Hanefar, Saleem, and Zulfiqar (2026) conducted a PRISMA-guided systematic review with a meta-literature component, drawing on 31 Scopus-indexed peer-reviewed articles (2013–2025) on [[ai-education|AI in education]] and [[personalized-learning|personalized learning]]. The review traces the field's evolution from 1970s–1980s rule-based [[intelligent-tutoring|intelligent tutoring systems]] and the 1990s emergence of [[learning-analytics]] through contemporary [[generative-ai]] and [[llm]] systems driving an "Education 5.0" vision of learner-centered, human-AI collaborative learning. Findings reveal a transition in research leadership from China, the USA, and Europe toward broader Asia (China six papers, India four), a predominance of qualitative methods (14 of 31), and interdisciplinary spread across Social Sciences (30.2%), Computer Science (26.2%), and Engineering. Six themes organize the literature: personalized learning pathways and student engagement, predictive analytics and [[assessment]] for early intervention, [[teacher-role]] and digital competence, [[ethics]] and data privacy governance, generative AI in Education 5.0, and [[equity-in-ai-education]] and socio-cultural impact. The authors stress that effective integration requires explicit alignment of AI features with [[pedagogy]] (constructivist and social learning theory), differentiated [[faculty-development|professional development]], and ethical governance frameworks addressing algorithmic bias and unequal access. Methodologically, the review is descriptive and thematic rather than a quantitative meta-analysis, and it excludes gray and non-English literature.

## Core Finding

AI-driven personalized learning is a maturing, interdisciplinary field whose benefits are real but conditional: AI-enabled adaptive systems show medium-to-large cognitive effects (g = 0.50–0.70), yet these are heavily moderated by implementation quality, context, learner characteristics, and study design. The review's central contribution is showing that progress has shifted from a narrow technological narrative to an integrated concern for pedagogical grounding, teacher readiness, and ethical governance. It positions [[personalized-learning]] as the flagship AIED application and identifies generative AI as the next transformative catalyst, while cautioning that its expansion — combined with persistent concerns about educational standards in developing countries — may create new institutional pressures. The field is dominated by qualitative exploration of lived experience, signaling that researchers currently privilege understanding how AI shapes learning over scalable, data-driven causal evidence.

## Personalized Learning Pathways and Student Engagement

The most prominent theme is AI's capacity to construct personalized learning experiences tailored to individual needs, providing real-time [[feedback]], flexibly adjusting content, and encouraging creativity and motivation. Reported outcomes include increased vocabulary retention, more accurate syntax, deeper comprehension of complex concepts, and heightened [[student-engagement]]. AI-driven gamification and continuous evaluation outperform traditional instruction. This theme grounds [[personalized-learning]] as an empirically supported pathway that benefits both academic performance and learners' motivation to take an active role in their learning.

## Predictive Analytics, Assessment, and Early Intervention

AI systems project academic progress, group learners by risk level, and enable early intervention strategies, including AI-supported oral assessments and simulation-based instruction that improve both [[assessment]] validity and inclusivity. Predictive algorithms and recommendation models equip educators to develop targeted strategies that reduce dropout rates and support struggling learners. This signals a broader movement toward data-driven, preventative approaches in education, linking [[learning-analytics]] and [[student-modeling]] to actionable instructional support.

## Teacher Training, Digital Competence, and Pedagogical Innovation

The review finds that AI's effectiveness depends heavily on pedagogical alignment rather than the technology alone. Both pre-service and practicing teachers require preparation to adopt AI for personalized education, and teacher [[agency]] and digital literacy emerge as pivotal for equitable integration. The authors argue for making pedagogical assumptions explicit, aligning system design features with constructivist and social learning principles that guide how learners actively engage, collaborate, and regulate their learning. This directly implicates [[teacher-role]], [[pedagogy]], and [[self-regulated-learning]].

## Ethics, Governance, and Data Privacy

A cross-cutting theme is the importance of [[ethics]] and governance in AI adoption. Recurring concerns include data privacy, algorithmic bias, unequal access, and epistemic trust. Learners voiced apprehension about inaccuracies and misuse of AI-generated information, especially in high-stakes fields such as medicine. Scholars propose robust governance structures, transparency protocols, and regular audits, and educators favor hybrid models that integrate AI with conventional methods rather than replacing them. This frames ethical practice as essential to equitable and trustworthy educational outcomes, linking to [[equity-in-ai-education]].

## Generative AI and Education 5.0 Transformation

The review operationalizes Education 5.0 as a learner-centered paradigm integrating advanced digital technologies, including generative AI, with humanistic values, inclusivity, and societal relevance. Generative AI is seen as pivotal in delivering adaptive instruction, personalizing content creation, and catering to learners with unique needs, including twice-exceptional and neurodivergent learners through strengths-based approaches. In professional disciplines like nursing and medicine, prompt engineering and AI tutoring strengthen analytical and clinical skills. This positions [[generative-ai]] and [[llm]] systems not merely as supportive tools but as catalysts reshaping learning design and inclusivity across [[higher-ed]] and professional education.
