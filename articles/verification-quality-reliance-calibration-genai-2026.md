> **Synthesis:** This Mini Review argues that "critical" or "responsible" AI use is an over-broad label that collapses seven analytically separable measurement targets: epistemic evaluation, verification initiation, process quality, verification success, reliance decisions, immediate task performance, and independent learning. [[trust-calibration|Reliance calibration]] is defined as an output-contingent classification of a reliance decision against an independently adjudicated standard of AI-output quality — not a chronological stage and not the same thing as [[trust]]. Across 493 deduplicated records and 14 priority empirical studies, no study jointly measured verification success and the subsequent reliance decision against such a reference standard, and delayed retention or transfer was rare.

## Key Findings

- **Adjacent measures are not interchangeable.** Eleven targeted Web of Science Core Collection searches (2022–2026, updated through 7 August 2026) produced 772 exports and, after DOI, accession-number, and title deduplication, **493 unique records**; 114 review records were identified, 10 closest reviews read in full, and 14 priority empirical studies audited. The review's central claim is a construct-validity one: neighboring indicators should support distinct interpretations rather than be treated as interchangeable.
- **The key direct-linkage gap.** Among the **14 priority empirical studies, none** jointly measured verification success and then classified the subsequent reliance decision against an independently adjudicated AI-output quality standard; delayed retention or transfer was also uncommon. Trust and reliance diverge: Amaro et al. (2024) found that false [[generative-ai|ChatGPT]] information changed reported trust but did not measure later acceptance or rejection of a specific recommendation.
- **Checking can coexist with unresolved error.** In Zhang et al. (2025), **7 of 15 students failed Task 1 despite self-checking or external cross-checking (46.7%; Wilson 95% CI 24.8%–69.9%)** — a task-specific estimate the authors explicitly refuse to generalize as a failure rate, but direct evidence that initiation is not success.
- **Acceptance decisions cannot be scored without ground truth.** Chen and Lou (2026) logged accept/reject decisions for ChatGPT [[feedback]] among **78 translation students** but lacked independent expert evaluation of each suggestion, so acceptance cannot be classified as appropriate nor rejection as justified.
- **Behavioral designs with a reference standard exist but are adjacent.** Dávila et al. (2025) used advice correct on **50% of items** (N = 59 one-shot; N = 82 across a four-week period) and found advice weighting moderated by [[prior-knowledge|prior knowledge]] and gender; Zheng et al. (2025) analyzed **315 student–ChatGPT quiz conversations** (182 students), separating appropriate/inappropriate reliance and a "failed application" pattern showing that appropriate reliance does not guarantee successful task execution.
- **[[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] does not uniformly increase checking.** Rheu and Cho (2025) reported a dimension-specific pattern: understanding [[llm]] processes was associated with more [[self-report-measures|self-reported]] fact-checking, whereas some forms of [[self-efficacy]] and feature knowledge were associated with stronger machine heuristics and *less* checking.
- **Correction is a learning opportunity only when it demands substantive work.** Olney and Cade (2025) found in an error-correction paradigm that effort during correction mattered for learning; simple answer substitution is unlikely to deliver the same benefit.

## Study Design & Method

Mini Review (Frontiers in Psychology, published 11 September 2026) combining a targeted, non-systematic evidence synthesis with a conceptual measurement framework. The authors ran 11 complementary topic searches in the Web of Science Core Collection (2022–2026, all editions) updated through 7 August 2026; 772 exports deduplicated to 493 unique records. Two authors independently screened the corpus and reconciled judgments. Priority was given to studies that operationalized a focal target, linked adjacent targets, or exposed a measurement boundary. The evidence set deliberately mixes small [[qualitative-research|qualitative]] studies, direct higher-education GenAI studies, adjacent AI-advice/HCI designs, and foundational or mechanistic sources. Supplementary File 1 holds search strategies, screening and appraisal details, operational definitions, calibration metrics, mixed-effects analysis guidance, multiplicity/reliability recommendations, an illustrative sample-size calculation, and reporting standards. A focal Table 1 maps representative studies (Urban 2025; Choi 2025; Chen & Lou 2026; Zhang 2025; Dávila 2025; Zainuddin 2026; Hou 2025; Pudasaini 2026; Zheng 2025; Hu 2026) onto verification, reliance, and task/learning outcome columns, with a diagnostic implication for each.

## Implications

- Evaluate interventions for the *specific* process they target. A lateral-reading lesson should be assessed for effects on verification strategy and, separately, verification success; a confidence prompt may mainly shift [[metacognition|metacognitive]] monitoring; a forced-delay interface may change reliance decisions; an explanation or error-correction activity affects learning only if it induces substantive processing. A single self-report measure of "responsible use" would obscure these different mechanisms.
- Designs should specify or adjudicate AI-output quality, capture verification initiation, code process quality, score verification success, record the accept–revise–reject decision, and evaluate that decision against AI quality — then, where learning is intended, follow immediate performance with unaided retention or transfer.
- Reducing inappropriate acceptance must also be tested for unintended rejection of correct assistance; the stated educational objective is effective, proportionate verification that supports calibrated reliance while preserving the cognitive work required for learning.
- Boundary conditions to model explicitly: prior/domain knowledge, learner characteristics, task stakes, task verifiability, verification costs, accountability, AI system/model/version and prompt configuration, and multidimensional AI literacy. The authors stress these mappings are analytic expectations, not established moderator effects.

## Limitations

- Searches were targeted rather than systematic, so the review does not estimate prevalence, effect sizes, or causal sequence.
- The evidence set mixes small qualitative studies, direct higher-education GenAI studies, adjacent AI-advice/HCI designs, and foundational or mechanistic sources, limiting the strength of inference.
- The seven-target map is an analytic ordering, not a validated causal model; the ordering is explicitly not a fixed temporal sequence (learners may accept then verify, or revisit evaluation after post-hoc doubt).
- The 7 August 2026 search cut-off plus rapid model evolution mean the map should be read as a time-bounded snapshot.
- Several audited studies did not standardize or report the AI system, model version, or configuration, which limits comparability of reference standards.
