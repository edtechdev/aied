> **Synthesis:** Passey et al. (2026a) develop a framework for social justice-oriented digital transformation in educational ecosystems serving marginalized populations. After clarifying digital transformation, justice-oriented transformation and digital ecosystems, they outline the challenges marginalized groups face and construct a two-part framework: five core principles — equity, inclusion, participation through community engagement in design, ethical responsibility (transparency and accountability), and digital competence fostering empowerment and agency — plus five pillars: accessibility and infrastructure, inclusive pedagogy and curriculum, capacity building, ethical governance, and monitoring and evaluation. The framework grounds technology-enhanced inclusivity in explicit social-justice commitments rather than purely technical or economic rationales.

## Key Findings

**Five core principles.** Equity, inclusion, participation (engaging marginalized communities in design processes), ethical responsibility (transparency and accountability), and digital competence (fostering empowerment and agency) anchor the framework's normative commitments.

**Five pillars.** Accessibility and infrastructure, inclusive pedagogy and curriculum, capacity building, ethical governance, and monitoring and evaluation operationalise the principles across the educational ecosystem.

**Social-justice grounding.** The framework explicitly positions digital transformation as justice-oriented — confronting the [[digital-divide]] and [[equity-in-ai-education|equity]] challenges faced by marginalized populations rather than treating technology as neutral infrastructure.

**Design participation.** A distinctive feature is centring marginalized communities in design processes, aligning with [[culturally-relevant-pedagogy]] and participatory design commitments.

**Governance and monitoring.** Ethical governance and monitoring/evaluation pillars connect the framework to [[governance]] and accountability requirements for educational digital transformation. The companion paper applies the framework to four community-led case studies.
