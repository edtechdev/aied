> **Synthesis:** Baran, Dilek, Ziba, and Xiao (2026) use a design-based research approach to examine how principles of Human-Centered AI (HCAI) and critical AI literacy can inform the design of professional learning resources for teacher educators. Across three cycles of needs analysis, prototyping, and refinement with seven teacher educators, they identified three convergent design needs: (a) improving instructional efficiency and effectiveness while preserving professional judgment, (b) modeling responsible and ethical AI integration, and (c) reforming teacher education pedagogies with AI. These findings were translated into a five-module curriculum operationalizing critical AI literacy competencies through seven HCAI-informed activities, including educator-in-the-loop tasks that strengthen professional judgment, transparency, and equity-oriented decision-making.

## Key Findings

**Three convergent design needs.** Design-based research identified: improving instructional efficiency while preserving professional judgment, modeling responsible/ethical AI integration, and reforming teacher-education pedagogies with AI.

**Five-module curriculum.** Findings translated into a five-module curriculum covering foundational AI knowledge, ethics and algorithmic bias, pedagogical integration, implementation/guidelines/policy, and human-centered AI in education.

**HCAI-informed activities.** Seven HCAI-informed activities, including educator-in-the-loop tasks, strengthen professional judgment, transparency, and equity-oriented decision-making.

**Teacher educators as pivotal.** Teacher educators shape how preservice teachers understand, evaluate, and integrate AI, yet rarely receive systematic preparation to lead this work.

**Implication.** The curriculum models critical [[ai-literacy]] for teacher educators, directly supporting the wiki's [[teacher-education]] concept and its emphasis on ethical, human-centered AI integration.
