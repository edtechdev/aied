> **Suitability of Artificial Intelligence Supported Lesson Plans from the Perspective of Science Education Experts** — Karaismailoglu, Surmeli, and Yildirim (2026) evaluate the instructional quality of [[generative-ai|AI]]-generated lesson plans for sixth-grade [[science-education|science]] through the expert judgment of eleven Turkish science education specialists, using a qualitative case study with descriptive quantitative elements. Two platforms — a general-purpose model (ChatGPT-4) and an education-focused tool (Teacher's Buddy) — each produced a plan for a "Sustainable Living and Biodiversity" unit aligned to the [[k-12|sixth-grade]] Engineering Design-Based Learning (EDBL) model and four skill domains of Turkey's revised [[curriculum-design|curriculum]]. Experts found both tools support key competencies (conceptual, domain-specific, literacy, and social-emotional skills) but fall short of fully capturing EDBL's iterative, reflective, and process-oriented stages, positioning AI-generated plans as useful drafts that demand pedagogical adaptation and [[teacher-role|teacher judgment]].

## Key Findings

- **Both platforms handle content generation but miss iterative process.** Eleven experts scored both plans against EDBL stages and five skill domains. Both scored highest for "Presenting the solution" yet lowest in Engineering Skills (general-purpose x̄ = 37.45; education-focused x̄ = 41.45), revealing a shared weakness in simulating the [[problem-solving|iterative, process-oriented design competencies]] — prototyping, failure analysis, revision, and [[feedback|reflective retesting]] — that are central to constructivist [[learning-theories|learning models]] like EDBL.

- **The education-focused platform aligned more closely with curriculum and sequencing.** Platform C2 (Teacher's Buddy) received higher average scores across all eight quality criteria and outperformed the general-purpose model in feedback-intensive stages (Evaluating & Retesting, Making/Testing Prototype), skill scaffolding (Domain-Specific x̄ = 50.72, Literacy x̄ = 49.45), [[curriculum-design|curriculum alignment]], and instructional sequencing — evidence that embedding [[pedagogy|pedagogical structure]] into AI systems yields meaningfully better instructional output.

- **Expert preferences did not track raw scores.** Although C2 scored higher numerically (7 experts preferred C2, 4 preferred C1, 1 neutral), some experts favored the general-purpose plan for its stronger [[student-engagement|social-emotional]] emphasis and [[student-experience|student-centered design]] — indicating expert judgment weighs affective and contextual dimensions, not just structural fidelity.

- **Experts largely endorsed conditional use, not rejection.** Only 3 of 11 judged plans directly "Applicable," 7 judged them "Can be applied by correction," and 1 "Should not be applied" (citing lack of originality and resemblance to existing ministry plans). Qualitative themes consistently framed AI plans as usable drafts that require teacher moderation for [[situated-learning|contextual]] and pedagogical depth.

- **Contextualization and language are real limits.** Neither platform adequately adapted plans to the local socio-cultural context of Mersin, Turkey even when prompted; and both generated pedagogically richer outputs from English than from Turkish prompts, exposing a [[digital-divide|digital equity]] concern where English-language proficiency shapes the pedagogical quality of AI tools.

- **Expert coding achieved substantial reliability.** Two researchers independently coded open-ended applicability judgments with quadratic weighted kappa κ = 0.79 (95% CI 0.68–0.90), indicating substantial agreement for the qualitative [[qualitative-research|thematic analysis]].
