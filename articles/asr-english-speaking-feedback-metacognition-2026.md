> **Synthesis:** Chen, Zeng, Ou, and Zhong (2026) examine how Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) technology shapes college English [[language-learning|speaking]] instruction by analyzing its interaction with [[metacognition|metacognitive strategies]] and learner differences. Using [[self-report-measures|questionnaires]], factor analysis, and structural equation modeling, they find that accurate error correction and well-designed reflection tasks significantly improve [[feedback|feedback]] processing and reflective behavior, while frequent ASR use mainly boosts reflection rather than motivation. ASR accuracy enhances [[motivation|intrinsic motivation]] but does not directly drive reflection; language proficiency moderates the effects, with more proficient learners internalizing feedback more effectively.

## Key Findings

- **[[ai-feedback-quality|Feedback quality]], not quantity, drives learning.** Accurate error correction and structured reflection tasks significantly improve feedback internalization and reflective behavior.
- **Usage frequency boosts reflection, not motivation.** Frequent ASR use primarily increases reflective behavior; it does not independently raise learning motivation.
- **Recognition accuracy affects motivation but not reflection.** Technical precision alone is insufficient to promote deeper cognitive engagement — accuracy enhances intrinsic motivation without directly driving reflective learning.
- **Reflective behavior and motivation both predict speaking gains.**
- **Language proficiency is a key moderator.** More proficient learners internalize ASR feedback more effectively and achieve greater oral gains; weaker learners may need additional [[scaffolding]] and support.

## Study Design & Method

The study surveyed college English learners using ASR-based speaking practice. Data were collected via questionnaire, then analyzed with factor analysis and structural equation modeling to assess how ASR-generated feedback quality, structured reflection tasks, and usage frequency influence feedback internalization, reflective learning, and motivation — with language proficiency tested as a moderator (via simple slopes and interaction plots).

## Implications for AI in Education

For [[language-learning]] and [[intelligent-tutoring]], the study shows ASR's value depends on thoughtful [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] integration rather than the tool alone. Educators should prioritize feedback quality over quantity, scaffold reflection through structured tasks, and differentiate instruction by proficiency level. Developers should design systems with pedagogically sound feedback (e.g., articulatory explanations rather than simple error flags) and adaptive features that personalize feedback complexity — connecting to [[self-regulated-learning]] and [[personalized-learning]] design.

## Limitations

- **Cross-sectional design** precludes causal inference; experimental and longitudinal designs are needed.
- **Single-university Chinese sample** limits generalizability to other cultural/educational contexts.
- **ASR treated partly as monolithic** rather than isolating the unique contributions of specific design elements.
