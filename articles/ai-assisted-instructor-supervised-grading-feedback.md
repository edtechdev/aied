> **Synthesis:** Cruz, da Silva, and Mamede (2026) presented the design, deployment, and evaluation of a GPT-4o-based grading and feedback pipeline for a Master's-level Information Systems Project Management course, developed using Design Science Research. The artefact orchestrates submission collection, rubric-based scoring with rubric-linked qualitative feedback, and analytics storage across six case-study assignments, with the AI grader supplementing rather than replacing [[teacher-role|instructor]] grading. Across 362 submissions from 66 students, the AI reproduced the instructor's mark within a pre-specified 0.5-point tolerance in 83% of cases (MAE 0.31). The pipeline operated at EUR 0.55 per student per assignment and delivered feedback within 24 hours, supporting LLMs as scalable assistants for [[formative-assessment]].

## Key Findings

- Across 362 submissions from 66 students, the AI reproduced the instructor's mark within the 0.5-point tolerance in 83% of cases, with a mean absolute error of 0.31 points and small systematic bias of −0.16.
- Chance-corrected agreement (ICC 0.49) was moderate at best, spanning the poor-to-moderate range, indicating limits on grading reliability.
- Among 112 survey responses (31% response rate), about 80% rated the feedback useful and 77% accurate.
- The pipeline operated at EUR 0.55 per student per assignment and delivered feedback within 24 hours, deployed as an instructor supplement under GDPR safeguards.
