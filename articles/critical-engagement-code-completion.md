Hutchison et al. (2026) develop and validate a method for measuring critical engagement with AI code completion tools in educational settings. Using behavioral signals (time-to-accept, edit distance from suggestion) and embedded attention checks, they find that the majority of students accept AI code suggestions passively, without critically evaluating correctness or appropriateness. This 'tab-and-go' behavior directly threatens the development of programming skills, as students bypass the cognitive effort required for [[transfer-of-learning]]. The work provides a methodological toolkit for [[formative-assessment]] in AI-augmented programming courses, enabling instructors to detect when students are [[cognitive-offloading|over-reliant]] on AI. The findings connect to [[student-experience]] research in [[stem-education]] by showing that the mere availability of AI tools does not lead to productive learning — structured pedagogical interventions are required to ensure students engage critically rather than deferring to AI output.
