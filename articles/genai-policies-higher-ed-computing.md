# A Comparative Analysis of Institutional and Course Generative AI Policies within Higher Education: Implications for Instruction in Computing Education

**Amrita Ganguly, Aditya Johri, Nora McDonald, Areej Ali, et al. (2026)** - George Mason University; Montgomery College. arXiv preprint.

## Key Findings

- Compares institutional-level GenAI policy documents against course-level instructor policies, exposing a gap between top-down guidance and classroom uptake in [[higher-ed]].
- Centers computing education (CS courses), where GenAI adoption is especially high and the implications for student learning are pronounced - links to [[ai-ed-implementation]].
- Shows instructors are often forced to improvise local policies, raising coordination questions for [[ai-governance-education]].
- Connects to broader [[policy-maker]] and [[equity]] debates about who sets AI-use rules and how consistently they apply across courses.
- Informs [[teacher-ai-adoption-confidence]] by showing uneven instructor readiness to translate policy into everyday practice.
