> **Synthesis:** Zhu, Yang, and Yang (2026) investigated how different AI interaction roles function as [[scaffolding]] strategies in AI-assisted mathematical modelling. In a randomized within-subjects experiment with 26 university students, they compared five AI roles—Tutor, Teaching Assistant, Peer, Excellent Student, and Struggling Student—on modelling competency, role preference, and learning experience. Students demonstrated higher modelling competency with the Peer and Teaching Assistant roles, which fostered [[collaborative-learning|collaborative]] reasoning, yet expressed stronger preferences for the Tutor and Excellent Student roles offering explicit guidance. The study reveals a notable divergence between learning performance and role preference, highlighting the importance of balancing cognitive scaffolding with collaborative sense-making in mathematics education.

## Key Findings

- Students demonstrated higher modelling competency when interacting with the Peer and Teaching Assistant roles, which fostered [[collaborative-learning|collaborative]] reasoning and co-construction of ideas.
- Despite lower performance, students expressed stronger preferences for the Tutor and Excellent Student roles, which provided more explicit guidance and structured explanations.
- The Struggling Student role was consistently perceived as least supportive across both performance and preference measures.
- The findings highlight a divergence between performance and pedagogical role preference, informing design of adaptive, learner-centered AI-supported modelling environments.
