> **Synthesis:** Aponte, Vargas Sánchez, Chiappe, and Martínez-Pérez (2026) offer a conceptual and critical synthesis arguing that AI supports teachers' socio-emotional [[educational-development|professional development]] only when it functions as relational infrastructure rather than a symbolic substitute for human accompaniment. They introduce relational densification as an evaluative criterion for whether AI-supported initiatives strengthen the professional relationships tied to teacher wellbeing — [[trust]], mentoring, peer support, collaboration, psychological safety, and reduced isolation — and distinguish among AI-mediated functions (coordination, recommendation, [[learning-analytics|analytics]], [[conversational-ai|conversational agents]], emotional AI) by their differentiated ethical and [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] risks.

## Key Findings

- **AI's value to teacher development is relational, not just efficiency-based.** AI supports socio-emotional professional growth only as relational infrastructure, expanding, protecting, and sustaining human professional relationships.
- **Relational densification is a new evaluative criterion.** Teacher-development initiatives should be judged by whether they strengthen trust, mentoring, peer support, collaboration, psychological safety, and reduce isolation — not only by efficiency or [[personalized-learning|personalization]].
- **Teacher–student downstream effects.** Strengthened teacher relationships have downstream implications for students through classroom climate, pedagogical responsiveness, and socio-emotional support.
- **AI-mediated functions carry differentiated risks.** Coordination systems, recommendation tools, analytics, conversational agents, and emotional AI each pose distinct ethical and pedagogical risks.
- **Critical AI literacy as a socio-emotional competence.** The synthesis foregrounds affective data [[governance]], the political economy of educational AI, cultural and linguistic specificity, and [[ai-literacy|critical AI literacy]] as a socio-emotional competence.

## Study Design & Method

This is a conceptual and critical synthesis (theoretical analysis) rather than an empirical study. The authors build an argument that oscillating between technocentric optimism and pre-emptive rejection misses the real question: under what relational conditions does AI genuinely support teacher wellbeing and emotional intelligence in an era of uncertainty.

## Implications for AI in Education

For [[teacher-role|teacher]] development and [[teacher-education|professional development]], the paper reframes evaluation: rather than asking whether AI-driven PD is efficient or personalized, ask whether it densifies professional relationships. For [[well-being|teacher wellbeing]] and [[ethics]], it warns that affective data governance and the political economy of educational AI must be scrutinized, and that AI must not substitute for human accompaniment in emotionally demanding teaching work.

## Limitations

- **Conceptual, not empirical** — proposes criteria and a framework without testing them against data.
- **Broad scope** spanning socio-emotional development, governance, and political economy means depth in any single area is limited.
