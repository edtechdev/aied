> **Synthesis:** Baez, Buchner, Ullrich, and Schallert-Vallaster (2026) explore how primary and lower secondary school students perceive AI in learning, and how student and teacher motivation and self-efficacy relate to those perceptions. Using questionnaire data from 907 students and 53 class teachers in German-speaking Switzerland, analyzed with doubly multilevel structural equation modeling (ML-SEM), they found that students' motivation to learn with digital media is significantly linked to their perception of AI at the individual level. Students' self-efficacy is positively associated with their motivation, with girls exhibiting lower self-efficacy to learn with digital media than boys. At the class level, teachers' motivation to integrate digital media was positively associated with student motivation.

## Key Findings

**Motivation drives AI perception.** Students' motivation to learn with digital media is significantly linked to their perception of AI at the individual level.

**Self-efficacy and motivation.** Students' self-efficacy is positively associated with their motivation, but girls exhibited lower self-efficacy for learning with digital media than boys — a gender gap relevant to [[equity-in-ai-education]].

**Teacher-level influence.** At the class level, teachers' motivation to integrate digital media in teaching was significantly positively associated with student motivation, highlighting the teacher's role.

**Multilevel design.** Doubly multilevel SEM on a large Swiss sample (907 students, 53 teachers) provides robust evidence on student and teacher AI perceptions.

**Implication.** Fostering students' self-efficacy and motivation — especially among female students — plus teacher AI professional development can promote more positive perceptions of AI in learning, informing [[ai-literacy]] programs in [[k-12]] settings.
