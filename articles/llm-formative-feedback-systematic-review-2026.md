> **Synthesis:** Maier, Seibold, and Klotz (2026) conduct a qualitative systematic literature review following PRISMA guidelines, synthesizing evidence from 47 peer-reviewed empirical studies (2022–2026, 121 distinct research questions) on LLM-generated formative feedback. They examine (1) theoretical framings and dominant research questions, (2) methodological and contextual characteristics, and (3) technological trends and empirical findings on feedback quality, feedback processing, and learning outcomes. The reviewed literature is primarily grounded in formative feedback theory, self-regulated learning, feedback literacy, and human-AI interaction frameworks, and is dominated by short-term experiments, quasi-experimental classroom studies, and expert-based evaluations.

## Key Findings

**Scalable formative feedback.** Since late 2022, instruction-tuned LLMs have enabled scalable generation of elaborated, formative feedback for text-based student assignments across domains.

**Theoretical grounding.** The reviewed literature is grounded in formative feedback theory, [[self-regulated-learning]], [[feedback-literacy]], and human-AI interaction frameworks.

**Broad evidence base.** Forty-seven peer-reviewed empirical studies (121 research questions, 2022–2026) were synthesized following PRISMA guidelines.

**Methodological profile.** Studies are dominated by short-term experiments, quasi-experimental classroom studies, and expert-based evaluations.

**Feedback dimensions.** The review covers feedback quality, feedback processing, and learning outcomes, providing a comprehensive account of how LLM-generated feedback is designed and evaluated — central to the wiki's [[feedback]] and [[formative-assessment]] concepts.
