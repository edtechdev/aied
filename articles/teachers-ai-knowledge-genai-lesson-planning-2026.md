> **Synthesis:** Velander (2026) investigates how K-12 teachers conceptualize and enact AI-related professional knowledge during GenAI-supported lesson planning, focusing on the relationship between articulated knowledge and enacted practice. Data were collected through a pre-workshop questionnaire (N=61), interaction logs of teachers' lesson planning with a GenAI system (~1,300 prompt-response pairs), and group-based SWOT reflections. Analysis using the Intelligent TPACK (I-TPACK) framework and a collaborative problem-solving framework reveals that teachers primarily articulate AI-related competence in operational terms, revealing a gap between the knowledge they claim and the knowledge they enact in practice.

## Key Findings

**Articulated vs. enacted knowledge gap.** Teachers primarily articulate AI-related competence in operational skills, but their enacted practice during GenAI lesson planning reveals a gap between claimed and enacted knowledge.

**Large interaction dataset.** Approximately 1,300 prompt-response pairs from teachers' GenAI lesson planning were analyzed, providing rich evidence on teacher-AI interaction patterns.

**Intelligent-TPACK framework.** The study applies the I-TPACK framework (Velander uses "I-tpack") to analyze teachers' articulated knowledge and a collaborative problem-solving framework to examine interaction patterns.

**K-12 focus.** Sixty-one K-12 teachers participated, situating the findings in authentic classroom planning contexts.

**Implication.** Professional development must move beyond operational AI skills to build pedagogically meaningful knowledge that teachers actually enact, connecting to the wiki's [[teacher-education]] and [[ai-literacy]] concepts.
