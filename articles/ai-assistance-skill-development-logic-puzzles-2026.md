> **Synthesis:** While AI assistance can improve immediate task performance, it may also undermine the longer-term development of skills. In a controlled logic-puzzle experiment with on-demand AI assistance, Wu and colleagues vary AI request costs and find that lower-cost assistance induces more frequent AI use. Participants who request AI assistance during the access phase perform worse at the task after assistance is removed, and their subsequent unassisted performance is overestimated when predicted from earlier AI-assisted performance. Using a Bayesian latent ability model, they find that greater independent problem-solving effort is associated with larger gains in latent ability, consistent with skill development being weaker when AI assistance substitutes for independent reasoning.

## Key Findings

1. Lower-cost AI assistance induces more frequent AI use by participants.
2. Participants who request AI assistance during the access phase perform worse at the task after assistance is removed.
3. Unassisted performance is overestimated when predicted from earlier AI-assisted performance.
4. Greater independent problem-solving effort during the AI-access phase is associated with larger gains in latent ability.
5. Skill development is weaker when AI assistance substitutes for independent reasoning, consistent with [[cognitive-offloading]] dynamics.
