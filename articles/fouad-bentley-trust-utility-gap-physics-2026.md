> **Synthesis:** Fouad & Bentley (2026) survey 81 introductory [[physics-education|physics]] students and find a striking 50-percentage-point [[trust-calibration|trust-utility gap]]: 91% use AI for coursework but only 41% [[trust]] AI physics explanations — evidence of domain-calibrated skepticism, not uncritical adoption. A [[mixed-methods-research|mixed-methods]] survey of 81 introductory [[physics-education|physics]] undergraduates reveals a 50-point [[trust-calibration|trust-utility gap]]: 91% [95% CI: 83%–96%] use AI for physics coursework, but only 41% [95% CI: 30%–52%] [[trust]] AI-generated physics explanations. Thematic analysis identified eight themes; the most distinctive finding was that 40% of [[qualitative-research|qualitative]] respondents spontaneously articulated where AI fails — visual-spatial reasoning, circuit analysis, and abstract physical reasoning — aligning with known benchmarks. Students show [[trust-calibration|domain-calibrated skepticism]] rather than uncritical adoption, and 65% prefer optional AI integration.

- 91% use AI for physics, only 41% trust it — 50-point [[trust-calibration|trust-utility gap]]
- Students show [[discipline-specific-aied|domain-specific]] skepticism, not naive acceptance
- 40% spontaneously identified AI failure modes: visual-spatial reasoning, circuits, abstract physics
- Student [[trust-calibration|trust calibration]] tracks [[ai-literacy|AI competence]] boundaries (consistent with Kortemeyer benchmarks)
- 65% prefer optional over mandatory AI integration
- Verification gap identified: self-reported vs actual verification behavior
