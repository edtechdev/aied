> **Synthesis:** Bai and Hsieh (2026) use the AI-[[tpack|Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge]] (AI-TPACK) framework, Social Cognitive Theory, and professional identity theory to model AI teaching innovation behavior among college teachers. In an SEM analysis of 898 Chinese university teachers, all AI-TPACK dimensions except AI technological knowledge and integrative knowledge were significantly associated with AI teaching innovation behavior. Professional identity and [[ai-literacy]] partially mediated the competence→innovation relationship, while teaching [[self-efficacy]]'s mediating role received only partial support; the professional-identity→innovation link was stronger among teachers with lower [[generative-ai|GenAI]] use frequency.

## Key Findings

- **AI-TK and integrative AI-TPACK did not directly predict innovation**; the other AI-TPACK dimensions did — GenAI-supported teaching innovation depends less on isolated technical AI knowledge and more on pedagogically and disciplinarily embedded AI competence.
- **Professional identity and AI literacy are key mediators.** Both showed direct associations with AI teaching innovation behavior and significant mediating roles between competence structures and innovation.
- **Teaching self-efficacy's mediation was only partially supported.**
- **Low-frequency AI users showed a stronger professional-identity→innovation link**, though the authors caution this finding should be interpreted carefully.
- **Theoretical integration** of AI-TPACK, Social Cognitive Theory, and professional identity theory yields a multiple-mediation model of GenAI-supported teaching innovation.

## Study Design & Method

A convenience sample of 898 Chinese university teachers from eight comprehensive universities completed standardized scales assessing AI-TPACK dimensions, teaching self-efficacy, professional identity, AI literacy, and AI teaching innovation behavior. Data were analyzed with structural equation modeling and multi-group analysis (comparing high vs. low GenAI use frequency).

## Implications for AI in Education

The findings reframe faculty support for AI integration in [[higher-ed]]: isolated AI technical training is insufficient. [[educational-development|Faculty development]] should build pedagogically and disciplinarily embedded AI competence, strengthen [[teacher-role|professional identity]], and cultivate [[ai-literacy]] — since these psychological and identity factors are what convert competence into innovative teaching. For administrators, the differential findings by usage frequency suggest support strategies may need to differ for early versus frequent adopters.

## Limitations

- **Convenience sample, [[self-report-measures|self-reported]] survey data** from Chinese college teachers; generalizability limited.
- **Cross-sectional design** cannot establish causal ordering.
- **The low- vs. high-frequency moderation finding is borderline** and should be interpreted cautiously.
