> **Synthesis:** Ziying, Yongchun, and Qiaoping (2026) conduct a PRISMA-ScR scoping review of 55 empirical studies (2020–2025) examining how artificial intelligence is applied in the professional development of preservice teachers. They map a diverse landscape of AI applications, technologies, and subject distributions, finding that AI acts as both a *cognitive partner* and a *practice simulator*. AI enhances instructional design, subject-specific instruction, practical teaching skills, evaluation efficiency, reflective practice, critical thinking, technology integration, and pedagogical innovation. However, the integration is a "dual-edged force": alongside the benefits sit challenges around data privacy, bias, over-reliance, and the need for ethical and pedagogical grounding in teacher preparation.

## Key Findings

**Broad application landscape.** Across 55 empirical studies, AI supports preservice teacher development in eight competency areas: instructional design, subject-specific instruction, practical teaching skills, evaluation efficiency, reflective practice, critical thinking, technology integration, and pedagogical innovation.

**AI as cognitive partner and practice simulator.** AI functions both as a cognitive partner (supporting reasoning and design) and as a safe practice simulator (offering simulated classroom environments for honing practical teaching skills) — dual roles that reshape teacher education by providing personalized, scalable, and evidence-based training pathways.

**Reflection and feedback gains.** AI provides data-driven feedback that sharpens preservice teachers' reflective skills and evaluation efficiency, enabling more targeted self-assessment during training.

**Challenges and ethical concerns.** The review surfaces a complex web of challenges, including data privacy, algorithmic bias, and the risk of over-reliance, indicating that AI integration in teacher education must be ethically and pedagogically grounded rather than adopted uncritically.

**Implication for teacher preparation.** The findings argue that teacher-education programs should intentionally design AI-enhanced training to preserve the human educator's role while leveraging AI's personalization and scalability — a core concern for the wiki's [[teacher-education]] and [[teacher-role]] concepts.
