> **Synthesis:** Jukiewicz (2026) presents a large-scale, side-by-side comparison of 18 contemporary Large Language Models in the automated grading of programming assignments, drawing on over 6,000 student submissions collected across four years of an introductory programming course. Analyzing grade distributions, mean-score differences, variability (stricter vs. more lenient grading), and the consistency and clustering of grading patterns, the study evaluated models from Anthropic, DeepSeek, Google, and OpenAI. Statistical analyses revealed clear and systematic differences in grading behavior across models, with distinct grading patterns ranging from more lenient to more restrictive styles, and models from the same vendor tending to cluster together.

## Key Findings

**Large-scale comparison.** Eighteen LLMs were systematically compared on over 6,000 programming submissions, the first such large-scale side-by-side assessment.

**Vendor clustering.** Models from the same vendor tended to cluster together in grading behavior, suggesting shared algorithmic characteristics.

**Lenient vs. restrictive grading.** Distinct grading patterns emerged, ranging from more lenient to more restrictive evaluation styles across models.

**Systematic differences.** Correlation, agreement, and clustering analyses revealed clear and systematic differences in grading behavior, with implications for fairness and consistency.

**Implication.** The choice of LLM architecture and vendor materially affects automated grading outcomes in [[programming-education]], a key consideration for [[automated-grading]] in [[cs-education]].
