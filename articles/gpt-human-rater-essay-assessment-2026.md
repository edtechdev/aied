> **Synthesis:** Wu, Chu, and Hsu (2026) investigate whether a GPT-based Automated Essay Scoring (AES) system can complement human raters in second-language (L2) writing assessment. Using a Many-Facet Rasch Measurement (MFRM) model, they analyzed ratings from 20 human raters — 10 native English-speaking (NES) and 10 non-native English-speaking (NNES) — and GPT across four analytic criteria (Content, Organization, Grammar, Vocabulary) on 181 university L2 essays. Results indicate raters demonstrated variability and potential rater bias; GPT exhibited high internal consistency but range compression at the upper end compared to humans. The authors argue GPT can supplement rather than replace human judgment, reframing it as a calibrated scoring agent.

## Key Findings

**Rater variability and bias.** Human raters demonstrated variability and potential bias that varied by linguistic background, underscoring persistent assessment reliability challenges.

**GPT's internal consistency.** GPT exhibited high internal consistency but range compression at the upper end of the score scale compared to human raters.

**Criterion-level differences.** GPT's strengths and limitations varied across the four analytic criteria (Content, Organization, Grammar, Vocabulary), with a content-strict profile, reduced score dispersion, and weaker grammatical alignment.

**Complementary role.** GPT-based AES is best framed not as a wholesale substitute for human judgment but as a calibrated scoring agent and consistency stabilizer that dampens rater variability.

**Implication.** The study informs how [[automated-essay-scoring]] and LLM-based assessment can complement human raters in large-scale and classroom settings while guarding against bias.
