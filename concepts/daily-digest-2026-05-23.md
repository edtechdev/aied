## Ingested Papers

### 1. Faster Completion, Less Learning: Generative AI Reduced Study Time on Math Problems
**Rismanchian et al. (2026)** — arXiv:2605.21629 — 📄 [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21629)

**Finding:** First large-scale behavioral evidence (3.2M ALEKS interactions) that generative AI has reduced student study time by 27% and caused a 25% decline in proctored retention measures. The authors introduce "cognitive surrender" to describe passive, unreflective AI dependency at population scale. → [[generative-ai-reduced-study-time-math]]

### 2. Exploring LLMs for Automated Assessment of Student Self-Explanations in Programming Education
**Lekshmi-Narayanan et al. (2026)** — arXiv:2605.21614 — 📄 [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21614)

**Finding:** Rigorous empirical comparison of LLM-based vs. semantic similarity methods for binary classification of student self-explanation correctness in programming education. Addresses the gap in domain-specific, balanced-class datasets for automated scoring. → [[llm-automated-assessment-student-self-explanations]]

### 3. The Efficiency-Gain Illusion: People Underestimate AI Use and Overestimate Its Benefits
**Yu et al. (2026)** — arXiv:2605.22687 — 📄 [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22687)

**Finding:** Three preregistered studies (N=2,691) documenting systematic miscalibration: people underestimate their AI usage rate and overestimate time savings, creating a self-reinforcing overreliance feedback loop. Provides a cognitive mechanism for the over-reliance phenomenon. → [[efficiency-gain-illusion-ai-overreliance]]
