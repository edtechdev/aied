# AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance

## Core Finding

Through randomized controlled trials (N=1,222) across mathematical reasoning and reading comprehension tasks, Liu et al. provide **causal evidence** that brief AI assistance (~10 minutes) produces two harmful effects:

1. **Reduced persistence**: Users give up more easily on difficult tasks after using AI
2. **Impaired unassisted performance**: People perform significantly worse without AI after using it

Crucially, AI *does* improve short-term performance — the harm is to what happens when the AI is removed. This creates a dangerous illusion: AI feels helpful in the moment while eroding the user's independent capability.

## Mechanism

The authors posit that AI conditions people to expect immediate answers, denying them the experience of working through challenges. Unlike human mentors who scaffold learning and track progress toward long-term goals, current AI systems are "fundamentally short-sighted collaborators" optimized for instant, complete responses.

Persistence — the willingness to struggle with difficult problems — is foundational to skill acquisition and one of the strongest predictors of long-term learning. By undermining it, AI assistance may produce superficial short-term gains at the cost of deeper learning.

## Implications

- AI systems should be redesigned to prioritize **long-term competence scaffolding** over immediate task completion
- The brief 10-minute window before effects emerge is alarming — even casual AI use may produce lasting effects
- Educational AI tools need persistence-preserving designs (hints, partial solutions, Socratic dialogue) rather than direct answer provision
- Performance metrics alone are insufficient for evaluating AI educational tools; unassisted post-test performance must be measured
