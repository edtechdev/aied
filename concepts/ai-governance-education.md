> **AI governance education** — how institutions and educators translate AI capability into responsible-use frameworks, policy, and the teaching of those frameworks. It spans institutional policy (acceptable-use, assessment, and data rules), the [[regulation]] and [[privacy]] landscape that shapes them, and education about governance itself so that students, faculty, and leaders understand and apply responsible-AI expectations.

AI governance in education operates at two levels that the wiki treats together: the *institutional rules* that govern AI use (policies, acceptable-use frameworks, [[assessment]] and declaration requirements) and *education about those rules* (preparing people to navigate them). Governance without education risks being unenforced or opaque; education without governance lacks teeth.

### How AI governance education appears in the research

- **Institutional policy and acceptable use:** [[genai-policies-higher-ed-computing|Institutional GenAI policy analysis]] documents how universities translate AI capability into acceptable-use frameworks and assessment rules — and the gap between institutional ambitions and the support needed to meet them.
- **Assessment and declaration frameworks:** [[genai-declaration-frameworks-higher-education|AI declaration frameworks]] and [[genai-assessment-governance|assessment governance]] address how institutions regulate AI use in assessed work, connecting directly to [[academic-integrity]].
- **Ethics and coordination:** [[ethical-ai-higher-ed-game-theory|Coordination-game frameworks]] model how ethical AI use emerges from collective faculty and student choices, while [[ai-uk-higher-education-policy-2026|UK AI policy research]] and [[raza-farooq-aied-review-2020-2025|the AIED review]] situate governance in the wider policy landscape.
- **Agentic and literacy dimensions:** [[agentic-literacy-debt|Agentic literacy debt]] shows how autonomous agents create structural AI-literacy gaps that governance education must address.
