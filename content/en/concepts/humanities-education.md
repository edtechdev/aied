> **Humanities education** — the teaching of history, philosophy, literature, languages, classics and religious studies, the disciplines whose object is interpretation rather than procedure. [[generative-ai|Generative AI]] matters here less as a tutor of content than as an epistemic mediator: it can produce a plausible reading instantly, which makes authorship, source judgment and the reader's position the things a course has to protect. The cluster sits under [[discipline-specific-aied|AI in the disciplines]] beside [[social-sciences-education|social science education]], which studies people in aggregate, and beside [[arts-and-media-education|arts and media education]] and [[music-education|music education]], where learning is embodied in a made artifact. Coverage is concentrated in [[higher-ed|higher education]] and in single-course studies.

## Questions to Consider

- If a model can supply a competent reading of a text in seconds, what is the course teaching — the reading, or the judgment about which reading to trust?
- Voicu distinguishes AI-dependent, AI-enhanced and AI-critical interpretation. Where do your students land, and what would move them toward the critical end?
- An audit found 58.6% of assessment items in Arts and Humanities units highly exposed to generative AI. Which of your assessments would survive that exposure, and which are exposed because of their format rather than their subject?
- When a history course restricts what a model may say about a source, what is the restriction protecting — the student's judgment, or the teacher's conclusion?

## Introduction

The humanities are the cluster of disciplines that interpret human culture: history, philosophy, literature, languages, classics and religious studies. Their signature pedagogy is the seminar, and their signature assessment is an argument defended in prose. That makes them the part of the curriculum where generative AI's central capability — fluent, plausible text — lands most directly on the thing being graded.

Two boundaries matter for reading the evidence. The first is the arts: [[arts-and-media-education|arts and media education]] and [[music-education|music education]] cover disciplines where learning is embodied in a made artifact, and the argument there turns on craft and performance rather than on interpretation. The second is the social sciences: [[social-sciences-education|social science education]] covers politics, economics, management and environmental studies, where the object of study is people in aggregate and the method is often quantitative. The humanities share a method with neither, and the studies below are about textual and historical interpretation specifically.

The [[philosophy-of-ai-in-education|philosophy of AI in education]] supplies the terms this cluster argues in: epistemic authority, authorship, and what counts as a defensible reading of a text.

## How AI changes interpretive work

- **Interpretation becomes externalized.** [[voicu-ai-interpretive-cognition-ssh-2026|Voicu (2026)]] describes generative AI as an epistemic mediator that reconfigures meaning-making, authorship and learner [[agency|agency]], through externalization of interpretive cognition, hybridization of authorship and distributed epistemic agency. The proposed response is developmental: teach toward AI-critical interpretation rather than banning the tool.
- **Source assembly moves to agents.** [[capodieci-humanities-hil-2026|Capodieci et al. (2026)]] document humanities students using agentic AI to assemble sources and draft, which shifts the assessment question from whether text was generated to whether the interpretation can be attributed to the student.
- **History teaching can filter the model.** [[paternalistic-filter-llm-history-education|Research on LLMs in history education]] examines how AI shapes historical reasoning and source interpretation, including through filters that protect students from claims a teacher would contest. [[dumancic-tool-to-method-ai-literacy-history-2026|Dumančić (2026)]] traces the adjacent move from teaching the tool to teaching the method, in a case study built on survey and thematic analysis.
- **Literary creation can be scaffolded rather than replaced.** [[incipit-axiom-grounded-scaffolding-literary-creation-2026|Incipit]] is a grounded scaffolding system for literary creation, built on a knowledge graph and an [[llm|LLM]], and its design problem is the discipline's: keep the student's invention in the loop while the model supplies structure.
- **The generic version of the problem is task format.** In a simulation of 148 texts, memoranda and revision defenses scored 3.16 and 3.10 on argumentative visibility against 2.63 for position essays, because the format itself required source explanation or a response to objections ([[liu-argumentative-work-visible-genai-assessment-2026|Liu & Liu (2026)]]).

The clearest framework for the humanities' own problem comes from [[stornaiuolo-humanities-genai-integrity-framework-2026|Stornaiuolo and Elkins]], who argue that [[academic-integrity|integrity]] and [[ai-literacy|critical digital literacy]] are one educational problem rather than a rule problem plus a skills problem. Their connective construct is evaluative judgment: the student's capacity to judge the quality of an interpretation, which is what both integrity and literacy require and what detection cannot supply. Because essays, summaries and commentaries are both the humanities' assessment instruments and the model's strongest outputs, they conclude that the discipline meets AI at its core, and that assessment has to require demonstrated judgment rather than better policing of the old genres.

## What the sector evidence shows

The exposure of humanities assessment is measured rather than assumed. [[villanueva-ai-vulnerability-assessment-audit-2026|Villanueva (2026)]] audited 15,587 Arts and Humanities unit records across Australia's Group of Eight universities and found 58.6% of 2026 assessment items highly exposed to [[generative-ai|GenAI]]. Take-home essays and research reports accounted for 71.4% of that exposure once weighted by marks, so the exposure is concentrated in one artifact rather than spread across the curriculum.

[[corbin-essay-time-genai-2026|Corbin et al. (2026)]] narrow the claim: the assessed essay had already contracted into a thesis-and-evidence report, so what GenAI simulates is a report genre rather than essayistic thinking. Read together, the two studies say the vulnerable artifact is the one whose thinking is already formulaic.

Institutional strategy is also documented. [[ai-digital-transformation-liberal-arts-lingnan-2026|Qin (2026)]] describes Lingnan University repositioning itself as a research-intensive liberal arts institution in the digital era, with mandatory GenAI literacy for undergraduates and a required first-year course covering latent spaces, diffusion models, prompting, fine-tuning, bias and misinformation. The argument is that the shift is intellectual rather than technocentric.

## Implications for humanities instructors

- **Grade the interpretation, not the text.** Design tasks whose visible work is source selection, objection and revision, following the format effect above.
- **Treat AI as an epistemic mediator.** Use [[voicu-ai-interpretive-cognition-ssh-2026|Voicu's]] three trajectories as a diagnostic, and aim at the critical end.
- **Interrogate the filter.** When a model is restricted from making contested claims, make the restriction itself an object of study.
- **State the outcome in interpretive terms.** [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] in the humanities is the ability to locate the reader's position in an argument, not the ability to prompt, and [[critical-thinking|critical thinking]] is the capacity that use is supposed to develop.

## Common Misconceptions

**"The humanities are the least AI-exposed part of the curriculum."** The exposure has been measured at 58.6% of audited assessment items, concentrated in essays and reports ([[villanueva-ai-vulnerability-assessment-audit-2026|Villanueva, 2026]]).

**"AI in humanities education is technocentric augmentation."** The Lingnan case treats the change as intellectual and puts digital fluency among the liberal arts rather than beside them.

**"Values education can be handed to a model as content."** [[genai-values-education-affordances-risks-2026|Chen (2026)]] reframes values education as a relational practice with three conditions — answerability, exemplarity and address — and reads each of GenAI's affordances as replacing a relation: the simulated perspective replaces the stakeholder who can be wronged.

**"The humanities face the same assessment problem as everyone else."** The artifact is different: a claim about what a text means, defended against counter-readings, is not a report of findings and does not become one when a model can write reports.
