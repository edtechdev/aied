Integrating AI in TVET is a UNESCO-UNEVOC guide for institutions that train technicians,
tradespeople and other skilled workers, developed with the UNEVOC Network and international
[[vocational-education|TVET]] experts and published open access in 2026. It is a normative document:
principles, a phased institutional path and working instruments, not a report on what AI does to
vocational learning.

## What you can do with it

Three annexes carry the usable material. Annex A is an AI readiness self-reflection tool for a small
cross-functional team: seven dimensions running from leadership and governance through data
readiness, curriculum, partnerships and funding, each scored on four maturity levels from not yet
started to established, ending with a few feasible first steps. Annex B is an illustrative AI risk
register. Annex C is an indicator framework in seven domains mirroring the institutional chapters,
each with sub-themes, key indicators and candidate data sources such as policy version history,
tracer surveys and staff surveys. The teaching chapter adds a ladder for how far AI enters a task:
AI-aware learning, where learners examine how AI is reshaping a trade without using it; AI-
supported, where AI plans or prepares while the learner performs the task; and AI-enhanced, where it
supports work in progress. Chapter 5.5 aligns staff and learner [[ai-literacy|AI competency]] with
UNESCO's own frameworks for teachers and students.

## Who it is for

Leaders of TVET institutions deciding what to do first, teachers and trainers who need a defensible
line between where AI helps and where unaided practice stays essential, and qualification staff
revising occupational standards. The recommendations chapter is sorted by audience, so each can
begin in its own section.

## Notes and caveats

It is guidance, not evaluation. The guide reports no outcome data, and its institutional examples, a
content-integrated AI learning model at the College of the North Atlantic in Canada and Kenya's 2023
TVET industrial attachment standard, are descriptive case boxes sourced to project reports, not
independent trials. The annexes call themselves indicative; the maturity scale is a discussion aid,
not a measurement instrument. Safety-critical and regulated occupations get a chapter, but it argues
a position on human oversight rather than offering regulation-level detail. The CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO
license permits adaptation and republication with attribution and share-alike.
