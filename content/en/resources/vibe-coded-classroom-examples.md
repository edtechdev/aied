**Vibe-Coded Classroom Examples** is José Antonio Bowen's gallery of just over thirty simulations, games and visualizations that teachers built by prompting an AI model, grouped by discipline and each one opening the running tool rather than a screenshot. It sits on the Teaching with AI site that accompanies Bowen and C. Edward Watson's book, and it gives the most concrete answer yet to the question of what [[vibe-coding|vibe coding]] produces in a classroom.

## What you can do with it

Browse six groupings: sciences and engineering, health and medicine, social and behavioral sciences, business, economics and law, arts and humanities, and AI and information literacy. Each entry names its author and the model that built it, and the range is the point, from a chirality exercise on ibuprofen and a mitochondrial electron-transport simulation to a nursing shift where the patient deteriorates, a Federal Reserve meeting from 1979, and an Ames room whose geometry is computed live.

The second half is a build procedure. Bowen's first prompt asks for five ideas, each with its learning objective, the action a student repeats, and how the tool will know whether the student got it right; the second prompt builds the chosen one, requiring an objective-to-screen table, a verified source list, one mission with graded levels, feedback that explains every wrong answer, a "show me" button, labels for what is real and what is simplified, and a single HTML file that can be deployed to GitHub Pages. A pre-share checklist follows: play it, answer something wrong on purpose, check every number, test it on a phone and a projector. The research section then documents larger agentic database projects.

## Who it is for

Instructors in any discipline who want a worked example of their own kind of tool before building one, and [[learning-design|instructional designers]] deciding what a tool should make a student do rather than what it should display. Faculty developers running workshops get a ready set of examples and prompts, and courses teaching [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] through building get both the artifacts and the caution that comes with them.

## Notes and caveats

These are teaching demos, not evaluated interventions: the page reports no learning-outcome evidence for any of them, and several are its author's own builds rather than independent classroom use. Build quality tracks the model and the number of iterations, so an example that worked in one model version can drift or break later, and generated tools state facts wrongly often enough that the page's own checklist is to verify every number against the sources the tool lists. The gallery mixes Bowen's work with other educators' contributions and third-party links that can rot. Reading needs no account; the page is free, while Bowen's workshops and consulting are paid.
