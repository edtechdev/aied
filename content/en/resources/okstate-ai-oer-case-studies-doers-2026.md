AI + OER Case Studies is an open anthology edited by Kathy Essmiller and published by Oklahoma State
University Libraries in 2026, collecting eleven cases on where [[generative-ai|generative AI]] meets
[[open-education|open educational resources]] in higher education. It grew out of a 2025 report for DOERS, Driving OER
Sustainability for Student Success, a consortium of 45 higher-education systems covering more than
1,000 colleges.

## What you can do with it

Read it as a set of project accounts rather than an evaluation. The book runs in three parts, Shared
Values, Shared Practice and Shared Future, and the editor maps each chapter onto one of four policy
priorities from the earlier report: centering students, engaging critically with generative AI,
keeping policy pedagogically driven, and attending to all learners. Chapters include the City
University of New York's route from a planned policy to published guidelines for generative AI in OER
creation, constrained by a federated campus structure and a union contract; a human-in-the-loop
revision of an open technical-communication textbook; co-creating institutional AI policy with
students; and system-wide professional development across AI and OER. It ships as a Pressbooks book with EPUB, PDF and
XHTML downloads.

## Who it is for

[[educational-policy-ai|Policy]] staff and library OER leads drafting guidance for a whole system,
[[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] coordinators planning professional development, and instructors or course
designers adapting a textbook or a writing assignment. Each chapter was peer reviewed by invited
scholars, and the cases were chosen for implications beyond one course.

## Notes and caveats

Chapter licensing is the trap. The collection is CC BY 4.0, but individual authors retain copyright
and chose their own Creative Commons licenses, so a chapter can be more restrictive than the book
page suggests; the textbook-revision chapter is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and reuse has to check the chapter
rather than the front matter. Authors had no required AI disclosure procedure, so most chapters
discuss their process in passing, generally following the OER Commons guidance. By editorial choice
each chapter keeps its author's disciplinary voice, punctuation and regional spelling, so the prose
does not read uniformly. Above all, every chapter documents one local project and no two share an
instrument or a comparison, so the volume offers practice to adapt rather than evidence to cite.
