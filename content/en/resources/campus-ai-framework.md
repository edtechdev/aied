The Campus AI Framework is a higher-education-native operating model for AI strategy and
governance, published by Joe Sabado. It sets out eight institutional pillars — from campus mission and
AI principles through policies, governance, engagement, readiness, roles and implementation — four
application domains where AI is put to work (teaching, learning and student success; research and
innovation; operations and infrastructure; student and community engagement), and an AI Strategic
Compass for choosing and sequencing initiatives.

## What you can do with it

The pillars name what an institution has to build; the Compass is the selection instrument, scoring
candidates against strategic fit, ethics and compliance, financial viability, operational efficiency,
efficacy and evidence, and stakeholder impact, and tracking them through a screen-score-select-plan-
track-reflect cycle with KPIs, OKRs and efficacy measures. Working tools include a maturity assessment
for readiness and capability, a tool evaluation matrix, and a governance guide. The site's tabbed
diagram view maps each pillar to the companion sites that carry it further.

## Who it is for

Cabinet leaders, provosts and CIOs deciding what to back; AI leads and governance committees
sequencing the work; and unit-level practitioners who need a first concrete step. The site routes the
three audiences to different entry points rather than one linear read.

## Notes and caveats

The framework and site are licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, so adaptation is permitted for non-commercial
use with attribution and share-alike; the site supplies its own suggested attribution. Its alignment
with OECD, NIST, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, FERPA and HIPAA is the publisher's own comparison and
should be read as such. The companion sites are separate projects, several by the same author, and are
worth knowing as a set: [Campus AI Exchange](https://www.campusaiexchange.com/) is the hub, holding
[interactive maps](https://www.campusaiexchange.com/ai-policies-guidelines-map) of institutional AI
policies and classroom guidance, [AI governance self-assessment](https://www.campusaiexchange.com/ai-governance-assessment),
data governance and AI positions maps, and a resource library. Around it sit
[campusaigovernance.com](https://campusaigovernance.com/) (nine AI governance domains),
[campusairisks.com](https://campusairisks.com/) (the NIST AI Risk Management Framework applied to those
domains), [campusaioperations.com](https://campusaioperations.com/),
[campusairegistry.com](https://campusairegistry.com/), a unit-level
[AI adoption plan template](https://unit-ai-adoption-plan.netlify.app/), and a briefing on
[agentic AI for executives](https://campusaiframework.com/sites). The Strategic Compass also circulates
as a shared Google document, which needs a Google account to open. A short optional onboarding question
about role and institution appears on entry; email is optional and the whole site is readable without
it.
