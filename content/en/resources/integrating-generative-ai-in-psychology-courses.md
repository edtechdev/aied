A free edited volume from the Society for the Teaching of Psychology (2026, ISBN 978-1-941804-82-7) that organizes generative AI course by course rather than tool by tool. Psychology supplies the respondents to many AI-use surveys without being their subject; this book is the counter-case, with 20 course chapters plus an introduction and a conclusion across 255 pages.

## What you can do with it

Read the chapter for the course you are about to teach. The three sections — foundational and cross-cutting courses, clinical and counseling courses, research methods and statistics — keep related chapters together. Examples: an AI tutor chatbot ("Zig") in psychological statistics, ChatGPT paired with Elicit in a senior seminar, AI as a debate coach, case conceptualization and cultural humility in clinical training, and a research methods chapter that has students generate questions with ChatGPT and then critique them.

The introduction states the book's common argument: [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] is developmental rather than a skill students either have or lack, so access should be staged and paired with verification requirements, and success is judgment about when and whether to use the tools. The opening chapter on policy treats expectations as a moving target — the integrity problem stated from inside a licensure-oriented program.

## Who it is for

Psychology instructors at any career stage, and instructors in adjacent [[social-sciences-education|social science disciplines]] whose assessment problems have the same shape: essay-heavy work, case discussion, statistics anxiety, small courses where one redesign is feasible.

## Notes and caveats

Free PDF, no account. The copyright is STP's, not a Creative Commons license: personal and classroom copies are permitted with attribution, while redistribution or commercial use needs the author's permission. Chapters are practitioner accounts, not studies — no designs, samples or effect sizes — so treat claims as experience reports and cite chapters individually.
