## Main highlights and questions

The strongest results this week are about what an assistant does to the work around it. Two experiments isolated the same quiet mechanism, one in a forum and one in a task: when the machine answers first, or answers without ever disagreeing, the thinking moves somewhere the score cannot see.

> A [preregistered trial](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/braun-cognitive-dormancy-ai-affordances-2026/) of 317 adults gave the same deep-processing task as a worksheet or as an open chat window, and the chat condition raised disorganization by 0.84 standard deviations with no mediator explaining the effect. A [forum study](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/davis-ai-ta-forum-peer-co-regulation-2026/) found an AI teaching assistant was the first responder in 97% of student-started threads, replying in a median of 0.92 minutes against 490 minutes for classmates, while the self-regulatory language in the thread fell from 49% to 36%.

- [Does an assistant that saves preparation hours also improve what gets taught?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/appel-genai-teacher-lesson-preparation-experiment-2026/) A randomized trial with 204 teachers, four incentivized tasks, and blinded expert ratings.
- [What is left of a pooled effect of 0.86 after bias correction?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/khodjaev-genai-outcomes-meta-analysis-2026/) A three-level meta-analysis of 58 studies and 6,941 participants.
- [Does adding AI to an open pedagogy project add a literacy gain?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/clinton-lisell-open-pedagogy-ai-tools-2026/) Two intact course sections, 113 students, one term.
- [Which model holds its ground when a student insists it is wrong?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/apostolakis-trusstutor-bench-statics-2026/) Thirteen models, 200 verified problems, three tutoring postures.
- [Does framing AI as empathic make people hand the empathy over?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/shastry-gray-empathy-offloading-2026/) Three experiments and one survey, 2,223 participants.
- [What does a course chatbot send to a third-party tracker?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/bekos-contextual-leakage-ai-chat-platforms-2026/) Network capture across 42 platforms, 17 of them education agents.
- [Who in a thread does the self-regulating?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/davis-ai-ta-forum-peer-co-regulation-2026/) 28,123 coded forum sentences from four graduate courses.

## For instructors

### [An open chat window raised disorganization by 0.84 standard deviations](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/braun-cognitive-dormancy-ai-affordances-2026/)

A preregistered experiment gave 317 adults one deep-processing task either as a worksheet or as an open chat window, and the [AI condition](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/braun-cognitive-dormancy-ai-affordances-2026/) raised disorganization with a total effect of 0.84 (credible interval 0.47 to 1.21) and a direct effect of 0.77 (0.41 to 1.15) that no mediator in the model explained. Deep processing itself did not move (median minus 0.22, interval spanning zero), so the effect landed in task organization rather than in what was learned. Pair the open window with structure around sequencing and stopping, which is [scaffolding](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/scaffolding/) the environment rather than the content.

### [The AI teaching assistant answered in under a minute and peer replies fell away](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/davis-ai-ta-forum-peer-co-regulation-2026/)

Across four graduate courses and 28,123 coded forum sentences, an AI TA was the first responder in 97% of student-started assignment threads, with a median reply time of 0.92 minutes against 490 minutes for peers. Self-regulatory language fell from 49% to 36% between the first and second half of the semester as peer replies dropped from 91% to 45% of follow-up posts, and peer replies carried roughly 70% of that self-regulatory language against 27% for thread starters. Cap or delay the first response so classmates still arrive first, because the [self-regulated learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/self-regulated-learning/) in a forum is produced by the replies rather than by the answer.

### [Prompt training buys teachers time, not better materials](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/appel-genai-teacher-lesson-preparation-experiment-2026/)

In a 204-teacher randomized trial, training in [prompt writing](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/prompt-engineering/) added a 21% preparation-time saving on top of plain ChatGPT access with no gain in rated material quality, because trained teachers wrote longer prompts and needed fewer refinement passes. Budget [teacher AI competency](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/teacher-ai-competency/) work as a workload intervention, not as a route to better resources.

### [A lesson assistant saved 49 minutes a week on a thin dose](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/roy-aila-lesson-planning-teacher-choices-2026/)

An independent cluster trial of Oak National Academy's [Aila](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/roy-aila-lesson-planning-teacher-choices-2026/) followed 416 English primary teachers and measured lesson preparation at 2.51 hours a week against 3.32 without it, about 49 minutes saved while the tool covered only 7% of lessons. Quality was tested for non-inferiority rather than improvement, so read this as a workload finding: worth trialling for time, not yet evidence of better materials.

### [Adding AI tools to an open pedagogy project changed nothing](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/clinton-lisell-open-pedagogy-ai-tools-2026/)

Two sections of an introductory child development course ran the same open assignment, 55 students as usual and 58 with generative AI search tools added, and reported the same perceived source-skill gain: 0.50 against 0.48 on a five-point scale, interaction F(1, 84) = 0.04, p = 0.85, partial eta squared 0.00. The assignment did the work in both sections, so fund the [open education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/open-education/) task you already have and treat a tool bolted onto it as untested until something moves.

### [Told it was wrong, the strongest models rewrote correct work](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/apostolakis-trusstutor-bench-statics-2026/)

Two hundred verified truss problems and thirteen models produced solving accuracy from 64.5% to 97.0%, a 32.5-point spread. When a student asserted that correct work was wrong, the models confirmed it at rates from 46.5% to 98.5%, a 52.0-point spread wider than the accuracy gap itself. Choose the assistant students use by its resistance to pushback rather than by its accuracy, and keep a pushback item in the [evaluative judgment](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/) check you run before a rollout.

### [Writing scores under AI support did not overlap with the control group](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/gunay-ozden-process-genre-ai-writing-2026/)

A quasi-experiment across intact classes put post-test means at 9.8750 for the section taught AI-assisted writing as a process genre and 3.8125 for the control, with no overlap between the two distributions (Mann-Whitney U = 0.00, r = 0.86). The treatment taught revision with the tool rather than around it, which is a cheaper change than a detection policy and belongs inside the [writing education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/writing-education/) curriculum rather than beside it.

## For developers

### [Structure beat a tutor persona by seven points in two randomized trials](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/ai-cosmos-agentic-infrastructure-situated-learning-2026/)

Two preregistered three-arm trials, 131 students on legal reasoning and 54 completing a competitive programming arm, gave the structured system 22.8 percentage points against 15.7 for a vanilla assistant, a 7.0-point advantage (p = 0.018, d = 0.56). An LLM-as-Teacher persona over the same base model gained 2.8 points and did not separate (p = 0.121). Put the [scaffolding](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/scaffolding/) in the scenario and the workbench, then tune the voice.

### [Tutors name the brute-force solution more often than the optimal one](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/cen-sonkar-muse-llm-tutors-multi-solution-2026/)

On 150 multi-solution programming problems run against four open-weight tutors with simulated students, brute force was the named approach in 22.2 to 32.1 percent of solution-specific openings while the optimal solution took 13.0 to 19.4 percent. In probe-first conversations every base model moved students toward a worse approach in 6.1 to 7.1 percent of cases. Audit which solution a tutor names first before it takes over a multi-solution problem, and write that audit into your [intelligent tutoring](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/intelligent-tutoring/) acceptance tests.

### [Counting AI calls measured nothing about progress](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/taufer-human-ai-interaction-haidl-2026/)

Across 140 student-assignment pairs from 35 students in one data engineering course, the number of AI interactions correlated with successfully executed cells at r = 0.12, and with touched cells at the same r = 0.12. Interaction counts are the easiest signal for a [learning analytics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-analytics/) pipeline to collect and the weakest thing to put on a dashboard; instrument the execution state on either side of each call instead.

### [The second half of a session predicted the test, not the number of questions](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/abdelghani-student-llm-epistemic-proactivity-math-2026/)

From 880 substantive turns by 112 students aged 14 and 15, the steepness of the within-session help-seeking trajectory predicted post-test performance without the AI, adding a change in R squared of .110 to a model that already held the pre-test score (beta = .34). Static interaction features added .023 and did not reach significance (p = .684), and overall 81.5% of turns were requests while evaluation was 2.2%. Judge a session by whether its second half drifts toward answer-seeking, and put a mandatory adequacy check after each response.

### [The scoring criterion moves a tutoring benchmark more than the model](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/misconcept-dialogue-llm-math-tutoring-2026/)

The same 2,250 dialogues between five tutors and [simulated students](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/simulating-students/) scored 42.1% under exact match and 81.3% under a human-calibrated judge, a 39.2-point spread larger than the combined effects of model, strategy, and pipeline, and four judges from three vendors agreed within 6 points. Structured pipeline labels gave no advantage (p = 0.263) and a wrong diagnosis scored the same as a gold one, so if your harness reports one number, you do not yet know what it measures.

### [The grading method, not the judge model, sets reproducibility](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wargaming-adjudication-ai-judged-grading-2026/)

Rerunning AI-graded submissions changed 2.9% to 6.1% of outcomes under a semi-rigid rubric and 13.5% to 15.9% under free holistic grading, while swapping the judge model moved only about three points. Publish an [adjudication statement](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/) naming the method, the harness, a repeat run and how many graded items sit behind each claim.

### [An AI peer that agrees with everything loses its users](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/lin-ai-peer-collaborative-mathematics-2026/)

Seven experts each spent 20 minutes in the student role with an agent cast as a peer rather than a tutor, and what made it read as a peer was social: tentative reasoning and deliberate imperfection built trust, while blind agreement with everything the learner said destroyed it. Configure agents to hold uncertainty and to decline agreement by default, since [trust calibration](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/trust-calibration/) is a behavior you write rather than a property a model has.

## For researchers

### [The pooled effect of GenAI instruction is 0.86, or 0.26, or zero](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/khodjaev-genai-outcomes-meta-analysis-2026/)

A three-level random-effects meta-analysis of 58 studies, 68 effect sizes and 6,941 participants pooled to g = 0.864 (95% CI 0.720 to 1.009), then reported heterogeneity at about 87.9%, a 95% prediction interval from minus 0.213 to 1.942, and a bias-corrected effect under PET-PEESE of g = 0.258. In a new setting the large number is not the one to plan on: pilot first, and print the prediction interval next to the pooled estimate where a reader cannot miss it.

### [GenAI experience reached critical thinking only through how the work was divided](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/akgun-toker-genai-critical-thinking-transactive-memory-2026/)

Among 891 college students in the United States and Türkiye, experience with generative AI predicted critical thinking only through specializing and coordinating cognitive labor (b = 0.636) and reflectively transforming output (b = 0.367), with no significant direct path, and both mediators carried their own association with critical thinking (0.230 and 0.175). Tool familiarity is the wrong variable to manipulate; assign an explicit division of labor and require students to rework the output, which is the [cognitive offloading](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cognitive-offloading/) question restated as a design choice.

### [A smaller education gap in AI use is not equity](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/watanabe-genai-education-gap-europe-2026/)

Matching each of 16 European countries at its 2025 adoption rate to its own internet-era year within five points, the median gap between the most and least educated fell from 56.4 points for the internet to 28.6 for generative AI, a change of minus 27.6, negative in 15 of 16 countries (p = 0.0005). Composition-aware matching shrinks that to minus 11.5 and minus 10.0, and the measure is a binary last-three-months item, so an [equity](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/equity-in-ai-education/) claim needs a stronger instrument than this one.

### [Romanians trusted AI more than Danes and used it a third as much](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/lucau-uneven-use-similar-trust-genai-2026/)

In 2025, 48.4% of Danes and 17.8% of Romanians aged 16 to 74 had used generative AI in the previous three months against an EU figure of 32.7%, yet Romanians rated their trust at 4.1 against 3.8 on a seven-point scale, and 47% of them called current laws and safeguards adequate against 36% of Danes. Use and trust travel separately, so a low-use country is not a low-willingness country, and the remedy is access and basic skills for the oldest and least educated rather than a campaign about trust.

### [Offloading is only erosive when it bypasses the goal](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/nakamura-augmentation-erosion-cognitive-offloading-stem-2026/)

The useful move is from whether a student used AI to which cognitive operations the tool discharged: offloading helps when it takes on work peripheral to the learning goal and risks erosion when it bypasses processing constitutive of that goal, and processing the output afterward can rescue an otherwise constitutive offload. Seven conditioned propositions replace one directional claim, and offloading regulation becomes a learnable dimension of [AI literacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-literacy/).

### [Sentiment tools miss about half the negative responses](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/aalhe-ai-sentiment-analysis-student-voice-2026/)

Against 1,220 hand-coded open-ended survey responses, the best transformer reached .79 accuracy but only .42 kappa and caught about half the negative cases, while lexicon tools misclassified roughly one in five because their dictionaries lacked the words. With a 76.5% positive corpus, accuracy hides exactly the cases you would act on, and automated coding of open text is a [validity](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/assessment-validity/) argument rather than a tool choice.

## For administrators

### [Twenty-one of 42 chat platforms sent the conversation title to a tracker](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/bekos-contextual-leakage-ai-chat-platforms-2026/)

Instrumenting 42 platforms, 25 general-purpose and 17 task-specific agents for education, health, and legal advice, found 21 sending conversation titles to at least one third-party tracker and nine transmitting the verbatim prompt, with 11 of the 13 leaking general-purpose platforms also sending the registration email. Education platforms averaged about 367 tracker requests each, 24% of them carrying user content. Require a third-party script inventory before you license or host a course chatbot, and ask which tags can read the page title, the URL, and the input field.

### [The return to a year of schooling fell where AI exposure was highest](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/levy-yeyati-tacit-knowledge-genai-returns-2026/)

A difference-in-differences over 655,919 Current Population Survey observations from 2018 to June 2026 found the return to a year of schooling in the most AI-exposed occupations fell by 0.55 log points after November 2022, about 7% of its pre-shock level, while the return to experience rose 0.26 log points at three years and fell 0.12 at twenty-five. Codified recall is the part of a credential losing value, so protect supervised practice and judgment under uncertainty, and read the [curriculum design](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/curriculum-design/) implication before the program marketing one.

### [Framing AI as empathic made people hand the empathy over](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/shastry-gray-empathy-offloading-2026/)

Across three experiments and one survey, 2,223 participants in four samples, framing AI as capable of empathy raised willingness to offload empathic responsibility (total effect 0.83, p < .001) entirely through perceived capacity (indirect 0.73, interval 0.52 to 0.95). Hearing that a peer had taken a problem to ChatGPT lowered emotion-reading accuracy to 7.13 correct out of 12 against 7.73 (p = .012). Position the system as triage that routes students back to people, and watch [help seeking](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/help-seeking/) as the behavior that quietly disappears.

### [Benchmarks passed, children still harmed](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/child-related-ai-incidents-safety-benchmarks-2026/)

Coded from 1,331 publicly reported child-related AI incidents, sexualization is the largest harm subtype at 316, ahead of sexual exploitation at 131, and harms cluster with deployment, [governance](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/governance/), and misuse failures rather than model defects. A per-output filter cannot reach a companion interaction where no single turn crosses a threshold, so evaluate at session and institution level rather than at the API.

### [Eighty-one percent had used AI and four percent used it daily](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/dewitt-conflicted-student-generative-ai-2026/)

Two anonymous surveys at one UK university found 81.05% of 94 first-year psychology students had used a generative AI tool to help complete an assignment, against 4.21% reporting daily use and 9.47% reporting none at all. That is a spread of occasional, conflicted use rather than a cheater and non-cheater split, so name refusal, support, and substitution as three populations the same assignment meets, and write [academic integrity](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/academic-integrity/) policy for the middle of the distribution.

### [Provenance appeared in 8 of 36 health AI programs, ethics in 29](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/giancotti-data-provenance-health-ai-curricula-2026/)

A census of 36 programs at 25 institutional units in 21 universities found ethics taught in 29 (80.6%) and data provenance at the level the authors set as their bar in 8 (22.2%), with no master's program and no executive or professional program meeting it, and 22 of the 29 programs that teach ethics missing provenance entirely. A provenance review assignment added to the ethics module you already run is a [curriculum design](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/curriculum-design/) change rather than a new course.

## Concept spotlight

Five [concept pages](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/) absorbed this week's findings, two of them new. Each is a standing page rather than a one-off, so the new evidence sits alongside what the page already argued.

### [Open education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/open-education/)

The page is new, and it was written because thirteen studies arrived at once on what generative AI does to openly licensed teaching. Its argument is that the license rather than the model is the thing to examine, and the two [new resources](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/) below both describe institutions adapting open content with AI faster than they can record who authored what. Read the [open education page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/open-education/) before writing an AI clause into an OER policy.

### [Cognitive offloading](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cognitive-offloading/)

It now separates what a tool discharges from how much a student used it, which is the distinction the [offloading ladder](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/akcaoglu-offloading-ladder-experience-2026/) draws and the [empathy experiments](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/shastry-gray-empathy-offloading-2026/) demonstrate in a place most people would not look for it. The [cognitive offloading page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cognitive-offloading/) also carries the [0.84 standard deviation](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/braun-cognitive-dormancy-ai-affordances-2026/) result, because it is the cleanest causal evidence the page has.

### [Evaluative judgment](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/)

Its new evidence is about what a judge is asked to do: [grading methods moved rerun stability](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wargaming-adjudication-ai-judged-grading-2026/) from 2.9% to 15.9%, and [thirteen models folded under student pushback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/apostolakis-trusstutor-bench-statics-2026/) at rates from 46.5% to 98.5%. The [evaluative judgment page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/) now treats resistance to a false correction as a separate capability from getting the answer right.

### [AI literacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-literacy/)

Two results narrowed the construct: [critical thinking ran through how students divided and reworked the cognitive labor](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/akgun-toker-genai-critical-thinking-transactive-memory-2026/), not through familiarity with tools, and [a rubric built from 16 core papers](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/ryu-developing-ai-literacy-oer-2026/) showed how few resources name the competency they claim to build. The [AI literacy page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-literacy/) keeps both, because a literacy test and a literacy resource are measuring different things.

The other new page is [statistics and data science education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/statistics-and-data-science-education/), whose first argument is that the discipline's AI question turns on the artifact, since datasets, charts, and code can now be generated faster than anyone can validate them.

## New resources

Four resources joined the [resources library](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/) this week, and all four describe an institutional decision rather than a single classroom trick.

### [Learning Design Wiki](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/learning-design-wiki/)

[Learning Design Wiki](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/learning-design-wiki/) is an openly licensed reference that splits teaching design into principles, elements, patterns, designs, and strategies, each bound to the claims and citations behind it, and carries 170 principles, 54 patterns, and 6,700 strategies as of October 2026. Its own evidence report counts 6,849 studies behind 26,495 claims, of which 31% are experiments or systematic reviews and 2% are preregistered trials or well-powered meta-analyses, and it states that 98% of claims rest on a single study. Language models maintain it under one human maintainer and no page has been checked by a person, so the site labels every page a well-sourced lead rather than a finding. Content is CC BY-SA 4.0 and the code is MIT.

### [teacher.dev](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/teacher-dev/)

[teacher.dev](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/teacher-dev/) is a nonprofit studio that ships one free classroom app a day and posts the build, with a streak at 53 days and 34 apps in the gallery as of October 24. The apps address single moments: printable figure generators for mathematics, chemistry, and physics, a word-building tool for prefixes and suffixes, a First/Then board for special education, and a quiet-time safari. Most are local-first with no account, so no student data leaves the device, and the source is Apache-2.0. Nothing here is a platform to adopt, which is the point for a teacher who wants one tool and no data agreement.

### [AI + OER Case Studies: A DOERS Project](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/okstate-ai-oer-case-studies-doers-2026/)

[AI + OER Case Studies](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/okstate-ai-oer-case-studies-doers-2026/) is an open anthology edited by Kathy Essmiller for Oklahoma State University Libraries, collecting eleven cases on where generative AI meets open educational resources, and it grew out of a report for a consortium of 45 higher-education systems covering more than 1,000 colleges. It reads as a set of project accounts rather than an evaluation: chapters cover a federated university drafting AI guidance under a union contract, a human-in-the-loop textbook revision, and co-created institutional policy with students. The trap is licensing, because the collection is CC BY 4.0 while each author chose their own license and one chapter is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so reuse has to check the chapter rather than the front matter. No two chapters share an instrument or a comparison, so read it as practice to adapt rather than evidence to cite.

### [Integrating AI in TVET: a practical guide for institutions](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/unesco-2026-integrating-ai-in-tvet-guide/)

[Integrating AI in TVET](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/unesco-2026-integrating-ai-in-tvet-guide/) is a 2026 UNESCO-UNEVOC guide for institutions that train technicians, tradespeople, and other skilled workers, and its usable material sits in three annexes: a seven-dimension readiness self-reflection tool scored on four maturity levels, an illustrative risk register, and a seven-domain indicator framework with candidate data sources. Its teaching chapter adds a ladder of its own, from AI-aware learning where students examine how AI is reshaping a trade without using it, through AI-supported work, to AI-enhanced work in progress. It is normative rather than empirical and reports no outcome data, and its own annexes call the maturity scale a discussion aid rather than a measurement instrument, so use it to structure a first institutional decision, not to justify one already made.
