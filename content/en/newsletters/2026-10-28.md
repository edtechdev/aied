## Main highlights and questions

Most of this week's studies are about the distance between a number and the thing it is supposed to stand for. Two of them measured the same number twice in the same week. A benchmark of math tutoring scored identical dialogues at 42.1% and 81.3% depending only on the scoring rule, and an AI referee changed 2.9% to 15.9% of its own outcomes depending only on the grading method. Neither result is about a model getting better; both are about how much of a published figure belongs to the instrument.

> Two randomized trials tested what a lesson assistant saves teachers, and the answer is time rather than quality. [Two hundred and four teachers](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/appel-genai-teacher-lesson-preparation-experiment-2026/) cut preparation time by 34% with ChatGPT, and prompting training cut it a further 21% without any measurable gain in the materials. A [ten-week English trial](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/roy-aila-lesson-planning-teacher-choices-2026/) of Oak National Academy's Aila found 49 minutes saved a week, while the tool covered 7% of lessons and fewer than one teacher in five said it changed their planning.

- [Does an assistant that saves preparation hours also improve what gets taught?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/appel-genai-teacher-lesson-preparation-experiment-2026/) Two hundred and four teachers, four incentivized tasks, and blinded expert ratings.
- [What happens to a lesson assistant's savings when teachers barely use it?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/roy-aila-lesson-planning-teacher-choices-2026/) A cluster randomized trial with 416 teachers keeping weekly diaries.
- [When a hint helps on one step, what does it do to the step after?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/mihaylova-llm-feedback-live-programming-course-2026/) Three deployments and 173,232 logged submissions from one live course.
- [How much of a tutoring benchmark's score is the benchmark?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/misconcept-dialogue-llm-math-tutoring-2026/) The same 2,250 dialogues scored twice, by exact match and by calibrated judges.
- [Is a repeated AI grade a stable one?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wargaming-adjudication-ai-judged-grading-2026/) Three hundred and seventy-eight submissions rerun under four grading methods.
- [Can a student explain AI and still use it heavily?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/genait-genai-literacy-test-high-school-2026/) A validated test given to 7,432 high school students, set against their own reported use.
- [What does a safety benchmark miss about a child who was harmed?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/child-related-ai-incidents-safety-benchmarks-2026/) One thousand three hundred and thirty-one reported incidents, coded by harm type.

## For instructors

### [A lesson assistant saved 49 minutes a week on a thin dose](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/roy-aila-lesson-planning-teacher-choices-2026/)

An independent cluster trial of Oak National Academy's [Aila](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/roy-aila-lesson-planning-teacher-choices-2026/) followed 416 English primary teachers and measured lesson preparation at 2.51 hours a week against 3.32 without it, about 49 minutes saved while the tool covered only 7% of lessons. Quality was tested for non-inferiority rather than improvement, so read this as a workload finding: worth trialling for time, not yet evidence of better materials.

### [Prompt training buys teachers time, not better materials](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/appel-genai-teacher-lesson-preparation-experiment-2026/)

In a 204-teacher randomized trial, training in [prompt writing](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/prompt-engineering/) added a 21% preparation-time saving on top of plain ChatGPT access with no gain in rated material quality, because trained teachers wrote longer prompts and needed fewer refinement passes. Budget [teacher AI competency](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/teacher-ai-competency/) work as a workload intervention, not as a route to better resources.

### [Instructional design support clusters in one ADDIE phase](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/spyropoulou-genai-instructional-design-systematic-review-2026/)

A review of 78 studies finds AI support for [instructional design](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-design/) concentrated in the design phase at 95% of studies, against implementation at 9% and evaluation at 13%, with none covering the full cycle and 76% reporting against no named competence framework. If you design courses, the tooling you can buy stops before implementation and evaluation, and the review's direction for [professional development](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/educational-development/) is oversight and evaluation rather than tool operation.

### [Prescribed question chains beat free-form chatting](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/question-chain-prompting-srl-metacognition-2026/)

Across three intact classes of 83 undergraduates, students working through sequenced question chains logged more strategy use and more monitoring than a class chatting freely, and scored about three points higher, while the two chain forms did not differ. The intervention was a written instruction sheet with no change to the model, which makes it the cheapest version of a [self-regulated learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/self-regulated-learning/) prompt set with evidence behind it.

### [Access to AI feedback moved nothing until students used it](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wenzel-conversational-ai-feedback-cognitive-load-game-2026/)

In a five-week simulation with 234 students, being assigned a conversational feedback chatbot changed no cognitive load measure, and only among students who actually used it was germane load higher. Read [learning analytics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-analytics/) here as an uptake question: report who used the tool, not who was offered it.

### [AI-refined peer comments land later than they look](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wang-student-ai-collaboration-peer-feedback-2026/)

Recipients rated AI-assisted [peer review](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/peer-assessment/) comments higher on four of five quality dimensions, but only from the second cycle onward, and reviewers mostly wanted language help: 104 of the coded requests concerned grammar, vocabulary or naturalness. Send the essay and the rubric to the assistant rather than the bare draft, or you get prose repair instead of argument.

### [Generated practice beat authored videos on vocabulary](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/gelli-experiential-language-learning-2026/)

Runtime-generated scenes lifted vocabulary recall from 4.40 to 8.60 against 4.40 to 7.75 for expert-authored videos, a small to moderate advantage that did not extend to speaking. Learners attempted 89.6% of communicative goals but only 51.4% of grammar goals, which is where to check [language learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/language-learning/) content you generate rather than author.

## For developers

### [The scoring criterion moves a tutoring benchmark more than the model](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/misconcept-dialogue-llm-math-tutoring-2026/)

The same 2,250 dialogues between five tutors and [simulated students](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/simulating-students/) scored 42.1% under exact match and 81.3% under a human-calibrated judge, a 39.2-point spread larger than the combined effects of model, strategy and pipeline, and four judges from three vendors agreed within 6 points. Structured pipeline labels gave no advantage (p = 0.263) and a wrong diagnosis scored the same as a gold one, so if your harness reports one number, you do not yet know what it measures.

### [Longer hints predicted worse next attempts](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/mihaylova-llm-feedback-live-programming-course-2026/)

Across logged submissions from three deployments in a live Aalto course, one standard deviation more hint text, 204.9 characters, predicted lower odds that the next submission was correct, more attempts remaining and a longer wait. Bound hint length in the interface and let students choose the format, which is what deployment logs can then test.

### [The grading method, not the judge model, sets reproducibility](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wargaming-adjudication-ai-judged-grading-2026/)

Rerunning AI-graded submissions changed 2.9% to 6.1% of outcomes under a semi-rigid rubric and 13.5% to 15.9% under free holistic grading, while swapping the judge model moved only about three points. Publish an [adjudication statement](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/) naming the method, the harness, a repeat run and how many graded items sit behind each claim.

### [A provenance log gives users something to re-run](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/filtered-decision-logs-transparency-genai-2026/)

Filtered Decision Logs pair each answer with a rationale and an influence-ranked list of the tool outputs that shaped it, scored by a deterministic pipeline that sits outside the reasoning layer, so a user can trust the ranking without trusting the recommendation. Keep the scores absolute and unsummed, and label them so, or readers will take each one for a percentage.

### [Only one of seven models made the right collaboration call](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/llm-calibrated-reliance-imperfect-partners-2026/)

Benchmarked on 324 scenarios asking whether to accept a partner's contribution, pay to verify it or act alone, one of seven models chose the optimal action at every stage; the other six bought zero-value verification, over-purchased safeguards or routed help down the wrong channel. Supplying explicit posterior distributions fixed some of those errors and created others, so more evidence is not the same as [calibrated trust](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/trust-calibration/).

### [Novice oversight is about fitness, not correctness](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/novice-se-students-oversight-genai-2026/)

Fitness problems drove 74.4% of the 172 declared interventions from 145 first-year students, and 82.2% of outputs were rated perfect, so novices were mostly judging whether plausible work suited the task rather than whether it contained an error. A product that rarely shows a defect is not building the [evaluative judgment](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/) its documentation claims.

## For researchers

### [Sentiment tools miss about half the negative responses](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/aalhe-ai-sentiment-analysis-student-voice-2026/)

Against 1,220 hand-coded open-ended survey responses, the best transformer reached .79 accuracy but only .42 kappa and caught about half the negative cases, while lexicon tools misclassified roughly one in five because their dictionaries lacked the words. With a 76.5% positive corpus, accuracy hides exactly the cases you would act on, and automated coding of open text is a [validity](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/assessment-validity/) argument rather than a tool choice.

### [Tutoring interaction features barely beat a constant](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/llm-tutoring-interaction-predictive-value-2026/)

Built from 16,851 exchanges across two offerings of an undergraduate AI course, the best model cut mean absolute error from 3.138 to 3.023 grade points, an interval that crosses zero, and all six fitted models had negative R squared. The cause is a ceiling, 160 of 175 matched records above 90, so report the baseline and the quartile carrying the error, 25.7% of records contributing 62.1% of it.

### [AI literacy and AI use point in opposite directions](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/genait-genai-literacy-test-high-school-2026/)

An 18-item [AI literacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-literacy/) test validated on 7,432 Estonian high school students correlated essentially zero with perceived ease of use and negatively with both general and school use, minus .16 and minus .19. Adoption is not literacy, and conditional reliability running from .55 at the 5th percentile means group comparison rather than individual classification.

### [Offloading is only erosive when it bypasses the goal](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/nakamura-augmentation-erosion-cognitive-offloading-stem-2026/)

The useful move is from whether a student used AI to which cognitive operations the tool discharged: offloading helps when it takes on work peripheral to the learning goal and risks erosion when it bypasses processing constitutive of that goal, and processing the output afterward can rescue an otherwise constitutive offload. Seven conditioned propositions replace one directional claim, and offloading regulation becomes a learnable dimension of [AI literacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-literacy/).

### [Self-reported dependence tracks intention, not harm](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wei-perceived-technological-dependence-genai-2026/)

Among 526 design students, perceived knowledge raised trust, [self-efficacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/self-efficacy/) and usage intention and lowered anxiety, while perceived ease of use was the one perception unrelated to self-efficacy. Usage intention tracked reported dependence on cross-sectional data that cannot order the two, and dependence here means reliance on a tool for design work rather than pathology.

### [Epistemic agency splits between output and system](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/bergdahl-student-epistemic-agency-ai-2026/)

Students in a Swedish year 7 pilot sorted nine agency items unevenly: strong on source evaluation, weak on data privacy and algorithmic manipulation, with 79% spanning three or more points and 35 leaning output-directed against four the other way. Those privacy scores were recorded a month after a lesson in which students watched their own logged behavior projected live, which is the gap a single lesson leaves open.

## For administrators

### [Benchmarks passed, children still harmed](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/child-related-ai-incidents-safety-benchmarks-2026/)

Coded from 1,331 publicly reported child-related AI incidents, sexualization is the largest harm subtype at 316, ahead of sexual exploitation at 131, and harms cluster with deployment, [governance](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/governance/) and misuse failures rather than model defects. A per-output filter cannot reach a companion interaction where no single turn crosses a threshold, so evaluate at session and institution level rather than at the API.

### [Students use AI four times as often as teachers integrate it](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/gillette-partnership-ethics-genai-higher-education-2026/)

At one Swedish university, 80% of 541 students had used generative AI in their studies against 20% of 128 teachers who had integrated it, and 86% of the 209 participants who raised ethics unprompted did not see themselves as having a role in deciding ethical use. That is a mandate for [educational development](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/educational-development/) rather than another integrity policy.

### [Engagement is a calendar problem before a culture problem](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/aalhe-capacity-disposition-genai-assessment-2026/)

Miller's framework names protected time and professional stance as the two conditions behind uneven AI engagement among assessment professionals, with low time plus caution as the calendar trap and time plus stewardship as the practice commons. Read low engagement as workload, unclear policy and unrecognized labor before resistance, and give the skeptic a validation role.

### [Regulation is aimed at the wrong object](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/elkington-human-centred-assessment-value-2026/)

Elkington argues that tighter regulation and stronger detection treat a wicked problem as a technical one, and that prohibition-based responses can disadvantage precisely the students who rely on AI for accessibility, language support or [scaffolding](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/scaffolding/). Bring that equity argument to a policy meeting; the paper supplies criteria for a decision rather than evidence that a design works.

## Concept spotlight

Four [concept pages](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/) took this week's findings into the synthesis layer, and one new discipline page joined them. Each is a standing page rather than a one-off, so the new evidence sits alongside what the page already argued.

### [CS education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cs-education/)

The page now carries two results from live courses: [hint length predicting worse next attempts](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/mihaylova-llm-feedback-live-programming-course-2026/) across 173,232 submissions, and [novices judging fitness rather than correctness](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/novice-se-students-oversight-genai-2026/) in 172 declared interventions. Read the [CS education page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cs-education/) before shipping feedback into an autograder.

### [Evaluative judgment](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/)

Its new evidence is procedural: [four grading methods produced different rerun stability](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wargaming-adjudication-ai-judged-grading-2026/) on the same submissions, and [professors reported scores that moved on repeated prompts](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/wu-lam-genai-writing-assessment-professors-2026/). The [evaluative judgment page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/evaluative-judgment/) separates what a judge can do from what a grading procedure can promise.

### [Trust calibration](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/trust-calibration/)

Six of seven models failed a [calibrated reliance benchmark](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/llm-calibrated-reliance-imperfect-partners-2026/), and a [ChatGPT source label raised acceptance](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/chatgpt-source-cue-answer-acceptance-2026/) of arithmetic answers without improving discrimination. The [trust calibration page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/trust-calibration/) now treats acceptance and sensitivity as separate things to measure.

### [AI feedback quality](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-feedback-quality/)

Two studies this week put feedback quality in tension: [a calibrated judge scored the same dialogues 39 points higher](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/misconcept-dialogue-llm-math-tutoring-2026/) than exact match, and [longer feedback did worse on every downstream outcome](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/mihaylova-llm-feedback-live-programming-course-2026/). The [AI feedback quality page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-feedback-quality/) records both, because a quality claim without a criterion and a length is not yet a measurement.

The new [statistics and data science education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/statistics-and-data-science-education/) page joined the discipline strand this week, and its first argument is that the discipline's AI question turns on the artifact, since datasets, charts and code can now be generated faster than anyone can validate them.

## New resources

Two resources joined the [resources library](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/) this week, one for a designer who wants a design decision with its evidence attached and one for a teacher who wants a small tool for one recurring moment.

### [Learning Design Wiki](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/learning-design-wiki/)

[Learning Design Wiki](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/learning-design-wiki/) is an openly licensed reference that splits teaching design into principles, elements, patterns, designs and strategies, each bound to the claims and citations behind it, and carries 170 principles, 54 patterns and 6,700 strategies as of October 2026. Its own evidence report counts 6,849 studies behind 26,495 claims, of which 31% are experiments or systematic reviews and 2% are preregistered trials or well-powered meta-analyses, and it states that 98% of claims rest on a single study. Language models maintain it under one human maintainer and no page has been checked by a person, so the site labels every page a well-sourced lead rather than a finding. Content is CC BY-SA 4.0 and the code is MIT.

### [teacher.dev](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/teacher-dev/)

[teacher.dev](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/teacher-dev/) is a nonprofit studio that ships one free classroom app a day and posts the build, with a streak at 53 days and 34 apps in the gallery as of October 24. The apps address single moments: printable figure generators for mathematics, chemistry and physics, a word-building tool for prefixes and suffixes, a First/Then board for special education, and a quiet-time safari. Most are local-first with no account, so no student data leaves the device, and the source is Apache-2.0. Nothing here is a platform to adopt, which is the point for a teacher who wants one tool and no data agreement.

## Site notes

The largest change was to every concept page: all 221 now carry a common misconceptions section that names the wrong belief a reader may already hold and corrects it with the evidence on the page.
