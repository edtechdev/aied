## Why this site exists

AI in education research arrives faster than anyone can read it: thousands of papers a year, most of them reporting one course, in one institution, under conditions the abstract does not state. An instructor who wants to know whether AI feedback improves writing has to find the studies that bear on that question, read them against each other, and notice that one of them tested a model two generations old. That is research work, and nobody hired that instructor to do it.

The site does that reading in public. An AI agent reads the open-access literature and drafts the pages, a human editor reviews and publishes them, and every claim ends in a link to the paper behind it, so a sentence you doubt can be checked in two clicks. It is free, it is in the public domain under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), and there is nothing to sign up for.

It is written for the people who teach, study, design, or administer AI in education, which is why one finding often appears three times in different places: once for the instructor who has to grade with it, once for the developer who has to build it, and once for the administrator who has to decide whether to buy it.

## How it is organized

Four kinds of page, and the kind tells you what it is good for:

- [Concept pages](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/) collect what the research says about one idea, such as [feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/feedback/), [assessment](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/assessment/), or [cognitive offloading](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cognitive-offloading/), and link every study behind the claim. Start here for the overview.
- Article summaries cover one paper at a time, with its findings, its method, its limitations, and the full citation. They are listed day by day on the [journal page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/journal/).
- [FAQs](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faq/) answer the practical question in the words people actually ask it, with the research attached.
- [Resources](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/) list tools, collections, and instruments worth using, with who made each one, what it costs, and when its link was last checked.

The left sidebar indexes everything by strand, and the concept map on the home page arranges the field into twelve strands with AI in Education at the center. Every page ends with what it connects to, so a topic can be followed across the whole set: from a concept to the papers behind it, from a paper to the concepts it touches, and from an FAQ to both.

## Questions the site already answers

Most of the questions people put to a chatbot have already been answered here by hand, with the research attached. A few worth opening before you ask:

- [Should we use AI detectors?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/should-we-use-ai-detectors/) Not as evidence in a misconduct case: a detector score cannot be validated against ground truth, and its errors are patterned rather than random.
- [Does using AI actually help my students learn?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/does-ai-help-students-learn/) It can, but better work produced with AI is not evidence of better learning.
- [How do I redesign assessment so that a grade still tells me something defensible?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/redesign-assessment-ai-era/) Name what the grade is meant to represent before choosing the task.
- [How do I keep students from over-relying on AI?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/reducing-over-reliance/) Graded work that reads better than the student can explain is a design problem, not a policing problem.
- [How do I write a course AI policy and communicate it to students?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/course-ai-policy/) It has to be fair to the student who plays by the rules and the one who does not, and something you can apply to forty submissions.
- [How do I teach students to verify AI output?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/verify-ai-output/) Warnings to check the sources ask for a disposition; a checklist and a worked example do not.
- [How can AI support disabled and neurodivergent learners in my course?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-disabled-neurodivergent-learners/) What to adopt, what to refuse, and how to tell whether a tool helps the students an accommodation letter names.
- [What are the top 10 findings from AI in education research that instructors should know?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/top-10-findings-ai-education-instructors/) Ten results that change ordinary teaching decisions, each with the study behind it.
- [How do we write and implement an institutional AI policy?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/institutional-ai-policy/) Publishing a policy is the easy part, and a document alone changes nothing.

There are [37 of them](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faq/) in all, indexed by question rather than by topic, and there is one for students too: [how should I use AI to study and learn effectively](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/study-with-ai/).

## Search it or chat with it

### Search

The box in the header searches every page as you type, and the [search page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/search/) does the same with room for results. Ctrl K puts the cursor in the box from anywhere on the site.

### Chat

The whole knowledge base is published as machine-readable files, so you can point your own assistant at it and get answers that cite these pages instead of inventing them. [Copy the prompt](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/ai/) from the AI page, paste it as your first message, and then ask your question; a good answer names the pages it used, so you can check it. If you would rather not set anything up, two assistants already know the material: the [AI in Education Advisor](https://gemini.google.com/gem/1oRhk2pouJb-RpTE36aAMaIRfJrBdMR95?usp=sharing) Gemini Gem, and a [NotebookLM notebook](https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/7de87d06-8998-4f0a-81f5-c2523fc635d0?utm_source=nlmm_share) built on the same pages. Assistants that cannot open links can be handed [llms-concepts.txt](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/llms-concepts.txt), every concept and FAQ page in one file.

## Read the whole thing offline

The knowledge base is also published as a book, in two formats: [EPUB](https://github.com/edtechdev/aied/raw/refs/heads/main/public/aied.epub) for a phone, a tablet, or an e-reader, and [PDF](https://github.com/edtechdev/aied/raw/refs/heads/main/public/aied.pdf) for printing or for a computer. Each one holds the concept pages, the FAQs, the home introduction, the page about using the site with an AI assistant, and an appendix of free tools and resources, organized into chapters with a numbered table of contents you can click. Neither includes the article summaries, which stay on the site, and both carry the same public-domain notice and the statement of how the text was made. The download links sit on the [home page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/) and on the [AI page](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/ai/).
