## Main highlights and questions

- [Can AI feedback replace a teacher's feedback?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/liu-ai-generated-teacher-feedback-writing-motivation-rct-2026/) [AI-generated feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-feedback-quality/) and teacher feedback improved [essay revisions](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/writing-education/) equally in a randomized trial with 312 first-year writers. Teacher feedback raised [motivation](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/motivation/) more because students felt connected to the person giving it. AI can carry a revision pass; the teacher still carries the relationship.
- [Can a supportive chatbot substitute for a peer?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/li-human-peer-vs-chatbot-loneliness-2026/) A [supportive chatbot](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/conversational-ai/) performed the same as journaling in reducing [loneliness](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/well-being/) in a randomized trial with 296 first-year students; a randomly assigned peer reduced it. The bot expressed more empathy while students gave less back, so measure loneliness itself.
- [Does a short ethics module change AI judgment?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/qi-information-ethics-ai-judgment-2026/) A four-week [information ethics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ethics/) module raised students' scores on AI-related ethical judgment scenarios in a trial that randomized 16 classes. About 60% of the gain held four weeks later, and the heaviest AI users gained most. Case-based teaching shows promise.
- [Does more AI assistance improve creative work?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/xiao-metacognitive-disengagement-knowledge-innovation-2026/) Four randomized experiments with 728 adults found that when AI did more of the work, judges rated the resulting innovation proposals lower, mostly because participants did less of the [cognitive work](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cognitive-offloading/). More assistance came with weaker work.
- [Can an AI-authored game teach better than a lecture?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/xie-genai-mystery-game-legal-education-2026/) A mystery role-playing game beat the same instructor's lecture on legal knowledge and reasoning in a randomized trial with 84 law students. The game was built with generative AI; the comparison was game versus lecture, so the result supports [game-based learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/game-based-learning/) and leaves chatbot use untested.
- [Do student-performance reports change teaching?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/lim-assignment-report-intelligent-tutoring-randomized-encouragement-2025/) Teachers new to an online math [tutoring system](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/intelligent-tutoring/) were randomly prompted to open a [learning analytics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-analytics/) dashboard report of their students' performance on the first assignment. Opening it changed neither the share starting the next assignment nor students' [test-score gains](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-gains/), though next-assignment completion rose, mostly in smaller classes.
- [Does matching human graders make an AI grader safe?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/porcaro-ai-grader-reliability-2026/) An audit of six AI models [grading](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/automated-assessment/) 300 short answers found all marked identical work higher when told the student was high-achieving, by 0.28 to 0.75 points on a ten-point scale. Agreement can hide a bias tied to the student's [prior-attainment record](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/student-modeling/); withhold the record.
- [Why is AI-generated language so hard to understand?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/clark-ai-generated-language-readability-2026/) AI text overuses rare word combinations, denominal verbs, inanimate subjects, reduced object relatives, stacked nouns, and long dependencies because post-training for math, code, and reasoning rewards compressed answers over clear explanations. The implication for education is that AI-written explanations, [feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-feedback-quality/), and reading material can be harder for students to process even when readability scores look fine, a problem for [reading education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reading-education/) that formulas miss. *[Editorial note: As a reminder, this newsletter and the AIEd Knowledge Base are AI-generated. This issue used the [Humanizer skill](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) and an explicit instruction to write affirmatively and avoid "not X, but Y" phrasing ([Dolan, 2026](https://www.psypost.org/one-of-ais-most-annoying-writing-habits-has-a-2000-year-old-history/)), plus heavy editorial guidance from the site developer to improve readability and usefulness.]*


## New FAQs

Two [new answers](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faq/) joined the FAQ this week.

### [How can AI help students with reading and reading comprehension?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-support-reading-comprehension/)

The [reading answer](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-support-reading-comprehension/) treats [reading-level labels](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reading-education/) as rough guesses: a few real readers' ratings beat a formula, and an AI told to [pretend to be a student](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/simulating-students/) cannot tell you how hard a text will feel. Use AI to find, simplify, and gloss readings, then try the result with students and keep the original beside it.

### [How does AI bias and unfairness show up in education, and what can we do about it?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-bias-fairness-education/)

The [bias answer](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-bias-fairness-education/) asks for [error rates](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/bias-mitigation/) for the groups you teach, since an average can hide a gap and the failure may sit in scoring, tutoring, dashboards, or detection. Put [group reporting](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/differential-effects-across-learner-groups/) and an appeal route into procurement, and fix the pipeline itself.

## New concepts

Eight [concept pages](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/) joined the knowledge base this week.

### [Reading education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reading-education/)

[Reading education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reading-education/) now has a page that separates four things which often share one label: helping a reader, [assessing reading](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/assessment/), adapting a text, and [measuring difficulty](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/assessment-validity/). It cites [a study where 193 students rated 3,954 sentences](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/thome-perceived-text-complexity-student-profiles-2025/); 72.8% felt easy or very easy even when Flesch scores said otherwise, so a grade-level label is a weak guide to how a particular reader will experience a text.

### [Reflection](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reflection/)

[Reflection](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reflection/) has a new page that separates it from the in-the-moment monitoring called [metacognition](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/metacognition/). The first design question is whether the student wrote the reflection. In a [302-adult experiment](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/structured-reflection-ai-explanatory-feedback-2026/), reflection prompts doubled time with [feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/feedback/) and cut practice by 40%, leaving lower mastery, 65% against 79%, so timing matters as much as the prompt itself.

### [Automation bias](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/automation-bias/)

[Automation bias](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/automation-bias/) names the human habit that can turn a correct AI recommendation into a wrong decision: accepting an answer you would have questioned if you had reached it yourself. It collects the places where that happens in education, from essay scores and early alerts to [proctoring](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/remote-proctoring/), and shows how a [review step](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/human-in-the-loop-ai/) that exists only on paper fails to catch it.

### [Civic and political education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/civic-and-political-education/)

[Civic and political education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/civic-and-political-education/) maps a gap in the evidence and synthesizes the field. An analysis of [310 AI-generated lesson plans](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/civic-education-ai-lesson-plans/) found 90% of activities asked only for recall, comprehension, or basic application; 94% had almost no multicultural content; and no study in the corpus measured civic learning at all.

### [Libraries](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/libraries/)

[Libraries](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/libraries/) treats the library as a place, a service, and a group of professionals. A [review of 29 studies](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/ayinde-ai-academic-libraries-systematic-review-2026/) finds adoption concentrated in reference and cataloguing, while the [Public AI Libraries pilot](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/resources/public-ai-libraries/) makes shared, staffed access the equity argument.

### [AI avatars and synthetic instructors](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-avatars-and-synthetic-instructors/)

[AI avatars](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-avatars-and-synthetic-instructors/) map the roles a synthetic human can occupy, from recorded lecturer and [tutor](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/intelligent-tutoring/) to [simulated patient](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/simulation/) and examiner. The evidence is uneven, and a realistic face tells you little about learning; [disclosure and trust](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/trust-calibration/) matter as much as the model.

### [Sensor and gaze analytics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/sensor-and-gaze-analytics/)

[Sensor and gaze analytics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/sensor-and-gaze-analytics/) covers eye tracking, brain and heart signals, and camera posture as ways to infer [attention and engagement](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/student-engagement/), and cognitive load. The field is better at watching learners than at changing instruction, and the page treats [privacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/privacy/), [bias](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/bias-mitigation/), and the gap between a body signal and a mental state as central.

### [Information and media literacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/information-and-media-literacy/)

[Information and media literacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/information-and-media-literacy/) treats the two as one instructional problem: information literacy asks whether a source deserves belief, while media literacy asks who made a message and why. [Generative AI](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/generative-ai/) collapses both questions because fluent text hides its origin; the page finds little evidence on a combined curriculum.

## For instructors

### [AI feedback matches a teacher on revisions while motivation lags](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/liu-ai-generated-teacher-feedback-writing-motivation-rct-2026/)

AI and [teacher feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/feedback/) improved the same essays by about the same amount in a [312-writer randomized trial](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/liu-ai-generated-teacher-feedback-writing-motivation-rct-2026/). Teacher feedback did more for motivation because the student felt connected to the person giving it.

Keep a human voice around the [feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/feedback/). Pair the generated comment with a short instructor response, or use AI for the first pass and the teacher for the conversation about the work.

See related: [AI feedback at scale](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-feedback-at-scale/)

### [One in five essays was written entirely by the chatbot](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/jelson-students-chatgpt-essay-writing-2026/)

A [study of 77 college writers](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/jelson-students-chatgpt-essay-writing-2026/) logged every prompt, response, and paste. Fifteen, or one in five, submitted chatbot-written essays, and most still felt the essay was theirs.

Ask for the intermediate work: the prompts, the revisions, and a short note on what changed and why. Make the [handover](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cognitive-offloading/) visible while there is still time to teach.

See related: [Redesigning assessment for the AI era](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/redesign-assessment-ai-era/)

### [Secondary students use AI almost universally, and lower achievers use it more](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/scherer-self-directed-genai-use-secondary-2026/)

A [year-long study of 4,062 German secondary students](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/scherer-self-directed-genai-use-secondary-2026/) found nearly universal AI use: 95% used it, and lower achievers used it more to finish tasks. That pattern predicted less effort later.

That makes [framing AI use](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/framing-ai-use-for-students/) and [self-regulated learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/self-regulated-learning/) part of the curriculum, revisited across the year. Ask students what the tool is for on this task, and give lower-achieving students a supported route that begins with understanding the work.

See related: [Reducing over-reliance on AI](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/reducing-over-reliance/)

### [A supportive chatbot matched journaling while a peer reduced loneliness](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/li-human-peer-vs-chatbot-loneliness-2026/)

In a [two-week randomized trial with 296 first-year students](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/li-human-peer-vs-chatbot-loneliness-2026/), a supportive chatbot performed the same as [journaling](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reflection/) in reducing loneliness, while a randomly assigned peer reduced it. The bot showed more empathy while students gave less back.

A bot can serve as a useful first contact while a peer or counselor remains available. Build that human route into any [student support](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/student-support-and-success/) service you recommend, and measure loneliness itself.

See related: [AI, anxiety and wellbeing](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-anxiety-wellbeing/)

### [Treat an AI simulation as supervised rehearsal](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/zhang-nursing-genai-simulation-meta-synthesis-2026/)

A [review of 14 studies](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/zhang-nursing-genai-simulation-meta-synthesis-2026/) found [nursing students](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/nursing-education/) valued repeatable, low-pressure AI [simulation](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/simulation/) and reported more confidence, especially in communication and clinical reasoning. Realism, body language, and response lag remained weak.

Brief students on the limits, require them to check generated content, and debrief afterward. Keep human interaction where nonverbal communication or sustained empathy is the point of the exercise.

See related: [Nursing education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/nursing-education/)

### [Capstone code tripled while comments held steady](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/shakil-code-commits-complexity-genai-2026/)

Ten semesters of [260 capstone projects](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/shakil-code-commits-complexity-genai-2026/) in [computer science education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/cs-education/) show median code rising from about 3,400 lines to 10,000, while comments stayed near 8%. Commit counts rose; individual commits barely changed.

Assess the design decisions, the explanation of the code, and the student's ability to change it. The artifact can now grow faster than the learning it is meant to show.

See related: [Redesigning assessment for the AI era](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/redesign-assessment-ai-era/)

## For developers

### [Without the figure, an AI tutor should refuse](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/croteau-mllm-visual-math-reasoning-refusal-2025/)

[Six image-capable AI models](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/croteau-mllm-visual-math-reasoning-refusal-2025/) tackled 376 middle-school [math](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/math-education/) items whose figures carried key information. With the figure, the best solved just over half; without it, they refused almost every time.

Treat a refusal as a missing-context signal. A score or profile update produced without the figure measures a different task.

See related: [Math education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/math-education/)

### [A writing tutor that asks questions and leaves the writing to students](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/favero-thinking-not-writing-chatbot-2026/)

[MAIKE](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/favero-thinking-not-writing-chatbot-2026/) asks questions and diagnoses arguments while leaving essay text to the student. Among 65 secondary students, improvement matched a control chatbot, 40% to 41%, and MAIKE users spent more turns on ideas; none declined.

Measure whether an idea discussed in the chat becomes a revision. A [writing education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/writing-education/) tool that only improves the final text can bypass the reasoning the assignment exists to build.

See related: [Writing instruction with AI](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/writing-instruction-ai-best-practices/)

### [Zoom and highlight the slide while the narration runs](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/lian-guideslide-narrated-slides-visual-guidance-2026/)

[GuideSlide](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/lian-guideslide-narrated-slides-visual-guidance-2026/) narrates a slide deck and uses [visualization](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/visualization/) to zoom to the part being discussed. In a study of 24 people watching on phones, workload fell, comprehension rose from 5.5 to 6.5 out of 9, and 21 of 22 preferred it.

The advantage a day later was not statistically significant, so the study supports attention while watching; durable learning remains untested. Build the cueing into the narrative itself.

See related: [Designing AI into learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/designing-ai-into-learning/)

### [A fine-tuned detector can de-identify student text without the name bias](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/ji-de-identification-personally-identifiable-information-2025/)

[Five tools](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/ji-de-identification-personally-identifiable-information-2025/) were tested on 13,613 student-text files. A fine-tuned GPT-4o-mini found about 96% of [identifying details](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/privacy/) at roughly one sixth of Azure's per-token price; the Microsoft tools missed African and Asian names more often.

Training on ten transcripts worked on a new set, so the approach travels. Budget for the second check when precision matters, and report the groups your tool misses.

See related: [Institutional AI policy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/institutional-ai-policy/)

### [Video analysis reached human-level agreement, with a human still in the loop](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/zhou-gaiva-human-ai-video-analysis-2026/)

[GAIVA](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/zhou-gaiva-human-ai-video-analysis-2026/) transcribes, labels, and interprets classroom video for [learning analytics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-analytics/). On five recordings, its labels agreed with human reviewers about as closely as two reviewers did, and it surfaced a kind of participation the original theory did not name.

Treat the model as a coding partner with a traceable prompt and a human decision at each stage. The model also surfaces patterns a human reviewer might not have looked for.

See related: [AI-assisted educational research](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/how-can-ai-assist-with-educational-research/)

## For researchers

### [A large average effect can still mean no reliable benefit](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/govea-sotomayor-personalized-learning-meta-analysis-2026/)

A [review of four controlled studies](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/govea-sotomayor-personalized-learning-meta-analysis-2026/) with 420 computing and engineering students found a large average improvement, while the studies disagreed sharply and the likely range included no benefit. The evidence was rated very uncertain.

Treat a large average from four dissimilar studies as a hypothesis about your course. Pilot the [personalized learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/personalized-learning/) where it will run, and say plainly how uncertain the evidence is.

See related: [What personalized learning means](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/what-personalized-learning-means/)

### [An AI grader that knows the learner's record marks the same work differently](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/porcaro-ai-grader-reliability-2026/)

[Six AI models](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/porcaro-ai-grader-reliability-2026/) graded the same 300 short answers with a true and a swapped note about prior attainment. Every model marked identical work higher under the high label, by 0.28 to 0.75 points on a ten-point scale.

[Student context](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/student-modeling/) is acting like a bias here. Report the context effect beside the agreement figure, and test grading without the student's record before calling a model reliable.

See related: [AI grading fairness and reliability](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-grading-fairness-reliability/)

### [A fair-looking tutor can hide a 26% skill gap](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/stinar-fairness-unmodeled-reading-skills-adaptive-learning-2026/)

[Two years of logs](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/stinar-fairness-unmodeled-reading-skills-adaptive-learning-2026/) from 8,549 students using MATHia, an adaptive math tutor, showed that students still learning to read mastered skills at 68.7% against 77.6%, while the overall [fairness measure](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/bias-mitigation/) looked almost neutral. One skill hid a 26% gap in predicted mastery.

The tutor had no reading-skill component, so [reading education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/reading-education/) was a missing factor in a math system. Model the skill, and report gaps at the level where a teacher would intervene.

See related: [AI bias and fairness in education](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-bias-fairness-education/)

### [Detection false positives are a governance problem](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/maghami-ai-detection-false-positives-2026/)

A [review of 90 studies](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/maghami-ai-detection-false-positives-2026/) found AI detectors flag legitimate writing at rates that cannot be pooled, because tools, thresholds, datasets, and languages differ. Abstracts, literature reviews, exam answers, translated text, and edited prose kept getting flagged.

The remedy is procedural: transparent use, human review, [AI literacy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/ai-literacy/), an explicit [institutional policy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/educational-policy-ai/), and an appeal route. That belongs in [governance](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/governance/), with detector settings as one part.

See related: [Should we use AI detectors?](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/should-we-use-ai-detectors/)

### [Matching human graders can still miss the work](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/porcaro-ai-grading-benchmark-2026/)

[Twenty-two AI models](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/porcaro-ai-grading-benchmark-2026/) [graded](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/automated-assessment/) 100 items per task across short answers, code, and essays. Most matched humans on short answers, four matched or beat them on code, and none reached the human level on essays.

A high [agreement figure](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/assessment-validity/) can coexist with an AI grader that grades the note and misses the answer. Add altered answers to the evaluation and report where the model departs from the work.

## For administrators

### [Neurodivergent edtech evidence is concentrated and researcher-led](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/gibson-neuro-inclusive-edtech-scoping-2026/)

A [review of 93 studies](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/gibson-neuro-inclusive-edtech-scoping-2026/) found autism dominated evidence on edtech for [neurodivergent learners](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/neurodiversity/) at 39.8%, ahead of intellectual disability, ADHD, and dyslexia. Extended reality (VR and AR) was most studied; social and peer outcomes got less attention, and children had little say.

Ask for the outcome the classroom cares about and for the learners who were included in the evaluation. A claim validated only by researchers can drift from the people it is meant to serve.

See related: [AI for disabled and neurodivergent learners](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/ai-disabled-neurodivergent-learners/)

### [Students wanted an institutional assistant for feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/rienties-institutional-ai-assistant-co-design-2026/)

A [13-month co-design program](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/rienties-institutional-ai-assistant-co-design-2026/) with 315 students and 20 staff found 73% valued round-the-clock [feedback](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/feedback/) tied to their studies. Flashcards and quizzes drew more positive comments than open chat, and 16 of 18 beta testers wanted the assistant.

The 10% to 15% who resisted on values grounds pose the adoption risk. Treat their concerns as substantive. Explain how student data is handled and what the assistant cannot do before the pilot, and give students a route to opt out.

See related: [Designing AI into learning](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/designing-ai-into-learning/)

### [Proctoring catches cheating and pushes the cost onto the least advantaged](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/ustun-surveillance-or-support-proctored-exams-2026/)

A [review of 32 studies](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/ustun-surveillance-or-support-proctored-exams-2026/) found AI proctoring catches impersonation and cheating, while false positives, lighting and bandwidth failures, anxiety, and [privacy harms](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/privacy/) fall hardest on the least advantaged. Automated proctoring was 59% of the studies.

The reviewers recommend [alternative assessment](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/authentic-assessment/) and a mix of human and automated monitoring. Reduce the high-stakes online exam before adding another detector.

See related: [Institutional AI policy](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/institutional-ai-policy/)

### [A dashboard report raised completion while test scores held steady](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/lim-assignment-report-intelligent-tutoring-randomized-encouragement-2025/)

Teachers new to an online math [tutor](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/intelligent-tutoring/) were randomly prompted to open a [learning analytics](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-analytics/) dashboard report of student performance on the first assignment. It changed neither the share starting the next assignment nor students' [test-score gains](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/learning-gains/), though completion rose in smaller classes.

Test whether teachers open the report, act on it, and see a learning gain before buying analytics; a completion bump alone is insufficient.

See related: [Checking whether educational AI works](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/faqs/checking-whether-educational-ai-works/)

### [Almost no institution can name who owns AI management](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/kaze-ai-management-higher-ed-2026/)

A [survey of 387 students and staff](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/pages/kaze-ai-management-higher-ed-2026/) in 13 countries found only 17% knew of a designated [AI governance](https://edtechdev.github.io/aied/concepts/governance/) role. Of the rest, 71% thought one was necessary and 76% expected it to improve their experience.

Respondents described a role with a mandate; a committee without one leaves that role unfilled. Give the owner authority over training, data security, ethical review, and strategy, and publish who holds it.
