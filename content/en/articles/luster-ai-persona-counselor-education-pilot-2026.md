> **Synthesis:** An exploratory pilot asked whether counselor-facilitated encounters with AI-generated personas could support empathic responding. Ten adults, four of them [[graduate-education|graduate]] counseling students, completed one session containing two text-based persona encounters. An eight-item [[self-assessment]] drawn from the Perspective Taking and Empathic Concern subscales rose from 23.10 to 25.80 on a 0–32 scale, a mean change of 2.70 points, and nine of ten participants reached the deepest tier of an embedded disclosure system. Transcript coding showed frequent perspective taking and empathic concern, with substantial agreement between two coders. With no comparison group and measurement immediately after one session, the results describe short-term in-session responding rather than lasting change or an effect attributable to the personas alone.

## Key Findings
1. **Ten adults completed the protocol.** Eleven were recruited and one was excluded for incomplete participation and post-test data.
2. **Each person had one facilitated session.** Two self-chosen personas were encountered in text chat, each for at least 15 minutes within a session of up to 50 minutes.
3. **Self-rated [[empathy]] rose over the session.** The eight-item score increased on average, with a large within-person effect estimate.
4. **Most participants reached the deepest tier.** Nine of ten opened the persona's most personal disclosure level, and one reached the middle tier.
5. **Transcript coding converged with the other strands.** Coders agreed on 22 of 29 entries and frequently observed perspective taking and empathic concern.
6. **The persona's inner thoughts were visible.** Participants could see some content they would normally have to infer, making this supported practice rather than a test of unaided accuracy.
7. **No causal or durable claim is supported.** There was no control condition, no follow-up, and no transfer measure involving interactions with people.

## What the pilot involved

Sessions took place between July and October 2025. Four custom GPT personas were built in [[generative-ai|ChatGPT]] Plus running GPT-4o, each with an Enneagram-based personality spine, a composite backstory and rules about tiered disclosure. The Enneagram was used as a character-design framework rather than a diagnostic tool. Participants chose two personas, opened each encounter with their own greeting and wrote all replies. The lead author, a licensed professional counselor, facilitated orientation and closing but did not intervene during encounters.

The Unlock Tier System served two linked functions. As a training scaffold, it governed how much the persona disclosed in response to eight empathic signals: Presence Without Pressure, Emotional Labeling, Gentle Curiosity, Validation, Patience Over Urgency, Personal Vulnerability, Consistency and Acknowledgment of Withdrawal. As a performance-based assessment, it recorded the highest tier reached. Each response updated a Perceived Emotional Safety index from 0 to 100; Tier 2 opened at 40 with clear naming and validation of emotion, and Tier 3 opened at 70 after three supportive responses and acknowledgment of withdrawal or balanced vulnerability. The tiers were labeled Rapport, Emotional Recognition and Core Wound Disclosure, and the authors stress that the labels describe what the persona disclosed rather than grades of participant competence.

The self-assessment used four Perspective Taking items and four Empathic Concern items from the Interpersonal Reactivity Index, rated 0 to 4 and summed to 0 to 32, with item 18 reverse-scored. This eight-item subset has not been independently validated as a standalone form. Two researchers independently coded 29 excerpts using IRI domains, tier levels and a neutral category; coding was completed before tier outcomes were compiled.

## What changed in one session

Ten participants completed both assessments. Mean scores rose from 23.10 (SD = 5.02) at pre-test to 25.80 (SD = 2.90) at post-test, a mean increase of 2.70 points. The paired-samples test gave t(9) = 2.83, p = 0.020, with a 95% CI of [0.54, 4.86] for the mean change and Cohen's dz = 0.89, 95% CI [0.14, 1.62]. One participant showed a nominal decrease. The authors note that these estimates are imprecise and cannot be attributed to the encounters alone.

Nine participants reached Tier 3 and one reached Tier 2. In the coded excerpts, a Tier 3 response asked "Why do you think you pulled away at that moment?", another elaborated a [[metaphors-and-analogies|metaphor]] about feeling unanchored, and a third linked back to an earlier disclosure about losing touch with a sister. A Tier 2 response said "That sounds painful. I hear how important it was." Agreement was 22 of 29 entries (75.9%), yielding an unweighted Cohen's κ of 0.70, described as substantial; agreement was essentially unchanged when either duplicate passage was set aside, with κ = 0.68 to 0.72. Disagreements clustered at adjacent tier thresholds. The authors report that coding and tier outcomes describe the same conversations, so convergence is not independent validation that the system measures empathy.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Consider structured AI persona encounters as a supervised rehearsal space before peer role-plays, not as a replacement for human practice.
- **Instructors.** Use transcripts for feedback on specific behaviors such as naming emotions, validating and tracking earlier disclosures.
- **Counselor educators.** Keep a licensed facilitator involved and preserve human authority, since the personas are assistive and cannot provide genuine mutual recognition.
- **Researchers.** Test the tier system against independent empathy ratings and compare it with standard role-play or reflective [[reflection|journaling]] conditions.

## Limitations
- The sample of 10 adults included only four counseling students, limiting power and generalization to trainees specifically.
- Without a comparison group, the increase may reflect facilitator attention, expectations, repeated items or reinterpretation of the questions.
- Tier attainment partly reflects the system's own design and reward rules rather than empathy measured independently.
- Displayed persona thoughts supported engagement but prevent conclusions about unaided empathic accuracy.
- The eight-item self-assessment covers only two empathy facets and lacks validation as a short form; coding reliability was estimated on 29 entries from a subset of materials.
- Outcomes were measured immediately after one session with no [[longitudinal-research|longitudinal]] follow-up or assessment of transfer to interactions with people.
