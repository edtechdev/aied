> **Synthesis:** Socorro Márquez, Reyes Ortiz and Torrez Meruvia synthesize 137 sources into a single role claim: the educator facing AI is not primarily a tool user but a *philosophical leader*, and that role has three dimensions — ethical judgment about whether a use is worth making, pedagogical guidance that keeps the learner doing the thinking, and policy translation that turns international governance language into classroom rules. The argument is teleological rather than empirical, in the sense the [[philosophy-of-ai-in-education|philosophy of AI in education]] uses the term. AI supplies content and speed, the review concedes, but it has no capacity for ethical discernment, so the educational value of any AI-mediated activity depends on an educator who can say what the activity is for. The paper closes with a three-phase lesson template that puts the dimensions in sequence and with an explicit turn to non-Western ethics — Ubuntu relational justice and Confucian virtue — as a corrective to individualist governance frameworks.

## Key Findings

1. **Three dimensions converge, and they are ordered.** Ethical judgment comes first as preparation: the educator interrogates bias, training data and the fiction of machine neutrality before the tool is used. Pedagogical guidance is execution: the AI is directed at a defined intellectual job rather than allowed to produce the finished work. Policy translation is governance: the educator enforces privacy, transparency and accountability rules in the room.
2. **Policy translation is the dimension the frameworks leave undone.** International [[governance]] documents supply vocabulary — responsibility, transparency, trust, responsible use — while the operational meaning stays with the teacher, who has to convert it into a prompt rule, an assessment condition, or a refusal.
3. **AI enhances rather than replaces the educator, and the reason is ethical, not technical.** The review treats ethical discernment as the part of teaching that no system supplies, which makes [[agency|teacher agency]] the mechanism that converts technical automation into learning.
4. **Coaching and mentoring are the proposed route to the role.** Philosophical leadership is presented as a disposition developed through professional relationships — a coach or mentor who works on the educator's judgment — rather than as a competency acquired in a training module.
5. **The historical lineage matters because it changes one variable.** Programmed instruction in the 1950s, computer-assisted instruction in the 1970s and 1980s, and digital learning in the 2000s all automated delivery with static, pre-programmed logic. Generative AI introduces autonomy and opacity, which is what turns pedagogy into continuous algorithmic governance rather than instruction.
6. **Ubuntu and Confucian framings are offered as governance content, not decoration.** Relational justice, communal interdependence, benevolence and harmony are proposed as criteria for algorithmic decisions and as a way to reduce epistemic injustice in system design.
7. **The framework is argued, not tested.** Nothing in the 137 sources measures whether philosophical leadership improves learning; the review's own contribution is the synthesis and the lesson template.

## The three dimensions in sequence

The ethical dimension is the one the review treats as prior to use, and its worked case is a history assignment. Before students touch a language model, the educator opens a [[critical-pedagogy|critical dialogue]] about algorithmic bias, Eurocentric training data and the limits of machine neutrality — the tool is selected and framed rather than handed over.

The pedagogical dimension then constrains what the tool is allowed to do. In the same assignment the model generates counter-arguments to theses students have already written themselves, and students fact-check its logic and hunt for hallucinated historical detail. That inversion is the practical content of the [[teacher-role|role]]: the learner's own argument exists first, so the AI cannot substitute for it.

The policy dimension is the smallest and the most concrete. The educator enforces data privacy by keeping personally identifiable information out of prompts, which models compliant use instead of merely announcing it. The review's point is that this is teaching, not compliance overhead: students learn what accountable technology use looks like by watching it enforced.

## What the synthesis rests on

The method is a [[meta-analysis-systematic-review|conceptual integrative review]]: a configurative qualitative synthesis with document analysis, drawing on 850 records from database searching and 75 from other sources, reduced to 780 after duplicate removal, 620 excluded at screening, 160 full texts assessed, 23 excluded for reasons including a lack of methodological transparency, and 137 included. Quantitative findings from the included randomized trials are read for pedagogical significance rather than pooled, which is the review's stated interpretive move: statistical significance in the documentary evidence is translated into a claim about what good education is for.

That move is the paper's strength and its limit at once. It lets the authors connect philosophical argument to current empirical work, and it also means the central construct arrives as an interpretation. The authors are explicit that they see philosophical leadership as the mediating variable between automation and human growth; what they do not provide is a study in which that variable is measured.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Place the ethical conversation before the task, not after it: name the bias, the training-data limits and the model's non-neutrality while the assignment is being set. Then constrain the tool to a job the learner has already begun, such as generating counter-arguments to a written thesis, and require verification of what it returns.
- **Administrators.** Treat classroom-level translation as the place where [[educational-policy-ai|classroom-level policy]] either holds or evaporates. A rule that teachers cannot restate as a prompt condition, an assessment condition or a boundary they can enforce has not been implemented, whatever the document says.
- **Policymakers.** Write guidance that names the classroom decisions it is meant to reach. The review's finding is not that frameworks are absent but that their vocabulary does not specify who does what, which leaves the last translation to whoever is teaching.
- **Faculty developers.** Build the role through mentoring and coaching relationships rather than one-off training. The review's mechanism is sustained professional dialogue about judgment, which is a different design from a tool-skills workshop.

## Limitations

- The contribution is conceptual: a synthesis of 137 sources with no primary data collection, and no measurement of whether philosophical leadership changes learning, ethical reasoning or classroom practice.
- The search covers English and Spanish publications only, which the authors flag as a linguistic and geographical bias, and non-Western traditions enter as cited proposals rather than as reviewed evidence.
- Publication lag is structural: the technical capabilities described in the reviewed literature may already differ from the models now deployed in schools, so the framework is offered as an adaptable guideline rather than a fixed rule.
- Most included studies are theoretical or short-term pilots. No included study follows learners across academic years, so the claim that sustained philosophical leadership develops ethical judgment over time is untested.
- The three-phase lesson template is the authors' own construction rather than a design validated with teachers or students.
