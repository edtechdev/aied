> **Synthesis:** Gutierrez, Govea and Valencia-Silva (2026) ask a question most reviews of [[generative-ai|generative AI]] in education skip: what happens when a generative system sits between a learner and a [[sustainability|sustainability]] problem, acting as a [[pedagogical-agent|pedagogical agent]] rather than as a tool for drafting materials. Their PRISMA-ScR [[meta-analysis-systematic-review|scoping review]] retrieved 172 records from Scopus, Web of Science and ERIC and retained 25 studies in 26 publications, all published between 2024 and 2026. The corpus is embedded in teaching more firmly than adoption research would predict — 20 of the 21 implemented interventions were coded as integrated — and evidenced far more weakly: measured change in learners appears in ten studies, against a comparison condition in five. The decisive finding is a measurement gap. Nineteen of the 25 studies invoke no sustainability competency framework, and of the twelve targeting constructs that map onto GreenComp, one assessed them with a corresponding instrument; in that study the group using generative tools scored lower than the control on four of the five competencies.

## Key Findings

- **Embedding is structural, not reflective.** Of the 21 implemented interventions, 20 were integrated into instruction, but only 11 (52.4%) framed the activity with briefing, debriefing or guided reflection.
- **Tutoring dominates.** The 25 studies yielded 44 role assignments: dialogic tutor in sixteen, instructional co-designer in twelve, scenario generator in seven, simulated actor in five, formative assessor in three.
- **The deliberative role barely exists.** The dilemma interlocutor, where the system sustains deliberation over a value conflict with no optimal resolution, appeared in a single study.
- **The geographic spread is unusually balanced.** The corpus spans sixteen countries and divides close to evenly between Global North and [[global-south|Global South]]; China and the United States contributed three studies each.
- **Competencies go unmeasured.** Nineteen of 25 studies (76.0%) invoked no sustainability competency framework, and of twelve targeting GreenComp-related constructs, one assessed them with a corresponding instrument.
- **Evidence of learning is thin.** Measured change in learners appeared in ten studies, five against a comparison condition; sixteen of the 18 studies with learner-outcome data confounded the technology with redesign.

## How the review was conducted

The authors followed Joanna Briggs Institute guidance and reported against PRISMA-ScR, mapping a heterogeneous literature rather than pooling effect sizes. Searches ran on 8 July 2026 in Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection and ERIC for records indexed from January 2023 to June 2026; 172 records became 120 after duplicates, 17 fell outside the window, and all 103 were read in full. Seventy-seven were excluded, most on the concept criterion, leaving 25 studies in 26 reports. Two reviewers screened independently, agreeing on 85 records (82.5% observed agreement) and sending 18 to a third adjudicator. Charting used 34 variables, seven of them interpretive judgments governed by written decision rules, with both reviewers coding all 25 studies and reconciling disagreements by consensus.

## What the corpus looks like

Studies appeared in 2024 (four), 2025 (eleven) and 2026 (ten); none in 2023 despite the window opening that January, which the authors read as the lag between model availability and classroom reporting. Nineteen appeared in journals, six in proceedings. Provision concentrates in [[higher-ed|higher education]]: fifteen studies (60.0%) were undergraduate, four primary, four secondary and two postgraduate, across sixteen countries that divide close to evenly between North and South. Access was mostly informal — sixteen (64.0%) used free consumer tiers, four institutional deployments, one a purpose-built system. Disciplines run from language education and accounting to environmental engineering, teacher education and interdisciplinary sustainability courses. Twenty-one studies reported learner-facing interventions, three evaluated designs without learner use, one was conceptual.

## What the agents actually did

The 25 studies produced 44 role assignments, since most interventions used the system in more than one capacity. The dialogic tutor was the most frequent role, at sixteen studies, followed by instructional co-designer at twelve and scenario generator at seven; simulated actor appeared in five, formative assessor in three, the dilemma interlocutor in one. Interaction was rarely free-form: twenty-one studies used structured prompt sequences or scripted [[scaffolding|scaffolds]], with prompts usually originating with the learner. Epistemic positioning split four ways — nine shared epistemic partner, seven oracle whose answers learners accepted, five instrument the learner directed, four interlocutor whose output was contested. [[curriculum-design|Curricular integration]] (18) and [[assessment|assessment linkage]] (16) outnumbered [[reflection|guided reflection]] (9), briefing (8) and debriefing (7).

## What the review found missing

The competency-by-role matrix the authors planned could not be built. Nineteen of 25 studies invoked no sustainability competency framework; four used the Sustainable Development Goals, one a discipline-specific framework, one a recognized framework in a mappable way. Twelve targeted constructs corresponding to GreenComp competencies, but only one assessed them with a corresponding instrument, and there the generative-AI group scored lower than the control group on four of five competencies. Reported outcomes count domains, not evidence: all 25 studies targeted cognitive outcomes, yet eight reported perceived or qualitatively described benefits, four stated intended outcomes without measuring them, three evaluated generated materials. Sixteen of the 18 studies with learner-outcome data confounded the technology with a wider [[learning-design|instructional redesign]], and 17 measured only immediately after the intervention. Twelve discussed no risk at all; 22 (88.0%) never mention the footprint of the systems they teach with.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Name the competencies before choosing the tool, then require learners to contest, justify or revise model output rather than receive it; deliberation over a conflict with no optimal answer is where normative judgment develops.
- **Learning designers.** Frame every generative episode with briefing, debriefing and guided reflection. Linking an activity to assessment while leaving the model's authority unexamined teaches students to accept fluent answers.
- **Researchers.** Isolate the generative element with a comparison condition, add a delayed measure, and align an instrument to each competency you name; redesign and technology introduced together cannot be told apart.
- **Assessment designers.** Score sustainability competencies with validated [[rubrics|rubrics]] applied by trained raters, and say what the instrument measures; engagement is not systems thinking.

## Limitations

- **Small corpus.** Twenty-five studies preclude subgroup analysis; the proportions describe this corpus, not a population.
- **Narrow window.** All studies appeared between 2024 and 2026, and pre-2023 work on generative conversational agents falls outside by design.
- **Search scope.** Only three English-language indexes were searched, and sustainability was not searched as a standalone term, so conference and differently framed work is incompletely covered.
- **Interpretive coding.** Seven charted variables are reviewer judgments, reconciled by consensus rather than agreement statistics, and no formal risk-of-bias appraisal was conducted.
- **Heterogeneous topics.** Content ran from marine education and medical waste to urban ecology and forest science, so the findings describe a topic-diverse corpus rather than a shared curriculum.
