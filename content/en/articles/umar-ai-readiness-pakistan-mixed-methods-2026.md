> **Synthesis:** Umar and Li (2026) examine how [[undergraduate-education|undergraduates]]' [[ai-literacy|AI readiness]] relates to their evaluations of AI-supported learning in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, using interviews with 25 students followed by a survey of 497 students at eight [[higher-ed|universities]]. The four readiness dimensions they measure — cognition, ability, vision and [[ethics|ethics]] — were all positively associated with attitudes toward AI and perceived AI-enabled learning practices, which in turn were associated with perceptions of AI-supported learning. Ethics carried the largest and most robust associations, with a strong positive link to both attitudes and perceived practices, while five of the 14 hypothesized paths did not survive correction for multiple testing. The authors also report that the hypothesized indirect-only model was misspecified: a model with direct paths fitted better, so the mediation story the study set out to test is not the one the data support.

## Key Findings
1. **Ethics was the strongest readiness dimension.** Ethics was associated with attitudes toward AI and with perceived AI-enabled learning practices at 0.42 in both cases, and those associations were essentially unchanged in the method-adjusted model.
2. **Most paths were positive but not all survived correction.** All 14 hypothesized paths were positive with confidence intervals excluding zero, but nine remained significant after Holm correction and five were tentative.
3. **The hypothesized mediation model was misspecified.** A model allowing direct paths fitted better than the indirect-only model, and the authors say its estimates need confirmation in an independent sample.
4. **Shared method variance was substantial.** An unmeasured method factor accounted for 27.2% of item variance on average, and explained variance fell by 0.16 to 0.25 when it was modeled.
5. **Students described a cautious, conditional view of AI.** Interview participants checked output before trusting it, grounded use in subject knowledge, worried about dependency, and framed ethical limits as personal responsibility.
6. **Teachers remained part of the condition.** Students saw AI's value for learning as conditional on the continuing role of teachers rather than as a replacement for them.
7. **The sample is outside the usual high-income setting.** The study examines AI readiness in a lower-resource context where access, [[educational-policy-ai|institutional policy]] and infrastructure vary, though it did not measure those conversion factors directly.

## What the study did

The design was sequential: 25 interviews first, then a questionnaire adapted from scales developed for medical students and teachers, piloted and administered to 497 undergraduates across eight universities. The analysis used confirmatory factor analysis and structural equation modeling with bias-corrected bootstrap confidence intervals, then a Holm correction across the 14 paths. The [[qualitative-research|qualitative phase]] was coded by one researcher from English notes translated from the interviews, which the authors acknowledge limits access to participants' exact words.

The measurement work is unusually candid. The scales were supported in this sample and across random halves, but not in an independent sample; the adapted items were not put through a formal [[assessment-validity|content-validity]] study or a test of English proficiency, although the questionnaire was not in students' first language. High inter-item correlations and high reliability are reported as a possible sign of item similarity rather than as a strength.

## Why the ethics result matters

The ethics finding is the most portable one. In this sample, students who reported stronger ethical readiness also reported more positive attitudes and more perceived AI-enabled learning practices, and that association was the least affected by the method-factor adjustment. The authors read this as a substantive signal: students who have thought about responsible use may be better positioned to integrate AI into study in a way they can defend.

The capability lens the paper adopts explains why the other associations are smaller. Readiness is not the same as capability, and the study did not measure what students can actually do with AI, what institutional access they have, or how disciplinary norms shape use. The authors list those as missing conversion factors and call for research that measures achieved functionings rather than [[self-report-measures|self-reported]] readiness alone.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Teach ethical use as part of AI literacy, not as a compliance add-on. In this sample, ethical readiness was the dimension most consistently associated with positive evaluations of AI-supported learning.
- **Administrators.** Do not treat a readiness survey as an implementation plan. The study measures perceptions, and the authors explicitly note that access, policy and infrastructure were not measured.
- **Researchers.** Test readiness models in independent samples and with performance measures. The hypothesized mediation model did not fit better than a direct-path alternative, which is a warning about assuming mediation from cross-sectional data.
- **Faculty developers.** Include subject-knowledge grounding and output checking in training; those were the practices students described when they explained what responsible use meant to them.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional survey data: associations cannot be given a causal or temporal interpretation, and a model with all paths reversed fitted identically.
- All constructs were self-reported at one time point; the method factor accounted for 27.2% of item variance and the adjusted estimates remain uncertain.
- The hypothesized model was misspecified, and the better-fitting direct-path model needs confirmation in an independent sample.
- Institution was not recorded, so clustering could not be modeled and the sample cannot be described by university.
- Interviews were not audio-recorded and were analyzed from translated notes by one researcher; [[critical-thinking|critical thinking]] and demonstrated capability were not measured.
