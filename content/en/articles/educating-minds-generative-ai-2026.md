> **Synthesis:** Di Paolo, Clark and Wachter argue that framing [[generative-ai]] as a tool for efficiency and [[personalized-learning]] misses what it does to schooling. Working from 4E cognition and extended views of mind ([[distributed-cognition]]), they treat schools as hybrid cognitive ecologies where tools, norms and routines shape how [[learners]] attend and remember. [[llm]] systems are therefore not an added aid but an epistemic infrastructure: unlike books or Logo they are active, persistent, generalist and autonomous, consolidating explanation, [[feedback]] and evaluation in one interface. Two misalignments are amplified rather than created: a [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] gap between learning-science accounts of learning and the screen-bound logic of most educational AI, and a goal gap between measurable performance and aims such as agency, collaboration and [[ethics|ethical]] formation. Their proposal: treat GenAI as a diagnostic catalyst and redesign schooling as a multitechnological cognitive ecology, AI one tool among many.

## Key Findings

1. **Schools are cognitive ecologies, not neutral settings.** Schooling is always multi-technological: the skills it cultivates are inseparable from the tools carrying them, and [[ai-technologies|technologies]] normalize particular epistemic practices.
2. **GenAI differs qualitatively from earlier artifacts.** Books, Logo and Montessori materials are multifunctional but bounded by their materiality; [[conversational-ai]] is active, updated after deployment, generalist, autonomous, and folds [[scaffolding]], instruction and evaluation into one system.
3. **GenAI behaves like an epistemic infrastructure.** Teaching-management systems consolidate [[curriculum-design|curriculum]] planning, instructional support, assessment and reporting, imposing one mediated environment on teachers, [[administrator|administrators]] and [[parents-and-families|parents]].
4. **The pedagogical gap is a design problem, not an AI property.** Systems reproduce a disembodied, screen-bound logic at odds with [[active-learning]], [[situated-learning]] and [[embodied-learning|embodied]] research, so students without [[ai-literacy]] rarely move past prompt tuning.
5. **The goal gap concerns what education is for.** Policy emphasizes collaboration, ethical formation and [[critical-thinking]], yet systems stay organized around efficiency and coverage, marginalizing [[metacognition]] and [[agency]].
6. **Confabulation is both a risk and a resource.** Learners struggle to tell accurate from spurious content, yet awareness of [[hallucination-risk|hallucination]] can make verification and fact evaluation integral, not optional.
7. **The proposal is a multitechnological cognitive ecology.** No technology should dominate: low-tech, embodied and collaborative tools combine with AI by pedagogical aim, though AI is among education's most resource-intensive tools.

## Schools as cognitive ecologies

They adopt 4E cognition (mind embodied, embedded, enacted, sometimes extended) plus a fifth dimension: schooling shapes how cognition develops across developmental timescales ([[learning-theories]], [[philosophy-of-ai-in-education|philosophical]]). Technologies are therefore non-neutral: the Orbis Pictus, Logo, Flatland and Montessori materials were multifunctional by design but capped by their materiality, so learners stepped outside them for clarification or assessment. [[generative-ai]] sits on a different register, framing problems, generating explanations and evaluating outputs. It arrives as a co-agent reflecting its [[stakeholders]]' values and, despite [[bias-mitigation]] efforts, embedding demographic, linguistic and [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] biases, so practice risks drifting toward what is easiest to generate and scale.

## What generative AI changes about thinking and knowing

Watch the interface: the learner queries, the system answers authoritatively, the exchange ends or iterates: instructionist pedagogy in a new medium. Variants built as cognitive partners using [[socratic-method|Socratic questioning]] or [[retrieval-spacing-interleaving|retrieval practice]] still conceive the interaction as one-to-one, leaving [[collaborative-learning]] unaddressed. The stronger claim is about distribution: classrooms spread teaching across people and rhythms, with teachers explaining, peers challenging, materials resisting, assessments imposing delay. GenAI compresses explanation, [[feedback]] and evaluation into one loop that smooths those frictions, shifting practice toward competencies centered on [[prompt-engineering|prompting]], with unstudied effects on [[motivation]]. One-to-one [[intelligent-tutoring]] displaces that resistance, and agreeable systems habituate students away from effortful [[problem-solving]], against [[desirable-difficulties]] and [[productive-failure|productive failure]] findings.

## Two misalignments: the pedagogical gap and the goal gap

The pedagogical gap is the mismatch between 4E-informed learning and the assumptions encoded in current systems, against a wider gap between the [[learning-sciences]] and design. That gap reflects disembodied frameworks across educational technology, and closing it needs reflection rather than technical innovation, since method, not medium, determines efficacy. GenAI in [[virtual-and-augmented-reality|mixed reality]] is structurally constrained, and tools built within [[teacher-role|teacher]]-centered [[learning-design]] models limit learner choice and overlook [[neurodiversity]] supports. The goal gap concerns aims: education is officially about collaboration, ethical formation and [[critical-thinking]], yet systems stay organized around efficiency and coverage under [[educational-policy-ai]]. Compressed-curriculum claims expose rather than answer whether [[learning-gains|academic achievement]] is schooling's primary aim, while STEM-centric AI research crowds out [[creativity]].

## Confabulation, epistemic authority, and the multitechnological ecology

Confabulation is the third concern: incorrect content delivered fluently and confidently, and asymmetrically dangerous because learners cannot check it. They also see a resource: awareness of [[hallucination-risk|hallucination]] can make verification integral to [[assessment]] rather than optional, and scaffold [[agency|epistemic agency]] rather than cast the learner as a recipient. The implication is institutional: knowledge was stabilized by teachers, textbooks and curricula, so an opaque probabilistic source forces schools to rethink how [[trust]] and accountability are distributed. The conclusion is a multitechnological cognitive ecology where no tool dominates and tools are combined by pedagogical aim, AI's energy and water costs included.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Make verification a graded part of the task: learners cannot separate fluent confabulation from accurate content, so source-checking belongs in assessment.

- **Instructors.** Choose tools by pedagogical affordance and keep low-tech, embodied and collaborative options in the mix rather than defaulting to a conversational system.

- **Designers.** Protect the frictions that carry conceptual change, and do not let the prompt-response loop define the interaction.

- **Researchers.** Treat the enhancement-versus-deskilling question as open: the cited evidence is preliminary, leaving intensive unguided LLM use unresolved.

## Limitations

- The paper generates no datasets, as its data availability statement says, so claims about classroom distribution and epistemic authority rest on illustrative cases.

- Its borrowed warrant is self-described as thin: pedagogically aligned AI implementations (refs. 14-16) against emerging evidence of eroded metacognitive capacity (ref. 17), leaving enhancement versus deskilling unresolved.

- The proposal is normative with nothing attached: no implementation, evaluation or measurement, its named systems will date, and the ecological cost that should bound the design is unquantified.
