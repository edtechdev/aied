> **Synthesis:** Chiappe and colleagues (2026) propose Productive Cognitive Latency (PCL) as a pedagogical construct for regulating when and how much generative-AI problem-solving capacity becomes available to a learner. The article begins from a paradox of cognitive benefit: learners with more knowledge are better placed to evaluate and direct AI assistance, while learners with less knowledge may need more help and be less able to judge what they receive. Repeated interactions can therefore form a virtuous cycle of cognitive expansion or a vicious cycle of cognitive contraction, depending on which operations remain with the learner. PCL is defined as the pedagogically regulated space during which a formatively valuable cognitive operation still requires learner activity before an external system resolves it; latency is not a fixed delay and does not mean starting every task without AI. The authors connect the construct to scaffolding, cognitive offloading, desirable difficulties, generative learning, expertise reversal, and productive failure, then outline how generative tutors and research designs could test it. This is a theoretical proposal: the article reports no empirical data, and its mechanisms are explicitly described as requiring validation across learning contexts.

## Key Findings
1. **A paradox sits at the center.** The same tool can expand a knowledgeable learner's judgment and substitute the very operations through which a less knowledgeable learner would develop that judgment.
2. **Two recursive trajectories are proposed.** Cognitive expansion leaves the next interaction with greater resources; cognitive contraction reduces the opportunities to exercise operations that would build independent capacity.
3. **Latency is not waiting.** A tutor can respond immediately and still preserve PCL by asking for a justification, pointing out an inconsistency, or offering a counterexample rather than a solution.
4. **The unit of analysis is relational.** An operation is protected only when performing it retains formative value for a particular learning objective; the same operation may be delegable in another task.
5. **Frequency of use is not dependence.** Intensive AI use can coexist with agency when delegation shifts toward operations that no longer carry formative value.
6. **PCL is proposed as distinct from scaffolding.** Scaffolding emphasizes contingency, fading, and transfer of responsibility; PCL adds the degree of external resolution and the learner activity that remains.
7. **Autonomy is an outcome, not only a prerequisite.** The authors propose "self-regulation of cognitive delegation" as a capacity that should be progressively transferred from teacher and system to learner.

## The paradox and its two trajectories

The argument draws on prior syntheses and experiments without presenting new data of its own. Chen and Cheung (2025) are cited for positive effects on achievement and higher-order thinking alongside heterogeneity across contexts and no significant aggregate effect on metacognition; Ma and Zhong (2025) are cited for an overall positive effect across 34 experimental and quasi-experimental studies; Han and colleagues (2025) for a moderate overall effect with very high heterogeneity. Kestin and colleagues (2025) are cited for a controlled trial in which a carefully designed generative tutor outperformed in-person active learning, with the authors stressing that the system intentionally incorporated scaffolding, sequencing, active learning, and feedback. Barcaui (2025) is cited for lower performance among students who used ChatGPT-4 freely during study despite initially more efficient assistance. The authors use these attributed findings to distinguish assisted performance from later independent capacity, not to claim that AI always helps or always harms.

Cognitive contraction is explicitly not neurocognitive deterioration: it names a pedagogical trajectory in which external resolution repeatedly replaces processes that would have contributed to knowledge, judgment, or autonomy. Cognitive expansion is the converse, in which an initial elaboration yields a more precise query, the generated response introduces alternatives, the learner evaluates and revises, and the revision improves the next question. Neither trajectory is treated as a permanent learner category; the same student can move between them across tasks, disciplines, knowledge states, and designs.

## The proposal in concrete terms

PCL regulates two things: the degree to which an external system performs the target cognitive operation and the point at which that performance becomes available. A novice may need frequent support while still needing particular operations to remain their own, because helping more does not require solving more on their behalf. In an argumentation task, the initial formulation of a thesis and its reasons might remain with the learner while AI generates counterarguments or tests consistency; in a task whose goal is critical evaluation, AI could generate arguments from the outset. In a simulation, generated perspectives can be present from the start because the learning lies in comparing, deciding, and justifying.

For generative tutors, the degree of problem-solving becomes a pedagogical variable: a question, a hint, a counterexample, an inconsistency, or a complete explanation are different interventions even when offered equally often. Personalization therefore means adjusting which part of the activity remains the learner's responsibility, not simply giving more help to novices. A Figure 1 schematic represents PCL as a regulatory mechanism between a learning demand and available GenAI problem-solving capacity, with expansion and contraction shown as hypothesized cumulative trajectories rather than consequences of a single episode.

The proposed empirical program starts with episode-level coding of how much of a formatively relevant operation GenAI performs, from questions and hints through partial execution to complete resolution. Preserved learner activity could be examined through intermediate artifacts, revisions, interaction logs, or verbal protocols. A randomized design would hold the frequency of assistance comparable while varying how much of the target operation AI performs, then measure assisted performance through product quality and independent capacity through a transfer task with reduced external resolution. A longitudinal repeated-measures design across several weeks or an academic semester would be needed to identify expansion or contraction as trajectories. The authors also urge tests of conditions where the proposal fails, such as excessive cognitive load or cases where studying a worked solution is more valuable than attempting the operation.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Name the cognitive operation that carries formative value for the objective before deciding where AI enters a task.
- **Instructors.** Keep frequent support compatible with learner responsibility by using questions, diagnoses, examples, and hints instead of complete solutions.
- **Instructors.** Do not treat visible effort as evidence of learning; a delay that leaves no valuable operation active is unproductive.
- **Researchers.** Code degree of external resolution separately from the amount of scaffolding, and test whether it predicts independent performance on later transfer tasks.
- **Tool builders.** Make response completeness adjustable and auditable, with transparency and learner control over assistance so regulation does not become algorithmic paternalism.

## Limitations
- The article reports no empirical data; the data availability statement says no new data were created or analyzed, and PCL is explicitly a theoretically derived construct rather than a validated instructional model.
- The proposed relationships among external resolution, preserved learner activity, and cumulative trajectories have not been directly tested as an integrated framework.
- Construct differentiation remains unresolved: PCL must show that degree of external resolution captures something beyond conventional scaffolding, cognitive effort, self-regulation, and frequency of AI use.
- Formative value is context dependent, so universal thresholds for high or low PCL are difficult to justify independently of a specific objective and learner.
- Expansion and contraction are proposed as cumulative trajectories; a single episode cannot establish either, and repeated observations over sufficient time are required.
- An adaptive tutor must infer what a learner knows and what still needs to remain theirs; inaccurate inferences can either substitute a formative operation prematurely or withhold help when progress is no longer possible.
