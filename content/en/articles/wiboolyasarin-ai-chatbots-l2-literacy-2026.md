> **Synthesis:** Wiboolyasarin, Wiboolyasarin, Kamonsawad, Tiranant and Jinowat (2026) asked what makes a gamified educational chatbot valuable for second-language reading and writing, then tested the answer rather than assuming it. Surveying 533 Thai pre-service teachers, in-service teachers and university lecturers, they built a design-feature questionnaire through literature synthesis, interviews, Q-methodology, expert review and a pilot, then reduced and refined it with exploratory and confirmatory modeling. Three interpretable domains emerged — adaptive, accessible and classroom-integrated support; gamified motivation and progress tracking; and pedagogically rich, culturally relevant content delivery — but the domains were too strongly correlated to stand as separate scales. A bifactor representation fit best and showed a dominant general factor of perceived pedagogical design quality with smaller domain-specific residuals. The authors present the resulting 39-item pool as provisional and require independent validation before evaluative use.

## Key Findings

- **Three content domains organize design priorities.** Educators distinguished adaptive, accessible and classroom-integrated support; gamified motivation and progress tracking; and pedagogically rich, culturally relevant content delivery.
- **The three domains did not hold up as separable constructs.** Confirmatory models found extremely high correlations between every pair of domains and failed standard discriminant checks, so the authors reject treating them as independent subscales.
- **A broad general factor dominated the item pool.** A strict one-factor model fit worse than the correlated three-domain model, but a bifactor model fit better than both, and most common and reliable variance belonged to an overall perception of pedagogical design quality.
- **Six items were removed on combined statistical and substantive grounds.** Near-duplicate, double-barreled, misallocated or expected-outcome items were dropped, leaving 39 items split 21, five and 13 across the domains.
- **Refinement sharpened measurement without changing the structure.** The refined bifactor model fit better than the refined three-domain model, while the general factor stayed dominant and domain-specific variance stayed small.
- **The measure is provisional by design.** The authors state that independent validation, cross-context replication and testing against functioning chatbots are needed before formal evaluation or comparative scoring.

## Who was surveyed and how the instrument was built

The sample comprised 533 respondents: 262 pre-service teachers (49.16%), 157 in-service teachers (29.46%) and 114 university lecturers (21.38%). Of these, 406 (76.17%) were female and 127 (23.83%) male. Affiliations were primarily universities (n = 362, 67.92%) and schools (n = 157, 29.46%), with a smaller college group (n = 14, 2.62%). Geographically, 209 respondents (39.21%) were based in Bangkok and the metropolitan area, followed by the northeastern (n = 116, 21.76%), central (n = 96, 18.01%), southern (n = 48, 9.01%), eastern (n = 24, 4.50%), northern (n = 23, 4.32%) and international locations (n = 17, 3.19%). A total of 358 participants (67.17%) reported 1-9 years of relevant study or teaching experience, while 175 (32.83%) reported 10 years or more. The questionnaire remained open for eight weeks, and participation was voluntary with informed consent.

Item development combined several sources. The authors synthesized prior research, then integrated field evidence from semi-structured interviews with 20 teachers or lecturers who taught Thai to non-native learners and 10 administrators or program personnel (n = 30), plus a Q-methodology study with 40 participants. An initial 60-item draft was reviewed by nine subject-matter experts — five language-teaching specialists and four instructional-technology specialists — using a four-point relevance scale. Item-level content validity ranged from .89 to 1.00, and the scale-level average was .98, yielding a refined 47-item instrument answered on a five-point Likert scale. A pilot with 30 respondents produced an overall reliability coefficient of .977, so all 47 items went forward to the main analysis. The psychometric work examined educators' perceptions of design features for a purpose-built [[conversational-ai|GAI-chatbot]] concept, not the effectiveness of a completed system.

## Three domains inside one strong common thread

Exploratory principal component analysis on the original 47 items was suitable (KMO = .985; Bartlett's χ²(1081) = 27,591.001, p < .001). Direct Oblimin rotation produced three components with eigenvalues of 30.660, 1.519 and 1.183, explaining 65.235%, 3.233% and 2.517% of the variance (70.984% cumulative). Two items lacked a clear primary loading of at least .40 and were removed, leaving 45 items: Adaptive, Accessible, and Classroom-Integrated Support (AACS; 25 items, loadings .438-.939), Gamified Motivation and Progress Tracking (GMPT; 5 items, .446-.485), and Pedagogically-Rich and Culturally-Relevant Content Delivery (PCCD; 15 items, .448-.895). Component correlations were .449 between AACS and GMPT, .833 between AACS and PCCD, and .420 between GMPT and PCCD.

The correlated three-factor confirmatory model achieved acceptable within-sample fit: χ²(942) = 3188.286, χ²/df = 3.385, CFI = .914, TLI = .909, RMSEA = .067 (90% CI [.064, .070]), SRMR = .015. Composite reliability ranged from .881 to .979 and average variance extracted from .597 to .655, supporting internal consistency and convergent validity. Discriminant validity failed: latent correlations were .883 (AACS-GMPT), .926 (AACS-PCCD) and .904 (GMPT-PCCD), the square roots of AVE (.809, .773, .807) fell below those correlations, and HTMT ratios were .884, .926 and .905. Educators' ratings therefore do not cleanly separate the three proposed domains.

## Why the bifactor model won

Alternative models tested whether the strong correlations reflected a broader construct. A strict one-factor model fitted substantially worse (χ² = 4158.817, df = 945, χ²/df = 4.401, CFI = .877, TLI = .871, RMSEA = .080 [.077, .082], AIC = 4338.817, BIC = 4723.884). The higher-order model reproduced the correlated three-factor fit exactly because its second-order portion was just-identified; its standardized second-order loadings were .951, .928 and .974, and the general factor explained 86.2%-94.9% of the variance in the three first-order domains. The orthogonal bifactor model provided comparatively better fit (χ² = 2803.252, df = 900, χ²/df = 3.115, CFI = .927, TLI = .920, RMSEA = .063 [.060, .066], AIC = 3073.252, BIC = 3650.852). Its diagnostics indicated dominance of the general factor: ECV = .910, ωH = .964, ωT = .989 and PUC = .581. In plain terms, most of what the items measure together is one broad perception of pedagogical design quality, with narrower residual domains around it.

## Refinement to a provisional 39-item pool

Six items were removed only where statistical evidence converged with substantive problems: expected outcomes rather than concrete features, near-duplicates, double-barreled wording and ambiguous domain allocation. The fixed pool held 39 items — 21 AACS, five GMPT and 13 PCCD. The refined PCA remained highly factorable (KMO = .984; Bartlett's χ²(741) = 22,225.426, p < .001), with three components above eigenvalue 1.0 (25.410, 1.383, 1.095) explaining 71.507% of the variance; the first alone explained 65.154%. Cronbach's alpha for the 39-item pool was .986. The refined correlated three-factor model showed modestly improved incremental fit (χ²(699) = 2390.009, χ²/df = 3.419, CFI = .923, TLI = .919, RMSEA = .067 [.064, .070]), but latent correlations stayed very high (.882, .921, .900) and HTMT ratios remained .885, .922 and .902. The refined bifactor model again fit better (χ² = 2043.799, df = 663, χ²/df = 3.083, CFI = .937, TLI = .930, RMSEA = .063 [.059, .066], AIC = 2277.799, BIC = 2778.386). General-factor loadings ranged from .614 to .854, specific-factor loadings were smaller (.000-.402, .190-.359, .122-.413), and the diagnostics stayed general-dominated: ECV = .892, PUC = .598, ωT = .988, ωH = .949, with ωH/ωT = .961. The authors conclude that AACS, GMPT and PCCD remain useful labels for organizing design priorities, but the evidence does not justify three validated subscales.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Treat chatbot selection as one integrated pedagogical decision rather than a menu of features: adaptivity, accessibility, gamified progress and culturally relevant content are judged together, so a tool strong on points but weak on feedback quality reads as poor design.
- **Instructional designers.** When specifying or evaluating a tool, ask whether each promised feature is concrete and observable — error-specific feedback, adjustable difficulty, multilingual navigation, teacher-facing progress tools — rather than an expected outcome such as improved motivation.
- **Educational technology developers.** Prioritize the infrastructure these educators weighted most: proficiency-sensitive adaptation, cross-platform accessibility and classroom integration, with gamification tied to literacy behaviors rather than applied as surface rewards.
- **Administrators.** Fund professional development that prepares teachers to mediate, curate and assess chatbot-supported work; the framework is a discussion instrument, not a validated scoring system, so it should not yet drive consequential decisions.

## Limitations

- Every stage used the same 533-person sample, so the evidence is within-sample psychometrics rather than independent cross-validation.
- The study is situated primarily in Thailand; the authors state that applicability to other languages and educational systems should not be assumed, and measurement invariance was not tested.
- Participants rated a conceptualized purpose-built chatbot, not a functioning system, so the findings indicate what educators consider important rather than what improves reading or writing performance; the cross-sectional design supports no causal claim.
- Discriminant validity among AACS, GMPT and PCCD was not established, so they remain content labels rather than validated subscales.
- The six-item refinement was also conducted on the same sample and requires prespecified independent replication.
