> **Synthesis:** Gao and colleagues (2026) ask who becomes over-reliant on [[generative-ai|generative AI]] and why, studying 249 foreign-language majors at a Chinese higher vocational college with a sequential explanatory [[mixed-methods-research|mixed-methods]] design. The answer upends the competence account: fear of missing out (β = 0.50) and anthropomorphic attachment (β = 0.27) were the strongest predictors, while perceived [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] (r = 0.08) and [[self-regulated-learning|self-regulated learning]] (r = −0.03) showed no zero-order relation to over-reliance. What did predict it was the gap between feeling capable and reporting that one regulates one's own learning (β = 0.21), and the [[latent-profile-analysis|person-centered analysis]] agreed: the profile low in affective engagement reported the least over-reliance (M = 3.19) and the uniformly high profile the most (M = 5.30). Interviews with five learners suggest that humanlike warmth, peer-normalized standard-lowering, and a competence that does not restrain behavior turn an affective pull into habitual delegation. The study is a direct challenge to the [[educational-policy-ai|policy]] intuition that [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] alone will make students use these tools more sparingly.

*Sequential explanatory mixed-methods study; every construct is [[self-report-measures|self-reported]] and the [[quantitative-research|quantitative]] strand is cross-sectional, so the authors describe associations and learner accounts rather than demonstrated causation.*

## Key Findings

- **Affective engagement dominated.** Fear of missing out (β = 0.50) and anthropomorphic attachment (β = 0.27) were the strongest predictors of self-reported over-reliance in the full model.
- **Self-rated competence did not protect.** Perceived AI literacy, self-regulated learning and critical evaluation were unrelated to over-reliance at the zero-order level.
- **The gap between feeling literate and regulating predicted over-reliance.** Learners whose sense of AI capability outran their self-direction reported more over-reliance (β = 0.21), and the measure survived controls for acquiescent responding.
- **Profiles agreed with the variable-centered result.** Learners low in affective engagement reported the least over-reliance, the middling profile an intermediate level, and the uniformly high profile the most.
- **The two affective predictors overlap heavily.** Fear of missing out and anthropomorphic attachment correlated strongly as observed scores, so their opposite-signed partial coefficients cannot be read separately.
- **Interviews point to process, not trait.** Five learners described humanlike warmth lowering the cost of delegation, peer-normalized fear of falling behind eroding standards, and enacted self-regulation interrupting the pattern.

## Two accounts of over-reliance

The paper sets a competence account against an affective account. The competence account holds that over-reliance reflects what learners lack — insufficient literacy, weak self-regulation, poor critical evaluation — and therefore counsels skills training. The affective account holds that it reflects how learners relate to the tool emotionally — fear of missing out, quasi-social attachment, [[anxiety-and-stress|anxiety]] — and therefore counsels attention to the relationship. The data support the affective account at the zero-order level and qualify the competence account in a specific way: perceived literacy does not protect, but the distance between perceived literacy and enacted regulation does predict. The authors are careful to note that because both constructs are self-reported, the study contrasts self-reported affect with self-rated competence, not with competence as tested.

## Why this population matters

The choice of higher vocational foreign-language learners is deliberate. Over-reliance is most feared among learners with weaker language foundations and fewer resources, yet most evidence on generative AI in [[language-learning|language learning]] comes from selective [[higher-ed|universities]] and proficient learners. The tasks generative AI performs most readily — translating, drafting, correcting — are precisely the tasks through which language proficiency develops, so delegation carries a direct cost in this setting. The authors also connect the findings to the broader psychology of problematic technology use, where affective and [[motivation|motivational]] pulls rather than skill deficits sustain the behavior, and to emerging work modeling teacher dependence on generative AI as an affect-laden habit.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Do not assume that students who understand AI better will use it more sparingly; in this sample feeling literate did not restrain use, so pair literacy teaching with explicit regulation practice.
- **Instructional designers.** Design tasks that make self-regulation visible and necessary — drafts, comparisons, and justification steps — because the learners who reported enacting regulation were the ones who resisted over-reliance.
- **Faculty developers.** Address the emotional relationship learners form with these tools directly, since warmth and fear of missing out tracked over-reliance more closely than self-rated competence.
- **Researchers.** Distinguish self-rated competence from tested competence in over-reliance research, and measure the gap between perceived capability and enacted regulation rather than either alone.

## Limitations

- Every construct is self-reported, so the design cannot adjudicate whether tested competence would behave differently from self-rated competence.
- The quantitative strand is cross-sectional and the interviews are retrospective, so the process language describes what learners reported rather than demonstrated temporal causation.
- Both strands come from one vocational college, so the findings describe this sample and may not transfer to other populations or subject areas.
- The Uniformly high profile did not survive scrutiny for response bias, so the profile analysis should be read with that caution.
- Over-reliance is defined against a learning standard rather than as compulsive use, and the authors note the construct overlaps with, but is not identical to, problematic-use dependency.
