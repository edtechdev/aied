> **Synthesis:** Li, Abdul Hanid and Zhang (2026) ask how much [[agency|effortful, self-directed control]] learners keep over their own reasoning when they work with [[generative-ai|generative AI]]. Following [[meta-analysis-systematic-review|PRISMA]] 2020, they screened Scopus and Web of Science for 2021–2025 and appraised 93 studies with the [[mixed-methods-research|Mixed Methods]] Appraisal Tool, then ran a realist synthesis that crosses the kind of [[cognitive-offloading|work offloaded]] (extraneous versus germane, from [[cognitive-psychology|cognitive load theory]]) with how learners process AI output (heuristic acceptance versus analytical scrutiny, from dual-process theory). The central result is an [[educational-measurement|measurement]] gap rather than a settled effect: 69 of the 93 studies report no learning measure taken independently of AI assistance, and only four combine processing evidence with such a measure — the combination required to tell [[cognitive-offloading|cognitive extension]] apart from substitution. The review restates the Heterogeneous Pathways of Distributed Agency (HPDA) Model as seven testable propositions and specifies the process-tracing and AI-independent outcome designs needed to test them.

*Systematic realist synthesis of 93 peer-reviewed studies; the reviewers code reported evidence only under manifest coding and add design-based inference under latent coding, and they contact authors for missing information.*

## Key Findings

1. **All four configurations of the program theory occur, but coverage is thin.** Where studies report evidence on both axes, nine show peripheral offloading with analytical scrutiny, ten core offloading with scrutiny, and one each core or peripheral offloading with heuristic acceptance.
2. **The decisive measurement is largely missing.** Of 93 studies, 85 report what students delegate, 44 report how they treat AI output, and 24 report what they learn without it; 69 report no AI-independent learning measure at all, and only four combine processing evidence with one.
3. **[[self-report-measures|Self-report]] dominates the processing evidence.** Of the 44 studies reporting how learners treat AI output, 22 rely on self-report or authors' statements rather than observed behavior.
4. **Substitution is documented, but mostly by inference.** Substitution appears as reported dependence, inability to explain AI-assisted work, or reduced independent performance in 13 of the 16 Path A studies; one study reports a fully specified substitution configuration.
5. **Heterogeneity is reported more often than explained.** Group or condition differences or part-sample outcomes appear in 30 studies, yet no coded learner, task, or [[assessment|assessment]] moderator shows an interpretable corpus-level association with the final pathway.
6. **Allocation behaves as a portfolio, not a trait.** In 29 studies learners both delegated core and peripheral work and both scrutinized and accepted output within the same task, supporting continuous measurement of the share of interactions that delegate core work and the share of outputs that are verified.

## The two axes: what is offloaded, and what is done with the output

The review's organizing move is to separate two questions that the literature often merges. The first is allocation: which cognitive work moves to the tool. Drawing on cognitive load theory, the authors distinguish extraneous work — effort that does not serve the learning goal — from germane work that does. The second is processing: whether the learner accepts the tool's output heuristically or scrutinizes it analytically, a distinction borrowed from dual-process theory. Crossing the axes yields four configurations. Cognitive Extension pairs peripheral offloading with scrutiny; Surface-level Friction pairs peripheral offloading with heuristic acceptance; the two substitution configurations pair core offloading with either mode of processing. The point is that a learner can offload a great deal and still think hard about the result, or offload very little and still delegate the reasoning that matters.

## What the corpus actually measured

The evidence audit is the review's most consequential contribution, and it reads as a warning about the field's instruments rather than a verdict on the technology. Most studies establish that students use generative AI, and a smaller set establishes what they do with what it returns; very few establish what they can do without it. Because extension and substitution predict opposite outcomes — accumulated competence versus eroded independent capability — the missing AI-independent measure means the corpus cannot adjudicate between them. The authors are explicit that their manifest coding reports only what studies state, and that latent coding adds design-based inference, which is why the configurations are described as observable in the evidence rather than as proven effects.

## From classification to testable theory

The HPDA Model converts the classification into seven propositions with an explicit evidential status: three of the four transitional propositions remain untested and the fourth has outcome-level support only. Two design implications follow. First, process-tracing designs should measure allocation and its moderators in the same sample rather than inferring mechanism from contrasts between studies. Second, outcome designs should include an AI-independent learning measure, since that is the only way to distinguish extension from substitution. The Matthew Effect is offered as a further hypothesis — repeated AI interactions may amplify initial differences — but no study in the corpus follows learners long enough to test it.

## What this means for practice

- **[[teacher-role|Instructors]].** Treat "students used AI" as an uninformative outcome; design tasks whose score depends on a step the learner must perform without the tool, so that extension and substitution become distinguishable in the work you read.
- **Instructional designers.** Build prompts and activities that require the learner to verify, transform, or reject AI output, and record whether they did — scrutiny is the axis that separates extension from substitution.
- **Researchers.** Report an AI-independent learning measure alongside any measure of AI use, and code both the work offloaded and the processing of output, so that extension and substitution can be told apart across studies.
- **Educational technology developers.** Instrument the share of interactions that delegate core reasoning and the share of outputs that are verified, because allocation behaves as a portfolio rather than a stable disposition.

## Limitations

- The review synthesizes what primary studies report; under manifest coding it cannot manufacture evidence the corpus does not contain, and the authors treat design-based inference as a separate, weaker layer.
- The corpus is limited to English-language, peer-reviewed work indexed in Scopus and Web of Science between 2021 and 2025, so it excludes book chapters, reports, and non-English studies.
- No moderator shows an interpretable corpus-level association with the final pathway, so the model's moderator layers remain hypotheses rather than findings.
- The review cannot test the Matthew Effect or any [[longitudinal-research|longitudinal]] claim, because no included study follows learners long enough.
- The 2 × 2 quadrants describe configurations at the point of observation; the authors note that allocation is better treated as a continuous portfolio, which the current studies do not measure that way.
