> **Synthesis:** Wang (2026) proposes a design-based research framework reorienting translator education away from routine translation skills — increasingly performed end-to-end by agentic translation systems — toward four competencies: cross-disciplinary knowledge (D1), AI orchestration competence (D2), higher-order strategic judgment (D3), and critical AI evaluation (D4). The framework includes a translation-task displacement inventory mapping 18 sub-tasks to current AI capability and remaining human role, draft assessment rubrics, and a pre-registered 12-month DBR protocol (5-person co-design team, five pilot participants). As a protocol paper it reports no empirical data: the framework's effectiveness is untested and contingent on the planned pilot's results, and its transferability beyond a single Chinese institutional context is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Summary

Agentic translation systems — autonomous multi-agent architectures that plan, orchestrate tools, and produce end-to-end translations — increasingly perform the routine translation tasks translator-education programs were historically designed to teach. Without reform, the author argues, programs risk graduating students into occupational categories projected to be increasingly automated. This protocol paper responds with a framework grounded in distributed cognition, task-based automation theory, human-AI collaboration theory, AI literacy, and the European Master's in Translation (EMT) competence framework.

The framework's components are threefold. First, a **translation-task displacement inventory** maps 18 translation sub-tasks to current AI capability, displacement likelihood, and the remaining human role — each displacement-likelihood rating annotated [E] (supported by published empirical evidence) or [P] (based on the author's informed projection), a transparency convention that keeps speculation distinguishable from evidence. Second, a **four-dimensional competence framework** (D1–D4) with draft assessment rubrics redirects training from producing translations toward knowing when to delegate, how to orchestrate agents, when to intervene strategically, and how to critically evaluate output. Third, a **12-month design-based research protocol** with pre-registered evaluation criteria on the Open Science Framework specifies a 5-person faculty co-design team and five separately recruited pilot participants, with implementation to be reported in a subsequent paper after ethical approval.

## Key Findings

- **Routine-skill displacement is the framework's premise, and the inventory makes it auditable.** The 18-sub-task inventory rates each task's displacement likelihood with an [E]/[P] provenance tag — a reporting convention that separates evidence-backed claims from the author's projections and makes the framework's empirical foundation inspectable rather than asserted.
- **Four competencies replace routine production as the training target.** D1 cross-disciplinary knowledge (subject-matter expertise to direct and verify agentic output), D2 AI orchestration competence (decomposing tasks, sequencing agent actions, managing tool chains), D3 higher-order strategic judgment (deciding what to delegate and when to intervene), D4 critical AI evaluation (verifying quality, detecting errors, owning the result).
- **The contribution is the method, not just the content.** The framework's operationalization is explicitly developed through a co-design process rather than fixed by the paper; the reproducible small-team methodology and transferable protocol design are themselves offered as contributions, alongside the rubrics.
- **Pre-registration and staged evidence.** Evaluation criteria are pre-registered on OSF, and the paper's claims are staged: the framework is presented as a design foundation whose warrant depends on the pilot's results — no educational effectiveness is claimed in this paper.
- **Grounded in existing competence frameworks rather than invented de novo.** The design builds on the EMT framework's higher-order/strategic sub-competences, distributed cognition, and human-AI collaboration theory, positioning itself as an operationalization of established constructs for the agentic-AI era.

## What this means for practice

- **Program designers (translation and beyond).** The displacement-inventory method transfers to any professional program whose routine tasks agentic systems increasingly perform: list the sub-tasks, rate displacement likelihood with provenance tags, and identify what human role remains — then redesign assessment around the remaining human work rather than the automated output.
- **Instructors in AI-exposed disciplines.** Treat AI orchestration as a teachable competence distinct from AI literacy: D2 covers decomposing tasks and sequencing agent actions, skills that "knowing how to use AI" framing typically omits.
- **Assessment designers.** The draft rubrics target delegation judgment (when to let the agent work vs. intervene) and critical evaluation of agentic output — competences invisible to assessments that score only the final translated product.
- **Faculty developers.** The 5-person co-design + pilot structure is a reproducible small-scale implementation pathway for curriculum reform where full-scale redesign is infeasible — the methodology is explicitly offered as transferable.
- **Researchers evaluating the framework.** The paper's honesty about what it does not establish (no empirical data, single-context, effectiveness untested) is the right model for reading any protocol paper: the framework is a hypothesis with a pre-registered test, not evidence.

## Limitations

- **No empirical data.** This is a protocol paper: the framework's educational effectiveness is entirely untested, and the paper says so — its warrant is contingent on the planned pilot's results, and ethics approval for the baseline survey and pilot was still pending at writing.
- **Single national and institutional context.** All design work is set at one university in China; cross-cultural and cross-institutional transferability is explicitly a hypothesis requiring replication.
- **Displacement ratings are part evidence, part projection.** The [P]-tagged ratings rest on the author's informed judgment about where AI capability is heading, not on measurement of current systems; the inventory is a design instrument, not a capability audit.
- **Co-design introduces the designers' priors.** The framework's operationalization is developed through the co-design team, so the resulting rubrics and curriculum prototype embed the team's conceptions of competence — a known design-based-research limitation the author acknowledges alongside context-dependence.
- **Longitudinal outcome questions unaddressed.** Whether graduates trained under the framework achieve better career outcomes than conventionally trained peers is left to future follow-up.
