> **Synthesis:** Semenikhina and colleagues (2026) tested a structured [[generative-ai|ChatGPT]]-mediated approach to linguistic [[creativity]] in Ukrainian [[higher-ed|higher education]]. They define the construct as four components — text production, figurative-expressive expression, transformation, and motivation — and link each to specific AI-mediated tasks. A quasi-experiment compared 88 first- and second-year students in six intact academic groups at three Ukrainian institutions: 45 students in an experimental group using structured ChatGPT tasks and 43 in a control group receiving traditional language instruction. Both groups improved over one semester, but the experimental group improved more strongly: its mean rose from 3.46 to 5.86 while the control group's rose from 3.26 to 4.14, and no experimental-group student remained at the low level by the end. [[qualitative-research|Qualitative]] observations placed the clearest changes in text production and genre-stylistic transformation, with more moderate change in figurative expression. The design was not randomized and the instrument had moderate internal consistency, so the authors present the results as evidence of stronger short-term dynamics rather than a general causal effect.

## Key Findings
1. **Creativity was broken into four teachable components.** Text production, figurative-expressive choice, transformation of genre and addressee, and motivation for creative language activity were each tied to specific learning actions.
2. **The AI tasks were methodically structured.** Students formulated a prompt, obtained variants, compared them, edited critically, explained their choice, and produced their own final text rather than copying a response.
3. **Both groups gained over the semester.** Traditional instruction also produced statistically significant change, so the finding is a stronger and faster gain under AI-mediated tasks, not a failure of conventional teaching.
4. **The low level disappeared in the experimental group.** Every experimental-group student reached the medium level by the final stage, while no student in either group reached the sufficient level.
5. **Text production and transformation changed most visibly.** Students wrote longer, more coherent texts and handled genre, style, tone, addressee, and format changes more successfully.
6. **Figurative expression lagged.** More [[metaphors-and-analogies|metaphors]] and epithets appeared, but their use was not always stylistically accurate, so generating variants did not by itself build stylistic sensitivity.
7. **Multilingual comparison was part of the intervention.** Students checked Ukrainian formulations against machine translations, identified calques from English, and argued for natural Ukrainian equivalents.

## What the study measured

Linguistic creativity was operationalized through an adapted questionnaire with four scales and eight items scored in 0.5-point increments, summed into an overall score. The published bands are low level up to and including 3.0 points, medium level 3.5–7.0 points, and sufficient level 7.5 points and above. Internal consistency for the whole sample at the initial stage was alpha = .67, described by the authors as moderate and acceptable for an exploratory [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] study. Levels were also described qualitatively: a sufficient text-production response was coherent and communicatively complete, a medium response was comprehensible but stylistically simple, and a low response was short or fragmentary.

The intervention ran for one semester in 2025 across sections of the Ukrainian language course on Lexicology, Phraseology, Morphology, Syntax, and Text. Diagnostics took place at stage 1, an intermediate recording was made two months after the start of classes at stage 2, and final diagnostics followed at stage 3. The experimental group used ChatGPT as a language assistant, a source of variants, a [[simulation|simulator]] of communicative situations, a transformation tool, and a means of reflective editing. In the control group the same topics were studied with traditional tasks. Participants were told to evaluate AI output critically, check content, explain their own decisions, and avoid direct copying. Ethical approval is reported as Protocol No. 4 of 19 December 2024 from the Academic Council of Sumy State Pedagogical University.

## What the results show

At stage 1 the groups were statistically comparable: the control mean was 3.26 and the experimental mean was 3.46, with t = −0.54; p = .589; Cohen's d = −0.12. In the control group, 21 students started at the low level and 22 at the medium level; by stage 3 the counts were 13 low and 30 medium. In the experimental group, 19 started low and 26 medium, and by stage 3 no student was low and all 45 were medium. No participant in either group reached the sufficient level at the initial stage or at the final stage.

Descriptive results show the control mean moving from 3.26 to 4.14 and the experimental mean from 3.46 to 5.86. Variability in the control group stayed relatively high and slightly increased by stage 3, where SD = 2.00; in the experimental group, SD decreased from 1.60 at stage 1 to 0.95 at stage 3, indicating a tighter clustering within the medium range.

Within-group paired tests were significant in both groups. The control group changed between stage 1 and stage 2 (t = −5.57; p < .001; Cohen's d = −0.85), between stage 2 and stage 3 (t = −3.10; p = .003; Cohen's d = −0.47), and across the full period (t = −5.48; p < .001; Cohen's d = −0.84). The experimental group changed between stage 1 and stage 2 (t = −10.55; p < .001; Cohen's d = −1.57), between stage 2 and stage 3 (t = −4.20; p < .001; Cohen's d = −0.63), and from stage 1 to stage 3 (t = −10.80; p < .001; Cohen's d = −1.61). Between-group differences were not significant at stage 1, became significant at stage 2 (t = −2.54; p = .013; Cohen's d = −0.54), and grew at stage 3 (t = −5.18; p < .001; Cohen's d = −1.10). The authors read this sequence as stronger positive dynamics under structured AI-mediated instruction while acknowledging that traditional instruction also helped.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Build AI tasks as a sequence of prompt, variants, comparison, critical selection, editing, and a learner-authored final version rather than a single generation request.
- **Instructors.** Tie each task to a named component of creativity, with genre and addressee changes for transformation and [[critical-thinking|critical analysis]] of metaphors for figurative expression.
- **Instructors.** In multilingual settings, ask students to identify calques, check stylistic naturalness, and defend their own linguistic choice against the generated alternative.
- **Researchers.** Treat the [[motivation|motivational]] component cautiously because its indicators relied largely on [[self-assessment]] and observation rather than independent performance measures.
- **Researchers.** Test whether gains persist after AI support ends and whether the four-component instrument transfers to other languages and disciplines.

## Limitations
- The sample was 88 students from three Ukrainian institutions in specific fields of study, which the authors say does not permit broad generalization to other programs or national systems.
- Participants were not randomly assigned to conditions because intact academic groups were retained within the regular educational process; initial comparability reduces but does not eliminate extraneous influences.
- One semester produced positive dynamics but cannot establish long-term stability once systematic AI support ends.
- The diagnostic instrument had moderate internal consistency, and some indicators depended on self-assessment, particularly for the motivational component.
- The study was conducted in Ukrainian language training, so transfer to foreign-[[language-learning|language learning]], English-medium instruction, or multilingual international courses requires caution.
- ChatGPT was used mainly for text-based tasks; [[multimodal]] work with images, audio, video, pronunciation, intonation, or visual rhetoric was not analyzed, and authorship, disclosure, bias, and [[privacy|data protection]] were not separately tested.
