> **Synthesis:** Chen and colleagues developed a Chinese-language self-report scale for medical students that brings digital systems, health data and artificial intelligence into six domains: readiness, theoretical literacy, application skills, health data practices and interpretation, [[critical-thinking|critical thinking]] and ethical literacy. Two Delphi rounds with 16 experts shaped a 34-item field test, and 710 usable [[self-report-measures|questionnaires]] from one university were split into exploratory and confirmatory samples. Item refinement produced a 32-item scale with high internal consistency; the correlated six-factor model fit the tested data better than five-, four- and single-factor alternatives but still showed limited absolute fit, and validity evidence varied by domain. The authors present the scale as a tool for exploring perceived learning needs and mapping curricula, not as a validated measure of demonstrated competence.

## Key Findings
1. **The construct is deliberately broad.** Digital-intelligence literacy covers digital systems, health data and AI in medical contexts, rather than [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] alone.
2. **Six domains were specified in advance.** Expert review supported the framework, but empirical factor structure differed in some item assignments.
3. **Two non-overlapping samples were used.** Exploratory and confirmatory analyses used 344 and 366 students respectively.
4. **The item pool was shortened twice.** A 34-item field test became 33 items and then a 32-item scale after problematic items were removed.
5. **Internal consistency was strong.** Overall alpha was high, while domain-level coefficients were more modest.
6. **Relative model comparison favored six factors.** The correlated six-factor model beat the tested alternatives, though overall fit remained limited.
7. **Validity evidence was mixed.** Some domains showed acceptable average variance extracted, and several domain pairs exceeded common discriminant-validity reference values.

## What the study measured

The initial framework was built from literature on digital, data and AI literacy and yielded 46 indicators; 33 were retained for two Delphi rounds with 16 experts from [[medical-education|health professions education]], clinical medicine, public health, educational technology, medical informatics, AI and measurement. In round 1, all 33 indicators met the prespecified domain-assignment agreement criterion of 0.85, with mean agreement of 0.983. Round 2 led to splitting one critical-thinking indicator into separate items on awareness of AI limitations and verification of AI-generated references, producing a 34-item field-test questionnaire.

Data were collected in April 2026 at one Chinese university. Of 1118 returned questionnaires, 369 were excluded for failed or missing attention checks, four for missing scale items and 35 for near-uniform response patterns, leaving 710. The mean age was 21.49 years; 433 participants were female and 277 male; 700 were undergraduates; and clinical medicine was the largest program at 360 participants, with fifth-year students accounting for 315. Sample A of 344 was used for principal components and exploratory factor analysis, and Sample B of 366 for confirmatory factor analysis using WLSMV for ordinal responses.

Exploratory analysis of the 34-item pool indicated seven factors and flagged reverse-worded items; parallel analysis on the revised 32-item version indicated six factors. In the confirmatory sample, A8 had a negligible loading and was removed, then F5 showed a negative loading despite reverse coding and was removed. The final 32-item scale has seven readiness items and five in each other domain, with standardized loadings from 0.419 to 0.850. The highest latent correlation was between critical thinking and ethical literacy at r = 0.854.

## What the psychometrics showed

The correlated six-factor model of the 32-item scale gave robust CFI = 0.802, robust TLI = 0.782, robust RMSEA = 0.090 with a 90% CI of 0.085–0.095, and SRMR = 0.072. The authors describe these indices as indicating limitations in overall model fit. The six-factor model nonetheless fit better than three five-factor alternatives, a four-factor model and a single-factor model, each of which showed lower robust CFI and TLI and higher robust RMSEA and SRMR.

In the full sample, overall Cronbach's alpha was 0.938 and domain alphas ranged from 0.726 for ethical literacy to 0.871 for health data practices and interpretation; ordinal-score omega ranged from 0.717 to 0.906 across domains. Average variance extracted ranged from 0.405 for readiness to 0.644 for health data practices, with application skills at 0.596 and the remaining four domains between 0.405 and 0.454. Heterotrait–monotrait ratios across 15 domain pairs ranged from 0.544 to 0.858, with the highest values for application skills versus health data practices and for critical thinking versus ethical literacy. The Fornell–Larcker criterion was met for only three of the 15 pairs. The authors conclude that the scale offers interpretable domain profiles and a descriptive overall composite rather than a measure of one underlying ability.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Use domain profiles to identify perceived learning needs in areas such as AI-output verification, data handling and professional responsibility.
- **Curriculum designers.** Map existing medical courses against the six domains before adding new content, since the domains are conceptually distinct in the instrument.
- **Assessment designers.** Treat the equally weighted 32-item composite as a descriptive summary, not as a single ability score.
- **Researchers.** Combine self-report profiles with knowledge tests, data-processing tasks or [[simulation|simulations]] before drawing conclusions about competence.
- **Administrators.** Interpret results from one institution and one language context cautiously pending independent multicenter validation.

## Limitations
- The study used non-probability sampling at a single university, with high proportions of clinical medicine and fifth-year students and fewer postgraduates.
- Possible clustering by teaching class was not modeled, since intraclass correlations and design effects were not estimated.
- The final 32-item model was not fully independently confirmed because confirmatory results informed removal of two items; absolute fit was limited.
- The instrument measures self-reported dispositions and practices rather than demonstrated competence, and ethical items may be affected by social desirability.
- Test–retest reliability, measurement invariance, external criteria and the English translations were not evaluated, and some items reflect Chinese digital-media context.
