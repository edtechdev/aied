> **Synthesis:** Bahassi, Azmi and Khiat review how AI-based education might move toward what they call cognitive education, organizing the literature around educational applications, enabling technologies and intelligent educational systems. A narrative review searched Scopus and Web of Science in March 2026 and retained 94 articles after screening 9698 records; study-level coding and document-level co-occurrences describe which application–technology pairings appear in that corpus. The authors then propose six functional orientations and examine six cognitive capabilities using an interpretive [[rubrics|rubric]] applied to selected platforms. They conclude that learning, adaptation and basic interaction are already established in many systems, while context awareness, perception and explicit reasoning remain emerging, and that none of the platforms examined met their proposed cognition criterion. The paper presents cognitive education as a conceptual research direction requiring empirical validation, not as a demonstrated new paradigm.

## Key Findings
1. **Three dimensions structure the review.** Educational applications, [[ai-technologies|AI technologies]] and the systems or platforms that implement them are analyzed together rather than in isolation.
2. **The corpus is modest after screening.** Two searches produced 9698 records; 94 articles remained after duplicate removal and two screening stages.
3. **Coding used predefined dimensions.** Each study could receive multiple application, technology and system codes, and co-occurrence counts describe documents rather than effects.
4. **Common pairings are reported separately for [[primary-education|primary]] studies and reviews.** The authors distinguish document-level co-occurrences in primary work from the broader coverage supplied by reviews.
5. **Six functional orientations are proposed.** Automation, assistance, intelligence, adaptation, generation and cognition describe what systems aim to do.
6. **Six cognitive capabilities are examined.** Perception, learning, context awareness, reasoning, adaptation and interaction are assessed through an interpretive rubric.
7. **No platform met the cognition criterion.** The authors treat this as a limitation of current systems and of the proposed framework's evidence base, not as a measured learning outcome.

## What the review covered

The narrative review combined a main database search of 7488 records with complementary targeted searches yielding 2210 records, for a combined 9698. After 3357 duplicates were removed, 6341 records were screened; 6106 were excluded at title and abstract, leaving 235 for full text, and 141 more were excluded, leaving 94 articles. Searches covered 2018 to 2026, [[cs-education|Computer Science]] and [[social-sciences-education|Social Sciences]], English-language articles, conference papers and book chapters. Coding was performed by one author using a predefined codebook, with multi-label coding and a second pass by the same author to harmonize categories. Gray literature and additional empirical or [[meta-analysis-systematic-review|meta-analytic]] sources informed the discussion but were not part of the coded corpus.

The application dimension includes personalized and [[adaptive-learning|adaptive learning]], intelligent tutoring, recommendations, [[automated-assessment|automated grading]] and assessment, virtual assistants and [[conversational-ai|chatbots]], emotion and engagement monitoring, [[academic-integrity|academic integrity]], [[simulation|simulations]] and [[virtual-and-augmented-reality|immersive learning]]. The technology dimension covers machine and deep learning, [[educational-nlp|natural language processing]], computer vision and [[multimodal|multimodal AI]], knowledge representation and reasoning, and [[generative-ai|generative AI]] including [[llm|large language models]]. The systems dimension includes adaptive and [[intelligent-tutoring|intelligent tutoring systems]], learning management systems, [[recommender-systems-and-learning-paths|recommender systems]], chatbots, assessment tools and immersive platforms. The review's co-occurrence tables report, for example, 46 documents pairing adaptive learning with machine or deep learning, 36 pairing it with natural language processing, and 29 pairing it with knowledge representation and reasoning; these are document counts, not evidence of effectiveness.

## From functions to cognitive education

The six functional orientations describe system roles: automation of routine tasks, assistance to learners and teachers, intelligence for inference and diagnosis, adaptation of interventions, generation of content and dialogue, and cognition as the proposed integrative orientation. Orientations and capabilities have a many-to-many relationship: adapting an activity may involve interpreting responses, updating a [[student-modeling|learner model]], considering the task and selecting an intervention. The six capabilities are then scored on a rubric applied to DreamBox Math, MATHia, ALEKS, Khanmigo and Squirrel AI. The authors stress that the scores are a [[qualitative-research|qualitative]], interpretative application of their rubric, based on scientific literature and technical documentation, and do not constitute independently validated measures.

The cross-dimensional framework has four layers: AI-supported educational functions, enabling technologies, educational systems and platforms, and cognitive capabilities, capped by cognitive education as a continuous [[feedback|feedback loop]] that identifies learner needs, adapts activities and responds to progress and context. In the conclusion, the authors state that within the rubric none of the platforms met the cognition criterion, which requires both the highest score for each capability and integrated operation. They also state that the framework offers a foundation for analysis and future system design rather than introducing a new educational paradigm.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Select AI tools by the educational function they serve, distinguishing content generation from analysis, feedback and assessment support.
- **Instructors.** Ask whether a system adapts to evidence of learning or only delivers content faster before treating it as cognitively capable.
- **Curriculum designers.** Map courses against the six capabilities so that perception, reasoning and context awareness are not assumed from the presence of an AI label.
- **Administrators.** Preserve [[human-in-the-loop-ai|human oversight]] and require documentation of how vendor claims about adaptation and [[personalized-learning|personalization]] are realized in teaching.
- **Researchers.** Operationalize the framework and test whether coordinating capabilities adds educational value beyond individual AI functions.

## Limitations
- The search was limited to two databases, two subject areas, a defined period and English-language publications, and may have missed relevant work under other terms.
- Publication bias may overrepresent successful applications, and heterogeneity across objectives, contexts and evaluation methods limits comparison.
- Coding and the platform assessment were conducted by a single author, with some information drawn from vendor documentation or vendor-affiliated studies.
- Co-occurrence patterns describe the reviewed corpus and do not demonstrate [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] effectiveness; primary-document coverage was thin for some applications.
- Cognitive education remains an emerging, not yet clearly defined concept, and the proposed framework has not been empirically validated.
