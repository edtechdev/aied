> **Synthesis:** A cross-sectional survey of 472 [[teacher-education]] students at one Romanian university separated general attitudes toward AI, two content-defined indexes of reported [[generative-ai|generative AI]] use, and perceived cognitive experiences. Positive AI attitudes were associated with both a strategic-use index and a shortcut-use index, while reverse-scored negative attitudes did not meet the focal false discovery rate criterion. Neither use index was clearly associated with perceived AI-related extraneous load. In an explicitly exploratory model, strategic use was associated with perceived improvement in understanding, whereas shortcut use was inconclusive. The authors stress that the use indexes are exploratory observed scores, engagement was not measured, and the cross-sectional, non-preregistered design supports no causal conclusions.

## Key Findings
1. **A single university sample.** All 472 responses came from initial teacher-education programs at one Romanian university, with no calculated response rate.
2. **Four regression models were specified.** The [[primary-education|primary]] specification was documented after earlier data examination but before the final analyses; the study was not preregistered.
3. **Positive attitudes accompanied both use patterns.** Favorable views were associated with strategic and shortcut reported use, not only with the strategic item set.
4. **Lower negativity was less informative.** The reverse-scored negative attitude index did not meet the focal multiplicity criterion in either use model.
5. **Perceived explanation difficulty was not clearly related to use.** Neither use index showed a clear association with perceived AI-related extraneous load.
6. **Only strategic use tracked perceived benefit.** In the exploratory model, strategic use was positively associated with perceived learning enhancement while shortcut use was inconclusive.
7. **The measures carry explicit qualifications.** Several indexes showed structural or local-fit problems, and perceived enhancement is not independently assessed learning.

## What the survey measured

Responses were collected between 9 February and 27 July 2026. The sample included 398 women (84.3%), 73 men (15.5%) and one person who preferred not to answer (0.2%); 307 respondents (65.0%) were affiliated with the Faculty of [[social-sciences-education|Social Sciences]] and Humanities. Eight respondents (1.7%) selected "never" for all 12 measured use activities. Year of study, prior AI-use experience and digital literacy were not separately assessed.

Seven observed scores were formed as unweighted item means. Positive AI Attitudes used 12 items from a General Attitudes towards Artificial Intelligence Scale adaptation (α = 0.830), and Negative AI Attitudes used eight items (α = 0.779), reverse-scored for regression so that higher values meant lower negativity. Strategic GenAI Use averaged six items about adapting teaching ideas, revising exercises, seeking feedback, clarifying concepts and planning learning (α = 0.872); Shortcut GenAI Use averaged six items about minimally revised material, answers without understanding and substitution for reading or analysis (α = 0.882). Both described frequency over the preceding four weeks on a 1 to 5 scale. Three scales based on Leppink-style items covered Perceived Content Complexity, Perceived AI-Related Extraneous Load and Perceived Learning Enhancement on recorded 1–10 responses. The written instructions specified endpoints of 0 ("not at all true") and 10 ("completely true"), but 0 was not selectable.

## What the models showed

All models used heteroscedasticity-consistent inference and family-specific Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment, with focal decisions at q < 0.05. In the model of strategic use, positive attitudes were associated with a 0.767-point higher score on its 1–5 scale (95% CI [0.641, 0.892], β = 0.518, q < 0.001), with R² = 0.293. For shortcut use, the corresponding estimate was 0.342 points (95% CI [0.206, 0.479], β = 0.251, q < 0.001), with R² = 0.060. Reverse-scored negativity was imprecise in both models (q = 0.098 and q = 0.112). The study did not formally test whether the two attitude coefficients differed.

The perceived extraneous load model accounted for 9.2% of observed variation (R² = 0.092). Strategic use had an estimated difference of −0.076 points (95% CI [−0.375, 0.223], β = −0.033, q = 0.617) and shortcut use of 0.180 points (95% CI [−0.078, 0.439], β = 0.072, q = 0.341). Both intervals included zero and allowed associations in either direction.

In the exploratory perceived enhancement model, a one-point higher strategic score was associated with a 0.520-point higher score on the recorded 1–10 scale (95% CI [0.288, 0.752], β = 0.199, q < 0.001), while the shortcut estimate was 0.074 (95% CI [−0.153, 0.302], β = 0.026, q = 0.522). The full model accounted for 49.4% of in-sample variation, which is not the unique contribution of strategic use. Perceived Content Complexity had the largest standardized coefficient in that model (β = 0.466), a descriptive comparison only. Excluding the concept-clarification item from the strategic index reduced the strategic coefficient from B = 0.520 to B = 0.378, with R² falling from 0.494 to 0.485.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Do not infer reflective use from favorable attitudes alone; the two attitude dimensions did not track reported practices in the same way.
- **Instructors.** Ask students to identify which suggestions they checked, revised or rejected, since the reported-use indexes cannot show observed decisions.
- **Assessment designers.** Pair self-reports of benefit with independently assessed work; perceived improvement is not evidence of [[learning-gains|learning gains]].
- **Researchers.** Validate the use indexes in independent samples before treating strategic and shortcut use as distinct constructs.

## Limitations
- Cross-sectional data from one university with convenience recruitment cannot establish temporal order or causal effects.
- The sample was 84.3% women, and the 236/236 exploratory–holdout splits reused the same survey after prior data examination rather than an independent validation sample.
- The two-factor confirmatory models had inadequate fit for both attitudes (scaled CFI = 0.865, RMSEA = 0.085) and use items (scaled CFI = 0.942, RMSEA = 0.142); the indexes remain exploratory.
- Perceived Content Complexity and Extraneous Load were supported only with reservations, and Learning Enhancement showed overlapping item content and local dependence.
- All variables came from one retrospective [[self-report-measures|self-report]] questionnaire, leaving common-method, recall and social-desirability biases possible; class clustering could not be assessed.
- The written scale endpoints conflicted with the recorded response range, and there was no separate measure of overall GenAI exposure, practicum participation, year of study or digital literacy.
