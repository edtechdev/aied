> **Synthesis:** Utegaliyeva and colleagues (2026) tested an AI-enhanced communicative-situational model with 280 undergraduates preparing to be foreign language teachers. In a quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test design, students were assigned at class level to an intervention group (n = 140) or a control group receiving traditional instruction (n = 140). Across a 12-week intervention, ChatGPT-5 generated cultural scenarios, simulated interlocutors and reflective prompts inside a four-stage sequence of cognitive, informational, pragmatic and communicative activities, under instructor supervision. Linguocultural competence was scored with a calibrated rubric covering cultural interpretation, communicative adaptation and professional decision-making. Both groups improved, but the intervention group gained substantially more on every dimension. The authors credit the integrated pedagogy rather than AI alone and recommend critical comparison of generated content with authentic sources.

## Key Findings
1. **Both groups improved; the intervention group improved more.** Gains appeared across all three competence dimensions, with larger changes where students worked through AI-mediated intercultural scenarios, cases and reflection.
2. **The largest descriptive gain was cognitive-conceptual competence.** Its mean rose clearly in the intervention group, while the control group changed only modestly.
3. **Group differences emerged once time was accounted for.** Interaction analyses favored the intervention group on every dimension, even though overall average scores did not differ when time was ignored.
4. **The groups started at equivalent levels.** Pre-test comparisons found no significant differences between them on any subcompetency, supporting the comparison.
5. **The competence dimensions were strongly related at post-test.** Students stronger on cultural interpretation also tended to communicate and act more effectively in multicultural settings.
6. **The design, not AI in isolation, is credited.** ChatGPT-5 served as a mediator inside instructor-guided activities; the authors warn that outcomes depend on the whole pedagogical package.

## What the intervention and measure looked like
Participants were 280 third- and fourth-year undergraduates in the Foreign Language Teacher Education program at Makhambet Utemisov West Kazakhstan University, aged 20–23. Existing classes were allocated to the experimental group (n = 140) or the control group (n = 140) to avoid disrupting the schedule, and baseline equivalence was checked through pre-test comparisons. The 12-week intervention ran inside the courses Intercultural Communication and Foreign Language Teaching Methodology. Instructors attended a preparatory workshop covering the protocol, prompt templates and assessment procedures, and students worked individually or in small groups under continuous supervision. Each AI-supported activity followed a common sequence of scenario presentation, AI-assisted interaction, collaborative discussion, critical evaluation of generated responses and instructor-guided reflection.

The four stages used distinct communicative-situational technologies. The cognitive stage drew on AI-generated interpretations of cultural concepts; the informational stage on comparison with authentic sources; the pragmatic stage on [[case-based-learning|case-based learning]], Scenario-Based Learning and Scenario Remix; and the communicative stage on Simulation Jam, Innovation Circle and role play with AI interlocutors. The control group received reading comprehension, translation, vocabulary and grammar practice, textbook-based discussions and teacher-centered explanations, with no AI-supported simulations or structured intercultural problem-solving.

Linguocultural competence was assessed with a [[rubrics|rubric-based instrument]] adapted from established intercultural frameworks. Three subcompetencies, each with three indicators, were scored on a five-level scale: "Beginning (1); Developing (2); Satisfactory (3); Proficient (4); Advanced (5)". Two independent raters scored structurally equivalent pre-test and post-test tasks after a calibration session, and internal consistency was reported as "Cronbach's alpha values of .897 at the pre-test stage and .874 at the post-test stage". Data were analyzed with paired t-tests, mixed-design ANOVA and Pearson correlations.

## What the results show
Pre-test comparisons found no significant differences for Cognitive-Conceptual Competence (t(278) = −1.312, p = .191), Pragmatic-Communicative Competence (t(278) = −0.477, p = .634) or Professional Cross-Cultural Competence (t(278) = −0.029, p = .977). In the experimental group, Cognitive-Conceptual Competence rose from 3.01 (0.58) to 3.24 (0.51), a change of +0.23, while the control group moved from 3.10 (0.55) to 3.18 (0.54), a change of +0.08. Pragmatic-Communicative Competence changed by +0.18 in the experimental group and +0.06 in the control group; Professional Cross-Cultural Competence changed by +0.19 and +0.08. Cohen's d values in the experimental group "ranged from 0.81 to 1.06", compared with "0.38 to 0.56" in the control group.

The mixed-design ANOVA produced significant Time × Group interaction effects on all three dimensions: Cognitive-Conceptual Competence, F(1,278) = 40.72, p < .001, partial eta squared = .128; Pragmatic-Communicative Competence, F(1,278) = 24.86, p < .001, partial eta squared = .082; and Professional Cross-Cultural Competence, F(1,278) = 19.41, p < .001, partial eta squared = .065. The main effect of Group was not significant when time was not taken into account. Levene's test flagged a pre-test variance violation for Professional Cross-Cultural Competence, F(1,278) = 5.72, p = .017; the authors treated the ANOVA as robust given the equal group sizes.

At post-test the three dimensions were positively correlated, with the strongest relationship between Cognitive-Conceptual and Pragmatic-Communicative Competence (r = .786, p < .001), followed by Pragmatic-Communicative and Professional Cross-Cultural Competence (r = .721, p < .001) and Cognitive-Conceptual and Professional Cross-Cultural Competence (r = .630, p < .001). The paper summarizes these as "r = .630–.786, p < .001". The authors explicitly decline to attribute the gains to AI alone, pointing to the combined effects of scenario work, collaboration and [[reflection|structured reflection]].

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Use AI to generate diverse intercultural scenarios and simulated interlocutors, but require students to compare the output with authentic sources and discuss its limits before treating it as evidence.
- **Instructors.** Keep each activity in the published sequence of scenario presentation, AI-assisted interaction, collaborative discussion, critical evaluation and instructor-guided reflection.
- **Assessment designers.** Score cultural interpretation, communicative adaptation and professional decision-making separately with a calibrated rubric, using structurally equivalent pre- and post-test tasks whose content differs.
- **Curriculum designers.** Embed AI-supported case analysis, role play and reflective journals across the four stages so cultural knowledge is applied in professional communication rather than only transmitted.
- **Researchers.** Replicate at other institutions, with other generative systems and delayed follow-up, since this study covered one semester at one university with ChatGPT-5.

## Limitations
- The study was conducted at a single university with pre-service language teachers from one educational context in Kazakhstan; transfer to other universities, systems or cultural settings is uncertain.
- The intervention lasted one academic semester, so long-term sustainability and transfer to authentic professional practice were not examined.
- Linguocultural competence was assessed through rubric-scored performance tasks; the authors call for validation with behavioral observations, portfolios, interviews or classroom-based assessments.
- ChatGPT-5 was the only AI system used, so findings may not generalize to systems with different interaction quality or pedagogical affordances.
- The intervention bundled AI with case-based instruction, collaborative problem-solving, role play and reflection, so the design cannot isolate AI's independent contribution.
- Levene's test detected a pre-test variance violation for Professional Cross-Cultural Competence (F(1,278) = 5.72, p = .017); robustness relied on equal group sizes.
