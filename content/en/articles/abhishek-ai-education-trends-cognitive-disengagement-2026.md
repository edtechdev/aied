> **Synthesis:** Abhishek and Martis mapped 3,861 Scopus-indexed journal articles on [[ai-education|artificial intelligence in education]] published between 2015 and 2026, using performance analysis and science mapping to trace growth, geographic concentration and the place of engagement and cognitive disengagement in the field. Output rose steeply after 2023, and [[student-engagement|student engagement]] rather than artificial intelligence itself emerged as the main bridge between technology and pedagogy. The paper carefully distinguishes two claims: constructs next to disengagement, such as motivation and attention, appear in 61–73% of the annual corpus, yet disengagement named as an independent construct accounts for only 2.2% of the disengagement sub-corpus. The authors read this as awareness of a concern without focused study of it, and propose treating engagement and disengagement as related but separate dimensions. The mapping cannot tell educators whether AI use actually reduces attention or deep processing; it describes the literature's structure and emphases.

## Key Findings
1. **The corpus is large but deliberately focused.** The search returned more records than the final set, and the engagement or disengagement proximity terms narrowed the material to a focused subset rather than an exhaustive census of AI in education.
2. **Growth accelerated sharply after 2023.** Publication counts rose over the study period, with the authors attributing the steep rise to [[generative-ai|generative AI]] and ChatGPT rather than to AI in general.
3. **Engagement sits at the centre of the network.** Student engagement functions as the main bridging construct across keyword clusters, while generative AI and ChatGPT also show strong intermediary roles.
4. **Disengagement is rarely studied directly.** Engagement, motivation and attention dominate the disengagement sub-corpus, while explicit disengagement appears in only a small share of it.
5. **[[critical-thinking|Critical thinking]] is less prominent than engagement.** Keyword counts for critical thinking are far below those for student engagement and artificial intelligence, which the authors treat as a gap for pedagogy and research.
6. **Research output is geographically concentrated.** China and the Asia-Pacific region contribute heavily, although the authors also describe international collaboration rather than isolation.
7. **The field is growing faster than it is consolidating.** Total citations rose in recent years even as mean citations per publication fell, which the authors interpret as a growth-before-consolidation phase.

## What the mapping showed

The Scopus search combined artificial intelligence terms with education terms and with proximity terms for engagement, disengagement, cognitive load, motivation and attention. It was limited to journal articles, English-language publications and five subject areas, and covered publications from 2015 through 2026, with PUBYEAR after 2014 and before 2027. The initial search returned more than 9,000 records; subject-area, source-type and language filters produced a final dataset of 3,861 publications. The authors note that the 2026 count is likely depressed by partial-year data. Analysis combined annual publication and citation trends, keyword co-occurrence, author, journal, country and institution analyses, Bradford's and Lotka's laws, and a dedicated disengagement trend analysis, using Bibliometrix and VOSviewer.

Annual publications moved from 41 in 2015 to 103 in 2020, then from 157 in 2021 to 320 in 2023, followed by 749 in 2024 and 1,600 in 2025; the 2026 count was 420. The authors tie the post-2023 jump to the public release of ChatGPT in November 2022 and argue that the field has shifted from studying AI as an instructional tool toward studying it as a cognitive interlocutor. Keyword clustering produced four streams: a 22-keyword technical foundation, a 34-keyword conceptual core, a small learner-motivation cluster and a small [[virtual-and-augmented-reality|virtual and augmented reality]] cluster. Student engagement had a betweenness centrality of 210.39, ahead of generative AI at 183.81 and ChatGPT at 114.8; artificial intelligence had a frequency of 3,797 and student engagement a frequency of 2,026, compared with 239 occurrences of critical thinking. Mean citations peaked at 60.19 in 2016, total citations reached 15,673 in 2024, and mean citations fell to 4.96 in 2025 and 0.37 in 2026.

## The disengagement distinction

The disengagement sub-corpus contained 2,692 papers. Within it, student engagement accounted for 48.5%, motivation for 29.1%, attention for 13.4%, cognitive load for 5.8% and explicit disengagement for 2.2%; boredom, low engagement and learning fatigue were marginal. The paper insists that this does not contradict the 61–73% annual share of disengagement-adjacent literature: many papers touch related constructs without making disengagement their object of study. In the thematic map, cognitive disengagement is absent from the motor and basic theme quadrants. The authors interpret this as a structural gap and call for a dual-continuum framework in which engagement and disengagement are independent but interrelated. The geographic analysis adds China at 42.1% and the USA at 32.4% of publications, with The Chinese University of Hong Kong at 54 publications and The University of Hong Kong at 53. China is also the setting the authors ask readers to consider when asking whose [[pedagogy|pedagogical]] assumptions shape the field's framing.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Design AI-assisted activities that require students to evaluate, question or extend generated output instead of accepting it.
- **Instructors.** Build [[metacognition|metacognitive]] checkpoints in which learners reflect on whether they are processing material deeply or handing cognitive work to the system.
- **Curriculum designers.** Treat [[automation-bias|automation bias]] and [[cognitive-offloading|overreliance]] as explicit [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] content, since critical thinking is far less prominent than engagement in the mapped literature.
- **Researchers.** Study cognitive disengagement, boredom and learning fatigue directly and longitudinally, since the existing corpus approaches them mainly through adjacent positive constructs.
- **Policymakers.** Adapt AI education frameworks to local contexts rather than importing models shaped mainly by high-resource, high-performance systems.

## Limitations
- The analysis covers only Scopus-indexed, English-language journal articles in selected subject areas, and the engagement-focused search terms deliberately produce a subset of the wider field.
- The 2026 publication count is based on partial-year data and is not comparable with full years.
- The study is bibliometric: it maps publications and keywords and cannot establish that AI use causes disengagement or shallower learning.
- The corpus is geographically concentrated, which limits claims about educational systems and cultural contexts outside Asia and the USA.
- The authors call for empirical, [[longitudinal-research|longitudinal]] and cross-cultural work, and for a comparison against an unfiltered AI-in-education corpus to test representativeness.
