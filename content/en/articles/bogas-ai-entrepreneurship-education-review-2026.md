> **Synthesis:** Bogas and colleagues reviewed how digital tools and artificial intelligence are being integrated into entrepreneurship education. Following PRISMA 2020, they searched Scopus, Web of Science and SciELO Brasil for studies published between 2019 and 2025, screened 792 records and retained 52 for synthesis. The included studies describe generative AI, ChatGPT, digital platforms, blockchain, immersive environments and simulations used for personalized and experiential learning, competency development and [[student-engagement|student engagement]]. The central conclusion is conditional: benefits depend on pedagogical design, teacher preparation, infrastructure and governance, not on tool adoption alone. One examined study found better learning performance and AI awareness among ChatGPT users but stronger [[critical-thinking|critical thinking]] among non-users. The authors organize these patterns into an integrated model and call for longitudinal and experimental research.

## Key Findings
1. **A broad, recent set of studies was screened.** PRISMA 2020 guided identification, screening and inclusion across three databases, with a 2019–2025 publication window.
2. **Technology frameworks dominate the themes.** The largest knowledge categories concern educational technology frameworks and innovation in higher education, with categories overlapping rather than partitioning the studies.
3. **Reported benefits are conditional.** Personalization, creativity, self-efficacy and digital entrepreneurship intentions appear when tools are embedded in coherent teaching and institutional arrangements.
4. **Critical thinking is a tension, not a settled gain.** One examined study found that ChatGPT users improved on performance and AI awareness while non-users showed stronger critical thinking.
5. **Institutional conditions recur across the literature.** Leadership, infrastructure, educator development and governance are described as conditions for meaningful integration.
6. **The authors propose an integrated model.** It links leadership and governance, institutional capacity, technologies and AI, pedagogical practices and student outcomes.
7. **The evidence base is mostly cross-sectional.** Longitudinal, experimental and cross-cultural studies remain scarce, and geographical coverage is uneven.

## How the review was conducted

The search ran on November 27, 2025, in Scopus, Web of Science and SciELO Brasil. The two international databases supplied 49 and 672 records respectively, while SciELO Brasil added 71, for 792 records in total. SciELO Brasil was included to capture Brazilian and Latin American research less visible in international indexes. After consolidation, 10 potentially duplicate records were checked manually against titles, authors and publication years; five were confirmed as duplicates and removed, leaving 787 studies for screening.

Titles and abstracts were screened in Rayyan through an independent, double-blind process. A total of 661 studies were excluded at this stage, leaving 126 publications for full-text assessment. The reviewers recorded 59 disagreements and resolved them by consensus; 17 studies classified as uncertain were reassessed after full-text examination. At the eligibility stage, 74 studies were excluded, mostly because they lacked a direct relationship with entrepreneurship education, and 52 were included in the qualitative synthesis.

The synthesis combined thematic analysis with bibliometric mapping in VOSviewer version 1.6.20. Keyword harmonization started from 306 keywords and produced 223 standardized terms; a minimum threshold of one occurrence was adopted so that low-frequency concepts were retained. The authors kept keyword clusters generated by co-occurrence analysis distinct from the thematic knowledge categories derived from close reading. Geographically, the 52 studies came mainly from Asia (n = 21), followed by South America (n = 13) and Europe (n = 10), with North America (n = 3), Central America and the Caribbean (n = 2), Africa (n = 2) and one multicontinental study (n = 1). The authors caution that this pattern reflects database coverage and search strategy rather than the overall volume of research in each region.

## What the included studies found

Five knowledge categories were identified, with studies allowed to count toward more than one category. Educational Technology Frameworks was the largest (n = 37), followed by Educational Innovation in Higher Education (n = 27), Entrepreneurial Education & Employability (n = 19), Social Entrepreneurship Ecosystems (n = 16) and Entrepreneurial Innovation Culture (n = 13). Smaller categories included Digital Knowledge Management (n = 8), Sustainability in Higher Education (n = 2) and several categories represented by one study each. The sums exceed the number of included studies because the categories overlap.

Technologies ranged from ChatGPT and other generative AI tools to digital learning platforms, web applications, virtual and augmented reality, the metaverse, blockchain and digital simulations. Reported associations included personalized learning, experiential and project-based work, collaboration, competency development, entrepreneurial self-efficacy and intentions toward digital entrepreneurship. The review also documents implementation costs, privacy and security concerns, algorithmic bias, inequitable access and the need for educator preparation. In the study by Ji et al. (2025), ChatGPT use came with gains in learning performance and AI awareness but non-users showed stronger critical thinking, which the review treats as a warning against uniform benefit claims.

The proposed Integrated Conceptual Model has five components: Institutional Leadership and Governance, Institutional Capacity, Digital Technologies and AI, Innovative Pedagogical Practices, and Student Outcomes. Ethics, equity, privacy and data protection, AI literacy and human oversight cut across all five, and a feedback loop runs from outcomes back to policy revision, educator development and resource allocation. Pedagogy is positioned as the mediating element between technological potential and learning outcomes.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Embed AI tools in projects, simulations and challenges that require students to formulate alternatives and make decisions, rather than introducing tools as stand-alone aids.
- **Instructors.** Preserve tasks that require independent analysis and evaluation, so that generative AI supports inquiry instead of replacing the reasoning the activity is meant to develop.
- **Institutions.** Align leadership, infrastructure, educator development and governance before scaling AI tools, and set explicit policies for privacy, transparency and equitable access.
- **Researchers.** Prioritize longitudinal, experimental and cross-cultural designs that can distinguish initial perceptions from sustained effects on competencies and critical thinking.

## Limitations
- The search was limited to three databases and to studies published between 2019 and 2025, which may have excluded relevant work indexed elsewhere or published outside that window.
- The included studies differ in design, educational context, technologies and outcome measures, limiting direct comparability and preventing pooled estimates.
- Quantitative and cross-sectional, perception-based studies predominate, so the review cannot establish causality or long-term effects on entrepreneurial competencies.
- Geographical representation is uneven, with Asian studies comprising the largest group; the authors state that future reviews could expand database coverage and add languages.
- The authors note that a more explicit assessment of methodological quality would strengthen interpretation of the available evidence.
