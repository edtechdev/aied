> **Synthesis:** Kondrashev and colleagues reviewed 32 peer-reviewed studies published after 2023 on communication between educators and AI agents in [[higher-ed|higher education]] classrooms. They organize the evidence around a complementarity–substitution axis defined by relational function rather than by tool. Educator communication behaviors still predict student outcomes; students judge AI agents with the same human categories but rate them lower on goodwill; and handing relational functions to a machine carries costs for loneliness and belonging. The review offers propositions and a research agenda rather than a new pooled estimate, and most of the underlying evidence comes from Chinese and English-as-a-foreign-language settings.

## Key Findings
1. **Educator communication still predicts outcomes.** In AI-mediated classrooms, immediacy, rapport, clarity and credibility continue to be associated with engagement and other student outcomes.
2. **AI agents are judged by human categories.** Students apply the same communication constructs to AI agents, but attribute lower goodwill even when competence is rated as equal.
3. **Warmth cues partly close the gap.** Politeness, verbal immediacy and self-disclosure raise perceived caring and trust, though they do not remove what the authors take to be the source of the gap.
4. **Substitution carries relational costs.** Where AI takes over relational rather than informational functions, the reviewed studies report declining belonging and rising loneliness.
5. **The axis is about functions, not tools.** The same system may be used complementarily for one function and substitutively for another, so the position of a use depends on what it does for the student.
6. **The propositions remain to be tested.** Four propositions and a research agenda are advanced; the review reports no new effect estimate of its own.

## What the review included

The search was run on 1 July 2026 across Scopus and Web of Science-based indexes (Consensus), the Wiley full-text archive (Scholar Gateway), OpenAlex and ERIC. Twelve counted queries combined communication terms (immediacy, rapport, clarity, credibility) with AI-context terms. The databases returned 195 records; after 34 duplicates were removed, 161 were screened at title and abstract level, 44 were assessed for eligibility and 13 were excluded. One further report came from citation searching, leaving 32 included studies. Inclusion required a peer-reviewed journal publication from 2023 or later, a higher education context or one transferable to it, and measurement or conceptualization of at least one communication construct.

Full texts were examined in depth for nine studies; the remaining candidates were assessed on record, abstract and DOI-verified metadata, and none was excluded for reasons of access. Screening was done by a single reviewer with AI assistance, so no agreement coefficient can be reported. All records were verified against Crossref and OpenAlex and checked for retractions. Of the 32 studies, 24 reported [[primary-education|primary]] data; eleven sampled mainland China, nine of those in EFL settings, one sampled Hong Kong and one did not state a country. The three strands were derived inductively through thematic clustering.

The review distinguishes instructional communication constructs from relational functions. Immediacy describes behaviors that reduce distance, rapport describes mutual trust and harmony, clarity describes understandable presentation, and credibility combines competence and attributed good intentions. Relational functions are what those behaviors accomplish: emotional support, social companionship and belongingness confirmation. Immediacy and rapport carry relational functions, clarity works at the rhetorical level, and credibility sits between them because its goodwill dimension rests on attributed benevolent intent. Scales used to measure these constructs were developed with human educators as the reference point, which is the source of the measurement problem the review raises.

## Three strands on the axis

On the educator side, a recent [[meta-analysis-systematic-review|meta-analysis]] pooled 156 effect sizes from 30 studies published between 2023 and 2025 and found a moderate-to-large association between educator interpersonal behaviors and student learning, r = 0.443, 95% CI (0.388, 0.495). More than 95% of the studies in that pool came from Eastern cultures and used cross-sectional designs, which limits generalization; neither type of support nor dimension of learning moderated the association. A second-order synthesis of 26 meta-analyses covering some 2.64 million students reported that educator–student relationships are consistently associated with achievement, motivation, engagement and [[well-being|wellbeing]], though that evidence largely predates [[generative-ai|generative AI]]. In an AI-mediated survey of 513 university students, instructor immediacy (β = 0.30, p < 0.001) and rapport (β = 0.35, p < 0.001) jointly explained 53% of the variance in engagement. Both also predicted resilience, with rapport the stronger predictor.

On the machine side, two experiments with 155 and 222 students found that human and AI evaluators were judged equally competent but that the AI was attributed lower goodwill, a gap that narrowed when verbal warmth cues were added. Polite feedback raised the perceived helpfulness of a [[conversational-ai|chatbot]], although the bot still lagged behind a human instructor. An AI instructor using self-disclosure produced emotional engagement comparable to a human instructor and higher learning interest in a study of 211 undergraduates, but knowledge acquisition did not differ. Over one year, [[community-of-inquiry|social presence]] supported autonomous learning among companionship-oriented users while interaction frequency was linked to negative outcomes among knowledge-seeking users. The review concludes that AI approximates emotional support and social companionship through designed cues, but reports no evidence of belongingness confirmation.

On substitution, a study of 387 university students found chatbot use for information retrieval positively related to performance, with the total effect turning negative once AI-specific social support, wellbeing, loneliness and sense of belonging entered the model. A survey of 819 higher education students found that concerns about lost human connection rose alongside chatbot experience and satisfaction. In a pre-registered comparison of 296 first-semester students over two weeks, messaging a randomly matched human peer reduced loneliness more than messaging a supportive chatbot; a twelve-month study of more than 2,000 adults suggested a bidirectional relation between weakening social connection and chatbot use. The authors caution that short-term chatbot effects may reflect novelty and that the negative trend is concentrated where the machine takes over relational functions.

The integrative framework places these strands on one axis. Proposition one holds that educator communication behaviors still predict outcomes directly. Proposition two holds that [[agentic-ai|AI agent]] cues contribute to the same outcomes, positively in complementary use and negatively through relational costs in substitutive use. Proposition three holds that educator behaviors moderate the student–AI pathway, inferred from a single mediation study and never directly tested. Proposition four holds that all three presuppose comparable measurement across human and AI references. The authors argue that the axis differs from earlier models because the relational function, not the task or the amount of mediated contact, is the unit of analysis.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Keep relational functions—emotional support, companionship and belonging confirmation—within human interaction, and reserve AI for complementary work such as preparation and review.
- **Institutions.** Include interpersonal communication competencies alongside [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] in [[educational-development|professional development]], since educators are expected to provide connection, cognitive guidance and clarity at once.
- **Instructional designers.** Build immediacy and politeness into agents where those cues extend communication, but do not treat a warmer agent as a substitute for human contact.
- **Researchers.** Test the propositions with cross-lagged panel designs over at least three waves, experimentally manipulate educator immediacy and rapport, and check measurement invariance across human and AI references.
- **Researchers.** Add samples from under-researched contexts and fields beyond [[english-education|English as a foreign language]] before generalizing the axis.

## Limitations

- Screening was performed by one reviewer with AI assistance, so no agreement coefficient could be reported, and most included studies came from Chinese and EFL settings with overlapping journals and author networks.
- The meta-analytic association is carried by the same concentrated pool, so it cannot be generalized beyond that context.
- Most findings on the human side come from cross-sectional self-reports, which cannot support causal interpretation; a reverse direction in which engagement fosters perceived immediacy cannot be ruled out.
- The measurement invariance of scales built for human educators has not been tested with AI references, so human and machine scores cannot be compared directly.
- The complementary pole is less established than the substitution costs; several of its routes are propositions awaiting examination rather than findings.
