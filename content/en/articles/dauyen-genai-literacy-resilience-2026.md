> **Synthesis:** A cross-sectional survey of 306 students at five universities in Xinjiang reports that generative AI literacy is positively associated with online learning resilience, and that self-efficacy carries part of that association. Literacy was measured as four dimensions — cognitive awareness, usage ability, evaluation ability and ethical awareness — and resilience as learning persistence, reflection and help-seeking, and emotional reaction. In the authors' structural model, literacy predicted resilience and self-efficacy, and self-efficacy predicted resilience; bootstrap tests supported an indirect pathway alongside a direct one. The paper proposes a literacy–efficacy–resilience pathway and argues that access to tools alone is insufficient. Because the sample was one of convenience, the data were cross-sectional and all constructs came from the same respondents at one time point, the results describe associations rather than causal effects and should not be generalized to Chinese university students as a whole.

## Key Findings
1. **Literacy and resilience moved together.** Students reporting stronger generative AI literacy also reported stronger online learning resilience in the fitted structural model.
2. **Self-efficacy was a partial mediator.** The bootstrap test supported both an indirect route through self-efficacy and a direct route from literacy to resilience.
3. **All four literacy dimensions contributed similarly.** Cognitive awareness, usage ability, evaluation ability and ethical awareness had comparable loadings on the higher-order literacy construct.
4. **All three resilience dimensions contributed similarly.** Learning persistence, reflection and help-seeking, and emotional reaction had comparable loadings on the higher-order resilience construct.
5. **Usage ability showed the largest dimension-level indirect effects.** Its indirect paths through self-efficacy to each resilience dimension were larger than the corresponding paths for the other literacy dimensions.
6. **The measures passed reliability and validity checks.** Internal consistency, convergent validity and discriminant validity were reported before the structural paths were interpreted.
7. **The design cannot establish direction.** All variables were measured at a single time point with one source of data, so the direction of the proposed pathway remains an assumption.

## What the study measured

Students were recruited from five colleges and universities in the Xinjiang region of China using non-probability, specifically convenience, sampling. The sample of 306 included 249 students aged 18–22 (81.4%), 142 men (46.4%) and 164 women (53.6%); 144 were in humanities (47.1%) and 162 in science and engineering (52.9%). All respondents were university students.

The questionnaire used three multi-item scales, all scored on a five-point Likert scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). [[ai-literacy]] was measured with 12 items adapted from a four-dimensional framework: cognitive awareness, usage ability, evaluation ability and ethical awareness, with selected items reverse-coded. Online learning resilience used nine items adapted from an academic-resilience framework and contextualized to online learning, covering learning persistence, reflection and help-seeking, and emotional reaction. [[self-efficacy]] was measured with five positively worded items drawn from established learning-motivation scales.

The authors used covariance-based structural equation modeling in AMOS 24.0 with a two-stage approach: first assessing the reflective measurement model, then estimating a second-order structure in which first-order dimensions served as indicators of the higher-order constructs. Average variance extracted exceeded 0.5 and variance inflation factors were below 3. To check common method bias, a Harman single-factor test extracted eight factors with the first accounting for 31.013% of total variance, below the stated 40% threshold; a single-factor confirmatory model also failed to meet recommended fit indices.

## What the structural results report

The first-order loadings on the higher-order literacy construct ranged from 0.585 to 0.653, and those on resilience ranged from 0.584 to 0.632. In the reported path table, literacy predicted resilience (β = 0.666, p < 0.001) and self-efficacy (β = 0.759, p < 0.001), while self-efficacy predicted resilience (β = 0.418, p = 0.004). The authors describe the literacy-to-efficacy path as the strongest, followed by the direct path to resilience and then the efficacy-to-resilience path.

Mediation was tested with 5,000 bootstrap iterations and 95% confidence intervals. The indirect effect of literacy on resilience through self-efficacy was 0.292 with an interval of [0.220, 0.370], which excludes zero. The mediation section separately reports a direct effect of 0.344 with an interval of [0.235, 0.452]. At the dimension level, all of the reported indirect paths were significant: cognitive awareness produced effects of 0.153, 0.137 and 0.179 through self-efficacy to learning persistence, reflection and help-seeking, and emotional reaction; usage ability produced 0.259, 0.233 and 0.249; evaluation ability produced 0.150, 0.141 and 0.149; and ethical awareness produced 0.138, 0.129 and 0.160.

The discussion interprets this as evidence that [[online-teaching-and-learning]] resilience depends on learners' beliefs about their own capability, not simply on access to technology. The paper connects the pattern to self-determination theory and social cognitive theory and frames generative AI literacy as a dynamic competency rather than a set of operational skills. Its recommendations are addressed to institutions, governments and platform providers: digital literacy courses, simulated human–AI collaboration, timely feedback and peer support, evaluation frameworks for AI literacy, and professional and psychological support alongside technical infrastructure.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Treat instruction in generative AI as more than tool operation: include evaluation of outputs and ethical awareness alongside awareness and usage skills.
- **Instructors.** Build opportunities for students to experience success with AI-supported tasks, since self-efficacy is the mechanism the model proposes between literacy and resilience.
- **Institutions.** Pair AI-literacy provision with academic support that includes timely feedback, peer support and personalized guidance.
- **Institutions.** Design support for online learners around persistence, reflection, help-seeking and emotion regulation, since those were the measured facets of resilience.
- **Researchers.** Use probability sampling, larger and cross-regional samples, and longitudinal or experimental designs before treating the proposed pathway as causal.

## Limitations
- The sample of 306 came from convenience sampling at five institutions in one region, whose cultural, economic and policy context may differ from other parts of China; the authors caution against generalizing to all Chinese university students.
- The cross-sectional, single-time-point design limits causal inference about the direction between literacy, self-efficacy and resilience.
- All constructs were self-reported by the same respondents, and the authors relied on procedural and statistical remedies for common method bias rather than independent measurement.
- The scales were adapted for this context and may not capture literacy requirements as generative AI technology develops; students with different backgrounds may interpret items differently.
- The direct effect attributed to literacy is reported with two different values in different parts of the results, so the size of that path should be read with care.
