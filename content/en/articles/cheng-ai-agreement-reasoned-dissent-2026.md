> **Synthesis:** Cheng and Liu (2026) separate two messages a student can receive: that [[generative-ai|generative AI]] agrees with an answer, and that a teacher welcomes reasoned disagreement with it. Surveying 386 undergraduates at one Chinese vocational and technical [[higher-ed|university]] after an [[language-learning|English]] learning task, they found that both perceptions were positively associated with state intellectual humility, and that humility was positively associated with reported [[critical-thinking|counterevidence seeking]]. Neither direct path to searching was statistically significant, so the modeled associations ran through humility, and bootstrap intervals supported both indirect paths with no clear difference between them. The authors distinguish perceived agreement from [[ai-sycophancy|sycophancy]]: agreement can reassure a student while leaving room to reconsider, whereas sycophancy is excessive or unwarranted. Treating [[metacognition|acknowledging uncertainty]] and searching for disconfirming evidence as separate achievements, the study measures them separately, with scale means clustered near the midpoint from 3.21 for perceived agreement to 3.61 for humility, and the main model left most of the variance in both constructs unexplained.

*Cross-sectional survey of 386 undergraduates who completed an AI-assisted English task, analyzed with confirmatory factor analysis and structural equation modeling; the paths are conditional associations, not causal mediation.*

## Key Findings

- **Both support messages tracked humility.** Perceived AI agreement and teacher support for reasoned dissent were each positively associated with state intellectual humility when the other was in the model.
- **Humility tracked searching.** Students who acknowledged possible error and incomplete understanding reported more counterevidence seeking.
- **The direct paths were not statistically significant.** Perceived AI agreement showed a small, imprecise direct association with searching, and teacher support's direct association did not meet the 0.05 criterion.
- **The indirect paths were supported.** Both indirect associations through humility had bootstrap intervals that excluded zero, with no clear difference between them.
- **Willingness and behavior are distinct.** Humility and searching formed separate factors and correlated moderately, so acknowledging uncertainty is not the same as acting on it.
- **Agreement is not sycophancy.** Feeling that AI supports one's view differs from excessive or unwarranted agreement, which experimental work links to uncorrected [[misconceptions]].

## Two kinds of support, one model

The study brings two perceived sources of support into a single framework: support for an answer and support for questioning an answer. Perceived AI agreement records whether a student feels the AI endorsed their initial judgment and reasons during the task. Teacher support for reasoned dissent records whether the student expects the teacher to welcome objections backed by reasons or evidence. The two need not convey the same message, and the model estimates each while accounting for the other. Participants were recruited from 33 teaching classes taught by 17 English teachers, and the questionnaire contained 13 items: three each for perceived agreement, teacher support, and humility, and four for searching. The task itself mattered: students read English material on whether online learning could replace face-to-face instruction, formed an initial judgment, discussed it with a generative AI tool, and could consult further material. [[conversational-ai|Generative AI]] makes that exchange possible, but the authors treat the student's reading of the exchange, not the tool's actual correctness, as the predictor.

## What the paths do and do not establish

In the main model, perceived agreement and teacher support were both positively associated with humility, and humility was positively associated with reported searching. The direct paths told a different story: the agreement-to-searching estimate was small and imprecise, and the teacher-support direct estimate did not reach the 0.05 criterion. Bootstrap intervals supported both indirect products, and the difference between them was not clearly nonzero. Scale means were 3.21 for perceived agreement, 3.56 for teacher support, 3.61 for humility, and 3.54 for searching. The authors therefore report conditional associations rather than causal mediation. They also show that the indirect estimates depend on how shared variation is modeled: under latent method construct specifications, both indirect intervals included zero, which makes the main indirect findings tentative. What survives is the separation of constructs — humility and searching formed distinct factors — and the finding that both support messages relate to humility.

## Why humility and searching are separate measures

The paper measures state intellectual humility as recognition of possible error, incomplete understanding, and willingness to reconsider within one task. Counterevidence seeking is measured as reported action: looking for differing explanations, evidence that the judgment might be wrong, examples the view cannot explain, and material that challenges it. The distinction is deliberate. A student may recognize uncertainty without searching, or search and keep the original view. The observed correlation between the two was moderate, and the authors cite evidence that interventions intended to increase humility produced no detectable change in search behavior. An independent pilot of 150 students and eight cognitive interviews shaped the items before the main survey. They also note that [[critical-thinking|critical thinking]] dispositions, epistemological beliefs, and [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] may explain variation the model leaves open — the main model accounted for 15.6% of the variance in humility and 30.4% in searching. The practical point is that willingness and behavior need separate items and separate designs.

## What this means for practice

- **[[teacher-role|Instructors]].** Ask students to state an initial judgment, identify evidence that would challenge it, and consult a credible alternative source before explaining why they kept or revised the answer.
- **Instructional designers.** Build tasks that connect an initial judgment to contrary evidence, since willingness to reconsider and reported searching were only moderately associated.
- **Assessment designers.** Reward sound reasoning, including a well-supported decision to keep an answer, rather than treating any change as the goal.
- **Researchers.** Measure willingness to reconsider and information seeking separately, and link questionnaire scores to recorded searches and final arguments.

## Limitations

- The cross-sectional design does not establish temporal order; prior humility may shape perceptions of agreement, and recalled searching may shape reported humility.
- The scales were brief and newly developed, with no established humility measure co-administered, and the three humility items loaded unequally (0.680, 0.900, 0.683).
- The 30 retained task records could not be matched to [[self-report-measures|questionnaires]], so the association could not be compared across levels of initial argument quality.
- The convenience sample came from one university and was mainly first-year (81.9%) and female (65.0%) students, with class and teacher identifiers unavailable.
- Common method checks were mixed: the single-factor model fitted poorly, but latent method construct models made both indirect intervals include zero.
