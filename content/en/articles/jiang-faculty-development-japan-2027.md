> **Synthesis:** Jiang, Wang and Taguchi (2026) read two Japanese staff-development tracks side by side: in-service faculty development (FD) and pre-faculty development (pre-FD), a term coined in Japanese higher education for the pre-service teaching preparation of doctoral students and postdoctoral researchers. The asymmetry between them is the story. FD became a legal mandate for graduate courses in 2007 and undergraduate courses in 2008; pre-FD has been a best-effort obligation since 2019. Using the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning as an analytical lens, the review traces how a mandated organizational shell outgrew the professional core that would give it substance. Coverage rose and then slipped: dedicated FD centers reached 87.5% of universities by 2016 and stood at 79% in 2023. Participation rose from 12.9% of universities reporting that every full-time faculty member took part in 2015 to 32.7% in 2023, while the collegial formats that carry developmental substance did not spread. Pre-FD is thinner still, with 2.5% of universities offering sustained, hands-on training. The authors argue that both tracks hit the same ceiling — the absence of a continuing professional role in [[educational-development|academic development]] — and propose joining them through shared centers, teaching-assistant reform and a SoTL frame.

*No primary data: the authors conducted no fieldwork, interviews or program evaluation of their own; every prevalence figure below is drawn from MEXT national surveys and published Japanese-language studies that the review re-reads.*

## Key Findings

- **Two tracks, one policy logic, very different legal force.** Both tracks advanced through what the authors call policy-driven and policy-induced reform — MEXT deliberation councils and statutory revision on one side, competitive funding on the other. In-service FD moved from a best-effort obligation in 1999 to a binding legal mandate in 2007 for graduate courses and 2008 for undergraduate courses, while pre-FD, recognized in policy discourse alongside the 2008 mandate, received only a best-effort obligation in 2019. The mandate obliged institutions rather than individuals, which is why coverage spread fast and unevenly.
- **Participation rose while the formats that give FD substance did not.** The share of universities in which every full-time faculty member participated in FD rose from 12.9% in 2015 to 22.8% in 2019 and 32.7% in 2023. Over the shorter 2016–2019 window where comparable items exist, workshops on active learning fell from 42% to 33% of universities, mutual classroom observation from 57% to 53%, and mutual peer review of teaching was essentially unchanged at 20% and 19%. The authors flag the mismatched windows and note that only the active-learning decline is large enough to read as substantive; the claim they defend is the weaker one — rising participation was not accompanied by a spread of collegial formats.
- **Dedicated provision expanded, peaked, and did not consolidate.** The share of universities with a dedicated FD center reached 87.5% by 2016, fell in each of the next three years to 76.9% in 2019, and stood at 623 of 786 responding universities (79%) in 2023 — below both the peak and the 2015 level of 84.4%. The composition of provision points the same way: lectures and symposia were the most common format in both 2019 and 2023 (62% and 57% of universities), individual teaching consultation was among the rarest (6% and 7%), outside specialists were brought in as needed by 59% and 57%, and a part-time specialist appointed inside the institution remained the rarest arrangement at 3% in both years.
- **The binding constraint is the absence of a continuing professional role.** The authors argue that the problem is not a shortage of American-style faculty developers but the absence of any stable professional role in academic development. Rotation, concurrent duties and fixed-term appointments erode the continuity and institutional memory that the collegial model itself depends on. The three tensions they identify reinforce one another: research-centered career incentives make low-cost, auditable participation rational; weak professional staffing limits the supply of deeper developmental alternatives; and countable participation lets universities demonstrate compliance with the mandate. Formalistic compliance, on this account, is an institutional response, not a failure of individual commitment.
- **Pre-FD is thin, concentrated, and divided by discipline.** Among 813 universities, 230 (28.3%) nominally mention pre-FD terminology, while only 20 (2.5%) offer sustainable, hands-on instructional training. Provision clusters in a few elite research universities and in the humanities and social sciences; participation is markedly lower in STEM, where laboratory demands compete for time and supervisors may regard teaching development as irrelevant. The authors trace this to the legacy of the *kōza* (chair) system, whose legal provisions were removed in 2007 but whose organizational logic persists in research-intensive laboratories, so access to pre-FD depends on the disposition of an individual supervisor rather than on institutional policy.
- **The teaching-assistant system drifted away from its developmental purpose.** By 2014 over 60% of Japanese universities had a TA system, modeled on the American one but constrained by 1995 ministerial guidelines that confine TAs to auxiliary support in laboratory, practical and seminar classes on part-time contracts limited to three-quarters of full-time hours. The result is a financial-aid mechanism dominated by clerical labor, with orientations that amount to administrative briefings. Later teaching-fellow schemes create real teaching opportunities, but job descriptions, compensation and instructional responsibilities remain poorly coordinated.
- **Reading the tracks together reveals shared carriers and a common ceiling.** The flagship pre-FD programs — Kyoto University's pre-FD program, the University of Tokyo's Future Faculty Program, Tohoku's PFFP — are designed and delivered by the same centers that serve in-service faculty, so staff expertise, formats and SoTL orientation circulate between tracks. TA and TF positions place graduate students inside undergraduate teaching under faculty supervision, making them a structural bridge; pre-FD completers who later enter faculty posts seed the next generation of mutual development. The 2021 JST-SPRING program, which ties doctoral stipends to transferable-skill provision, could fund pre-FD as a core skill, but its evaluation metrics reward research outputs and placements almost exclusively. Generative AI is the one domain where both tracks face an identical learning demand at the same moment, which makes it the natural site for cross-track [[collaborative-learning|mutual development]].

## How the study was done

The article is a narrative, document-based conceptual review, combining policy analysis, document analysis and conceptual comparison. It is interpretive rather than systematic: the authors claim no systematic-review or pre-registered protocol, and they describe source selection as purposive and iterative, seeded from their own sustained engagement with Japanese FD scholarship and extended through structured searches of CiNii Research and J-STAGE, with policy and institutional documents retrieved from MEXT, e-Gov, JST, university websites and council archives. The principal period covered is January 1990 to June 2026. English-language literature was selected for comparative reference rather than assembled through systematic searching, and the authors say explicitly that it does not constitute a comparative review.

Three kinds of evidence are kept distinct throughout: national-prevalence evidence from government statistics and national surveys, including Kadomatsu et al.'s survey of 813 universities; institutional-practice evidence from program-level studies and evaluations, such as Naganuma's quantitative evaluation and Oyama et al.'s qualitative study of SoTL-based pre-FD; and conceptual interpretation from theoretical and comparative scholarship. Synthesis proceeded in three stages: policy documents ordered chronologically, empirical studies grouped by organizational form and reported constraint, then cross-track comparison. SoTL serves as both analytical lens and interpretive concept — criteria for judging whether development is substantive, held as a diagnostic rather than a remedy because its own standing is contested in research-intensive systems.

## What this means for practice

- **Policymakers.** Legal force without aligned incentives produces auditable compliance. Japan's sequence — best-effort obligation, then binding mandate, then a best-effort obligation for the pre-service track — raised institutional coverage far faster than it raised teaching quality, so pair any new requirement with changes to how teaching counts in hiring, promotion and workload.
- **Administrators.** Stop reading participation rates as a quality indicator. Full participation reached 32.7% of universities in 2023 while active-learning workshops, peer observation and peer review were flat or falling; report the format mix alongside the headcount, because the measurement regime otherwise rewards the cheapest countable activity.
- **Faculty developers.** Staff the coordinating role before importing a specialist model. The audit trail here is not a missing American-style developer but missing continuity: rotation and fixed-term contracts break the relationships that peer-led development depends on, and a network can distribute scarce capacity but cannot do without someone who convenes it over time.
- **Graduate deans and doctoral schools.** Treat the TA and TF system as a joint in-service and pre-FD project rather than a graduate-education housekeeping matter. Supervised teaching with structured mentoring develops the graduate student and gives the supervising faculty member a genuine SoTL activity; leaving TAs as clerical support loses both.
- **Institutions planning AI-era development.** Generative AI is unusually well suited to [[collaborative-learning|mutual study]], because faculty and doctoral students are beginners at it together. Build AI capability into shared centers and cross-career networks rather than as one-off tool training, and extend the existing regional consortia downward into graduate education.

## Limitations

- **No primary data and no systematic protocol.** The review analyzes published policy documents and surveys; source selection is purposive and interpretive, so the account is an argued reading of the Japanese record rather than a measured one, and a different selection could support a different emphasis.
- **The international comparison is selective by design.** English-language literature supplies conceptual reference points only; the authors disclaim a comparative review, so the claim that Japan's configuration also describes professional schools elsewhere is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **The national series are not directly comparable.** Participation figures cover 2015–2023 while collegial-format items cover only 2016–2019, the 2023 center figure is 623 of 786 responding institutions rather than a fixed population, and centers may have been reorganized into broader units rather than closed — all caveats the authors raise themselves.
- **The SoTL lens is contested.** SoTL has proved difficult to establish in research-intensive universities, and the authors use it diagnostically rather than as a settled standard; readers who reject its normative framing will read the integration proposal differently.
- **The integration proposal is a proposition, not a tested design.** Shared centers, TA reform and SoTL framing are derived from the analysis, and the authors set out the studies that would test them — whether pre-FD participation predicts later collegial engagement, whether redesigned TA arrangements change supervising faculty's teaching — as future work.
- **Much of the evidence base is Japanese-language scholarship.** The article's contribution is partly one of access, and its claims rest on the authors' reading of a literature most readers cannot check.
