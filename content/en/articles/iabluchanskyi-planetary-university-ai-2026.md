> **Synthesis:** This Perspective proposes a "planetary university" of lifelong education and science: a federation of autonomous universities, research centers, professional societies, clinical institutions, industry partners and local communities connected by shared standards and remote access to research infrastructure. The authors argue that international networked science already exists and that AI, agentic systems and robotic laboratories extend its reach rather than inventing it. The proposal combines four functional layers, governance by subsidiarity, four funding components, recognition of distributed academic work, and a notion of cognitive sovereignty defined through four capacities. Implementation is framed as three overlapping stages with provisional indicators. The article reports no empirical data; it is a conceptual proposal offered for discussion, evaluation and collaborative refinement.

## Key Findings
1. **A federation, not a replacement.** The planetary university is presented as a framework through which local institutions participate in a shared global academic environment.
2. **Four functions organize the system.** Local nodes, shared standards, infrastructure access and [[developmental-psychology|human development]] are described as interconnected functions rather than an administrative hierarchy.
3. **Subsidiarity governs decisions.** Global coordination is reserved for interoperability, mutual recognition, shared infrastructure and [[academic-integrity|academic integrity]], while local nodes retain authority over priorities, recruitment and resources.
4. **Funding is layered.** Stable base funding, trajectory-linked funding, international research grants and shared infrastructure funds are proposed together.
5. **Academic work must be recognized.** Cross-border mentorship, program development and infrastructure contributions should count in professional evaluation, including participation after formal retirement.
6. **Cognitive sovereignty is the human safeguard.** It means identifying problems, evaluating AI outputs, deciding what to delegate and accepting responsibility for decisions.
7. **Implementation is adaptive.** Three stages proceed by demonstrated outcomes, with indicators that the authors explicitly label preliminary proposals.

## The proposal in concrete terms

The authors trace the idea to Einstein's emphasis on independent thinking and to Asimov's 1988 vision of [[lifelong-learning|continuous learning]] enabled by information technology. The "university throughout life" is not endless formal enrollment but recurring participation as student, researcher, practitioner, mentor or expert. Existing precedents include Prigogine's research center established in 1967 and robotic telescope networks that let researchers submit remote observing requests. In 2026 the European Universities initiative encompassed 65 alliances involving more than 570 [[higher-ed|higher education]] institutions, and ECIU University develops interuniversity learning modules.

The proposed federation adds research centers, societies, clinical institutions, companies, observatories, [[libraries]], computing facilities and communities. Its four layers are the local layer of autonomous organizations, a shared standards layer for interoperability and quality assurance, an infrastructure layer for remote access to equipment and AI tools, and a human development layer for personalized trajectories and mentorship. Governance follows subsidiarity, with academic freedom, transparency and accountable decision-making treated as conditions. Funding combines stable support for local nodes with payments distributed according to teaching and mentorship contributions, grants organized around scientific questions, and shared infrastructure funds. The authors illustrate this with a student enrolled locally, completing a module through an international network and remotely using another institution's equipment, while warning that this is an illustrative model requiring economic and legal work.

## Agentic research and cognitive sovereignty

Two developments illustrate changing research organization. In 2026 Ghareeb and colleagues described Robin, a [[agentic-ai|multi-agent]] system for literature analysis, hypothesis generation and data interpretation in which laboratory experiments were still performed by humans. Wu and colleagues reported AutoDNA, which combined agentic AI with robotic laboratory infrastructure, coordinating more than 20 types of laboratory instruments and over 11,000 operations. The authors stress that these examples do not establish general reliability for autonomous scientific research; verification, reproducibility, data provenance and responsibility remain essential.

The paper's educational caution rests on mixed findings it cites. Kestin and colleagues found a designed [[intelligent-tutoring|AI tutoring]] intervention outperformed [[active-learning|active learning]], whereas Liu and colleagues reported a randomized university study with 2,379 students and 30 instructors in which an integrated AI tutor did not improve performance and could displace other learning activity. A [[meta-analysis-systematic-review|systematic review]] by Crompton and Burke covering 72 studies highlighted [[process-oriented-assessment|process-oriented assessment]] and dialogic evaluation. Cognitive sovereignty is then described through four capacities: identifying meaningful problems, critically evaluating AI outputs, maintaining fundamental competencies while deciding what to delegate, and accepting responsibility. The authors connect this to [[cognitive-offloading|cognitive offloading]] and to autonomy and competence in [[self-determination-theory|self-determination theory]], while insisting that sovereignty is not refusal to use AI.

## What this means for practice
- **Institutions.** Start with existing partners and shared educational activities, then evaluate funding and mutual recognition mechanisms before consolidating arrangements.
- **Institutions.** Keep authority over scientific priorities, recruitment and resource allocation at the local node while adopting shared standards for interoperability and recognition.
- **Instructors.** Treat AI-supported work as a setting for developing evaluation and responsibility, not simply as a way to reduce effort.
- **Researchers.** Test the proposed indicators, compare federated governance models and examine whether networked arrangements actually improve outcomes.

## Limitations
- The article explicitly reports no original empirical evaluation of the planetary university model.
- The examples demonstrate feasibility of components but not that integration into a federation would improve educational or scientific outcomes.
- Differences among disciplines, legal systems, economic conditions and cultural traditions are not investigated.
- The performance indicators are preliminary proposals whose validity, feasibility and thresholds have not been established.
- Cognitive sovereignty and its four capacities are described as requiring further development and validation.
