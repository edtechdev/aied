> **Synthesis:** A cross-sectional survey of 570 university English-as-a-foreign-language students at one university in eastern China examined how perceived classroom social climate and self-reported [[ai-literacy]] relate to classroom engagement. Structural equation modeling found direct positive associations from both resources to engagement and indirect associations through resilience and [[self-efficacy]]. A fuzzy-set analysis identified four combinations associated with high engagement and four associated with low engagement, with supportive climate or AI literacy pairing with psychological resources in the recurring high-engagement patterns. No single condition was necessary for either outcome. [[self-determination-theory|Self-Determination Theory]] served as an interpretive lens rather than a measured model. The authors caution that the indirect associations were sensitive to a common method factor specification and that the cross-sectional, single-site self-report design cannot establish temporal order.

## Key Findings
1. **One university, one questionnaire.** All 570 participants were EFL students at a single university in eastern China who reported using AI tools for English learning in the preceding semester.
2. **Two complementary analyses were combined.** Net statistical associations were estimated with structural equation modeling, while fuzzy-set analysis identified combinations of conditions linked to high and low engagement.
3. **Climate and AI literacy both tracked engagement.** Each resource was directly associated with engagement and also associated with resilience and self-efficacy.
4. **Psychological resources carried part of the association.** Resilience and self-efficacy were each linked to engagement, and the indirect routes through them were statistically supported in the [[primary-education|primary]] model.
5. **Nothing was necessary on its own.** No individual condition reached the necessity threshold for either high or low engagement.
6. **Different combinations accompanied high and low engagement.** Supportive climate paired with a psychological resource, or AI literacy paired with a psychological resource, appeared in the high-engagement solutions; low engagement clustered around several absent resources.
7. **The indirect paths were fragile.** A method-factor sensitivity analysis reduced one path and removed statistical support for some of the indirect associations.

## What the study measured

Participants were recruited by convenience and snowball sampling in October 2025. The retained sample included 119 males (20.88%) and 451 females (79.12%), with 300 freshmen (52.63%), 160 sophomores (28.07%), 54 juniors (9.47%), 50 seniors (8.77%) and 6 [[graduate-education|graduate]] students (1.05%). Medicine or agriculture accounted for 283 students (49.65%) and [[social-sciences-education|social sciences]] for 181 (31.75%); science and engineering contributed 85 (14.91%), economics and management 20 (3.51%), and arts or physical education 1 (0.18%). All 46 analyzed items were complete. Detailed AI-use patterns, class identifiers and instructor identifiers were not recorded.

The Chinese questionnaire contained five blocks. Classroom social climate used nine items covering [[teacher-role|teacher]] emotional support, teacher [[student-support-and-success|academic support]] and mutual respect (α = 0.967). The 12-item AI literacy scale covered awareness, usage, evaluation and [[ethics]] (α = 0.952). Resilience was measured with six items adapted from the Brief Resilience Scale (α = 0.937), self-efficacy with a 10-item adaptation (α = 0.973), and engagement with nine items spanning behavioral, emotional and cognitive engagement (α = 0.958). The authors report high internal consistency across constructs, ranging from α = 0.937 to α = 0.973.

## What the two analyses showed

The five-factor measurement model fit the data: χ²(979) = 1706.58, χ²/df = 1.743, CFI = 0.973, RMSEA = 0.036, 90% CI [0.033, 0.039], and SRMR = 0.030. In the structural model, classroom social climate (β = 0.284) and AI literacy (β = 0.158) were positively associated with engagement; both were also associated with resilience and self-efficacy, which in turn were associated with engagement (β = 0.160 and β = 0.286), all p < 0.001. The model explained 44.6% of the variance in engagement. Total indirect associations were B = 0.159 for classroom social climate and B = 0.192 for AI literacy, representing 29.4% and 47.6% of their respective total associations.

The common latent method factor analysis told a more qualified story. The alternative model fit as χ²(979) = 1745.52, CFI = 0.971 and RMSEA = 0.037. The AI literacy–resilience path fell from β = 0.260 to β = 0.135 (p = 0.079), explained variance in engagement fell from 44.6% to 30.3%, and seven of the eight structural paths remained supported. Delta method tests supported three indirect associations, whereas none of the bias-corrected bootstrap intervals excluded zero.

In the fuzzy-set analysis, no condition reached the 0.90 necessity threshold; the highest necessity consistency was 0.795 for self-efficacy with high engagement and 0.783 for relatively low perceived climate with low engagement. Four high-engagement configurations had overall consistency 0.808 and coverage 0.863; four low-engagement configurations had consistency 0.866 and coverage 0.727. Relatively low perceived climate appeared in three of the four low-engagement configurations. Across sensitivity checks, three high-engagement configurations recurred, while the low-engagement pattern combining low climate, low AI literacy and low self-efficacy was the most stable.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Treat supportive relationships as part of AI-assisted language teaching, using structured peer exchange and shared norms for respectful interaction during AI tasks.
- **Instructors.** Build tasks that let learners evaluate and revise AI-generated text, so students practice judging suggestions rather than only accepting them.
- **Instructors.** Use observation and student reflection to see which resources a learner is missing, since different combinations accompanied engagement in the configurational analysis.
- **Researchers.** Collect detailed AI-use records and class identifiers, and test indirect pathways with designs that separate self-report method variance from the constructs of interest.

## Limitations
- The cross-sectional design and concurrent [[self-report-measures|self-report measures]] cannot establish temporal ordering or causality.
- Convenience and snowball sampling at one university, with uneven demographic distributions, limits generalizability.
- AI-tool experience was defined broadly, without frequency, specific activities or instructional arrangements, and class identifiers were unavailable.
- The indirect associations were sensitive to the common latent method factor specification and to the inferential procedure used.
- The self-efficacy response format was modified, there was no independent pilot validation or measurement invariance testing, and basic psychological need satisfaction was not measured.
