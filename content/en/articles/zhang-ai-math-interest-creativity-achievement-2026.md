> **Synthesis:** Zhang and Leong (2026) asked whether students' [[motivation|interest]] in [[personalized-learning|AI-supported mathematics learning]] reaches achievement through mathematical [[creativity|creativity]] rather than directly. With 355 Grade 11 students from eight classes in four schools in Jiangsu Province, they measured three interest dimensions by questionnaire, creativity with five open-ended tasks scored for fluency, flexibility and originality, and achievement with a citywide examination. All three interest dimensions were associated with creativity, creativity was associated with achievement, and every direct path from interest to achievement was non-significant, leaving an indirect-only pattern in the specified model. The model explained 62.6% of the variance in creativity and 76.4% in achievement. The authors are unusually explicit about the limit: AI use itself was never measured, so the "AI-supported" in the construct is the students' framing of their mathematics learning rather than documented engagement with a tool.

## Key Findings

1. **Interest dimensions were moderately associated with creativity.** Enjoyment, value and [[self-efficacy|self-efficacy]] each carried a positive standardized estimate between 0.27 and 0.33, with bootstrap intervals excluding zero.
2. **Creativity was the strongest associate of achievement in the model.** Its standardized estimate of 0.594 was the largest in the structural model.
3. **Direct paths from interest to achievement did not reach significance.** Enjoyment, value and self-efficacy reached achievement only through the mediator, an indirect-only pattern.
4. **Indirect estimates were positive for all three dimensions.** The unstandardized indirect effects were 2.560 for enjoyment, 3.359 for value and 2.380 for self-efficacy, none spanning zero.
5. **The model explained most of the outcome variance.** It accounted for 62.6% of the variance in creativity and 76.4% in achievement, high enough to suggest the constructs are tightly coupled and to invite concern about overlap.
6. **Value had the largest point estimate but not a demonstrably stronger association.** The authors state explicitly that the estimates do not establish a statistically stronger path for value than for the other two dimensions.
## What "AI-supported" means in this study

The instrument asks students about their interest in mathematics learning that uses AI support — adaptive resources, immediate feedback, personalized practice, interactive hints. No log, task trace or tool record was collected, so the study measures motivational beliefs about a learning context rather than the effect of a system. That distinction matters for how the result should travel: the finding is that interest and creativity are associated in a sample of students who report AI-supported mathematics learning, not that AI support produced either. The authors put the point in their own limitations, which is the right place for it, and it makes the paper a useful prior for designing a study that does measure engagement.

## Reading a mediation with no direct path

An indirect-only pattern is easy to over-read. Because the design is cross-sectional, the ordering of interest, creativity and achievement is a modeling choice, and an equally defensible model with achievement predicting later interest would fit the same covariances. Two further cautions come from the measurement: enjoyment and self-efficacy sit close together (HTMT = 0.852), and the creativity tasks produce sample-relative originality scores whose comparability across schools is unestablished. What survives is a reasonable claim that in this sample, students who report more interest also produce more creative mathematical work, and that the creative work is what tracks the examination score.

## What this means for practice

- **Instructors.** Treat creative mathematical production — generating approaches, flexibly switching representations, original solutions — as a route to achievement worth assessing in its own right, not as an enrichment activity for after the exam content.
- **Instructors.** Build interest through value and enjoyment as well as confidence. All three dimensions carried similar weight here, so a course that only raises self-efficacy is not using the whole lever.
- **Curriculum designers.** If AI support is being introduced to raise achievement, plan for the creative work in between. The direct path from interest to score was not significant in this model.
- **Researchers.** Measure actual AI engagement before attributing an effect to AI support; this study could not, and says so.

## Limitations

- The sample is 355 Grade 11 students from eight classes in four schools in one city, and school and class clustering was not adjusted, which may affect confidence intervals.
- No demographic or prior-achievement covariates were included, leaving possible confounding of the motivation–achievement associations.
- AI engagement and AI literacy were not measured, so the findings cannot separate associations specific to AI-supported learning from pre-existing mathematics motivation or competence.
- Enjoyment and self-efficacy were only empirically separable (HTMT = 0.852), and two items showed unresolved local dependence.
- The creativity tasks' suitability and the comparability of sample-relative originality scores require further validation, and the sensitivity analysis indicated limited power for weaker indirect associations.
