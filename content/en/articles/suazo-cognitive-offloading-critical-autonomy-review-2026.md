> **Synthesis:** This PRISMA 2020 systematic review synthesized 26 empirical studies of generative AI in higher education, identified through searches of Scopus, the Web of Science Core Collection and databases accessed through EBSCOhost up to May 2, 2026. The included studies run from 2024 to 2026 and are dominated by qualitative work and by studies conducted in the Global South. Across the studies, structured and verification-oriented AI use is associated with feedback literacy, conceptual clarification, reflective engagement and self-regulated learning, while convenience-driven or weakly supervised use is associated with [[cognitive-offloading|cognitive offloading]], automation bias, superficial processing and weaker evaluative judgment. The authors conclude that pedagogy, [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] and assessment design mediate the consequences more than the technology itself, and offer HEEP-HE as a provisional four-domain framework rather than a validated intervention.

## Key Findings
1. **The included evidence is recent.** Of the 26 studies, three were published in 2024, fourteen in 2025 and nine in 2026; no eligible study appeared in 2022 or 2023.
2. **Global South settings predominate.** Eighteen studies came from institutions in the Global South, seven from the Global North and one from a mixed North–South collaboration.
3. **Designs were heterogeneous.** The review included 11 qualitative studies, seven mixed-methods studies, four analytical cross-sectional studies, two quasi-experimental studies and two randomized controlled trials.
4. **AI appears as both scaffold and risk.** The same literature reports gains in explanation, feedback, accessibility and reflective inquiry, and warns about overreliance, hallucinated output, reduced source checking and erosion of agency.
5. **Conditions of use matter more than access.** Guided, reflective and verification-oriented use was associated with higher-order learning, while unstructured or assessment-driven use could displace the work needed for epistemic development.
6. **Integrity is reframed as epistemic integrity.** The debate shifts from detecting AI use to questions of disclosure, verification, authorship and who remains accountable for final claims.
7. **The integrative model is explicitly provisional.** HEEP-HE organizes four interdependent domains and is described as a review-level conceptual extrapolation requiring independent evaluation.

## How the review was assembled

The protocol was registered in PROSPERO as CRD420261390452 on May 2, 2026, before screening. Searches on that date returned 7,215 records: 2,674 from Scopus, 2,182 from the Web of Science Core Collection and 2,359 from databases reached through EBSCOhost. After 3,122 duplicates were removed, 4,093 unique records underwent a criteria-based automated eligibility assessment in Rayyan; 3,962 were marked ineligible and 131 proceeded to blinded independent title and abstract screening. Reviewers excluded 101 at that stage, sought full texts for 30, failed to retrieve one, and assessed 29 full texts. Three were excluded, leaving 26 studies. Overall agreement across screening was κ = 0.85.

Eligibility was framed with SPIDER rather than PICOS because no single intervention or pooled effect was being tested. Original peer-reviewed empirical studies in higher education were eligible if they addressed an epistemic dimension of generative AI use; preprints, gray literature, reviews and conceptual articles were excluded. Two reviewers extracted data and applied Joanna Briggs Institute appraisal tools by study design. Affirmative appraisal responses ranged from 80.0% to 100.0%, reported descriptively rather than as validated risk-of-bias categories. Meta-analysis was ruled out because populations, settings, questions and outcomes were too heterogeneous; synthesis combined descriptive mapping, three-stage thematic synthesis and framework synthesis under the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis reporting guideline.

## From themes to the HEEP-HE model

Thematic synthesis produced six descriptive themes: reconfiguration of epistemic authority; fragility of knowledge validation and source evaluation; transformation of academic integrity into epistemic integrity; cognitive offloading and erosion of learner agency; generative AI as a scaffold for higher-order thinking; and institutional transition from prohibition to governed integration. A further five analytical themes reframed the findings as a move from information access to epistemic responsibility, from misconduct to hybrid authorship governance, from digital competence to epistemic AI literacy, from cognitive assistance to calibrated delegation, and from individual use to institutional epistemic governance.

Framework synthesis mapped these patterns onto four domains, which were then refined. The epistemological domain added synthetic plausibility, epistemic responsibility and hybrid authorship; the pedagogical domain added epistemic AI literacy, a pedagogy of verification and process-based assessment; the sociotechnical domain added calibrated delegation, distributed agency and human oversight; and the civic-democratic domain added civic epistemic resilience. The resulting HEEP-HE model treats responsible use as the alignment of epistemological validation, pedagogical scaffolding, sociotechnical calibration and civic-democratic resilience. The review stresses that the model was not proposed or empirically tested by any included study and should not be read as a causal theory or measurement instrument. Throughout, the authors separate direct empirical findings from evidence-informed interpretations and keep normative recommendations in the discussion.

## What this means for practice
- **Instructors.** Ask students to compare AI claims with external evidence, verify citations and explain why an output was accepted or rejected.
- **Instructors.** Make reasoning visible through drafts, reflective records of AI use and oral justification rather than grading only the submitted product.
- **Institutions.** Move beyond prohibition and detection toward governed integration: AI literacy, faculty development, library support and policies that specify acceptable delegation.
- **Researchers.** Use longitudinal and experimental designs with objective tasks for citation verification, evidence discrimination and uncertainty calibration before claiming effects on autonomy.

## Limitations
- The search covered only Scopus, the Web of Science Core Collection and EBSCOhost databases; relevant work indexed elsewhere may have been missed, and gray literature was excluded.
- The search ended on May 2, 2026, and the authors note that the field's publication rate makes early updating necessary.
- Conceptual and outcome heterogeneity prevented statistical pooling and limited direct comparison across studies.
- Much of the underlying evidence is cross-sectional, perception-based or reliant on self-reported AI use, which restricts causal inference and invites recall and social-desirability bias.
- Critical autonomy, cognitive offloading, agency and AI literacy were operationalized inconsistently across studies.
- Thematic and framework synthesis involved interpretive judgment, so HEEP-HE remains a provisional, evidence-informed framework open to alternative readings.
