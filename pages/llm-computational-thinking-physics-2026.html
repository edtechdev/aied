Using LLMs to Detect Growth in Computational Thinking in Introductory Physics

Created: 2026-08-07 | Tags: llmcomputational-thinkingphysics-educationstem-educationassessmenthigher-ed
Savage, Shanker, Michlitsch & Rebello (2026) demonstrate that LLMs can reliably mirror human coding of computational thinking growth in introductory physics — successfully scaling assessment of Data Practices and Computational Problem-Solving Practices, while revealing that Systems Thinking remains a challenge for both human and machine raters.

Context

As computation becomes increasingly central to physics education, scalable methods to assess authentic computational-thinking are critically needed. Written student responses capture nuanced reasoning but are labor-intensive to evaluate manually, especially in large-enrollment courses.

Methodology

The study used a pre/post semester survey design in introductory physics courses. Researchers first established a human-coded baseline grounded in CT literature, identifying significant growth in two practice areas. The same student responses were then evaluated by an LLM, and the results were compared against the human baseline.

Key Findings

  • LLM mirrors human coding: For Data Practices and Computational Problem-Solving Practices, the LLM successfully replicated human evaluations and scaled trend detection across large datasets
  • Systems Thinking remains hard: Both human raters and the LLM struggled to reliably evaluate more complex constructs — revealing the frontier of automated CT assessment
  • Scalable assessment: The approach offers a viable path to evaluating computational thinking at the scale of large-enrollment stem-education courses where manual coding is infeasible
