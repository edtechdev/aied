Pragmatic Users and Skeptical Nonusers: ChatGPT Adoption in Physics Education

Created: 2026-08-08 | Tags: physics-educationstudent-experienceai-literacyhigher-edstem-educationtrust-calibrationqualitative-researchstudent-ai-interactiontechnology-adoptionsurvey
Becker, Bauer, Schrader, Bitzenbauer & Veith (2026) analyze 1,189 survey responses and find two distinct ChatGPT user profiles in physics-education: 70% are Pragmatic Users who scaffold with AI despite knowing its inaccuracies, and 30% are Skeptical Non-Users who avoid it over over-reliance concerns. Both groups make calculated trade-offs — challenging one-size-fits-all AI policies and calling for differentiated pedagogy.

Context

While 61–88% of university students now use ChatGPT, the student-experience of AI in physics is poorly understood — especially in a discipline where Fouad & Bentley (2026) recently documented a 50-point trust-utility gap. This study develops the first data-driven typology of how physics students perceive and use ChatGPT.

Two User Profiles

Pragmatic Users (70%)Skeptical Non-Users (30%)
Use ChatGPT for conceptual clarification, finding starting points, scaffoldingAvoid ChatGPT over concerns about overreliance and hindered independent problem-solving

Key Findings

  • Qualitative content analysis of 1,189 responses + latent class analysis identified exactly two profiles
  • Both groups make calculated risk-utility trade-offs — neither is uncritical or naive
  • Findings challenge institutional one-size-fits-all AI policies (mandatory AI, blanket bans)
  • Recommend differentiated pedagogy: support reflective AI use for Pragmatic Users while validating practices of Skeptical Non-Users
  • Complements trust-calibration literature — domain-specific skepticism, not blanket rejection or adoption
