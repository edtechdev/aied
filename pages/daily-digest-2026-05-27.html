1. Student Misconceptions: Conditionals & Loops Taxonomy

Eckert & Kautz (2026) — arXiv:2605.26966

2. GenAI and Epistemic Marginalization of Disability Knowledges

Tali-Otmani (2026) — arXiv:2605.26769

Argues that generative-ai systems in higher-ed actively marginalize non-hegemonic epistemologies through Anglophone/Western-centric training data. Identifies a double marginalization for disabled learners: underrepresented in training data AND excluded from AI design processes. Proposes researcher-machine hybridization as a path to preserving epistemic plurality.

3. Persistent AI Agents in Academic Research (96-Day Case Study)

Alzahrani (2026) — arXiv:2605.26870

First empirical study of persistently embedded AI agents in a real academic environment: 96 active days, 17 configured agents, 75,671 telemetry records, and 73.95M tokens (82.9% cache reads). Introduces PARE-M measurement framework. Key finding: the economic unit shifts from cost-per-token to cost-per-completed-artifact in persistent agentic environments. 889 failure/correction events recorded — roughly one per 1.5 hours.

→ persistent-ai-agents-academic-research

4. SlidesQAQA: Pedagogical Question Generation from Lecture Slides

Salsman (2026) — arXiv:2605.26428

A Flask-based four-stage llm pipeline for generating pedagogically coherent questions from PDF lecture decks. Innovations: joint text+visual reasoning, deck-level scaffolding (not per-slide isolation), bounded question budgets, and a reconciliation stage that eliminates redundancy. Successfully tested on two technical lecture decks. Live demo available.

5. ⚠️ Recovery: AI Assistance Reduces Persistence (RCT)

Bastani et al. (2026) — arXiv:2604.04721

Causal evidence from N=1,222 participants: brief AI assistance reduces persistence and impairs unassisted performance across math and reading tasks. AI-treated participants spent less time on unassisted tasks, skipped more questions, and scored lower. Previously partially ingested by a stalled run; fully recovered in this scan.
