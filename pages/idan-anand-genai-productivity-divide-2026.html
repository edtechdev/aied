Generative AI and the Productivity Divide: Human-AI Complementarities in Education

Created: 2026-08-07 | Tags: generative-aiai-literacyequityefficacy-studyhigher-edscaffolding
Idan & Anand (2026) conduct an RCT showing GenAI access boosts mean task performance — but gains are concentrated among those with high AI Interaction Competence (AIC), the ability to elicit, filter, and verify model outputs. GPA and prior knowledge don't predict GenAI-augmented performance; AIC does. A conceptual-map scaffolding intervention reduced variance, revealing that structured workflows can mitigate the new AI-driven equity gap.

Context

As GenAI becomes ubiquitous in knowledge work, a critical question emerges: does AI level the playing field or widen it? Prior research on ai-literacy and cognitive-offloading suggests uneven returns, but rigorous causal evidence has been scarce.

Study Design

Participants — analogs of early-career knowledge workers — were randomly assigned to self-study a technical domain using either traditional resources or LLM assistance. The researchers measured task performance, prior knowledge, GPA, and crucially, AI Interaction Competence (AIC): skill in prompting, filtering outputs, and critically verifying AI-generated content.

Key Findings

  • Mean boost, uneven distribution: GenAI significantly increased average task performance, but the top quartile captured most gains while the bottom quartile saw limited or negative returns
  • AIC predicts success, GPA does not: Neither GPA nor prior domain knowledge predicted GenAI-augmented performance — only AIC did, suggesting a new axis of capability inequality
  • Scaffolding mitigates the divide: A conceptual-map scaffolding intervention reduced outcome variance, demonstrating that standardized workflows can narrow the productivity gap
  • Managerial implications: Firms should pair GenAI access with short AIC micro-training and standard operating procedures to capture value consistently

Connections to Wiki

  • Directly extends the equity literature by identifying a new mechanism of AI-driven inequality: interaction skill rather than domain knowledge
  • Complements ai-literacy frameworks by operationalizing the specific competencies that predict effective GenAI use
  • The scaffolding finding connects to metacognition and self-regulated-learning — AIC is fundamentally a metacognitive skill
  • Contrasts with cognitive-offloading-speedup-illusion by showing that some users can benefit from GenAI — the question is who and under what conditions
  • Parallels Lodge & Loble (2026) on the metacognitive equity gap — both papers identify that existing skills determine AI's educational value

Citation

Idan, L., & Anand, B. (2026). Generative AI and the Productivity Divide: Human-AI Complementarities in Education. arXiv:2605.18143.
