A Multi-Agent AI Classroom for Prospective Physics Teachers

Created: 2026-08-07 | Tags: multi-agentphysics-educationteacher-trainingsimulationstem-educationhigher-ed
Tufino (2026) pilots a simulated multi-agent AI classroom where five AI students each enact distinct dual-process theory reasoning hazards — giving prospective physics teachers rare practice in responding to authentic student ideas. Diagnostic scores improved significantly, but the simulation revealed a knowing-doing gap between understanding theory and applying it in real-time responsive teaching.

Context

Responding productively to the reasoning students actually produce — in real time — is among the most difficult and slowest-to-acquire teaching skills, yet prospective teachers get few opportunities to practise it before entering a classroom.

The Simulation

The simulated classroom consists of five AI students, each consistently enacting a distinct reasoning pattern drawn from the dual-process theory (DPT) "hazards" framework from physics education research. Fifteen graduate students in a university teacher-training course worked through an instructional sequence on DPT and questioning strategies, then diagnosed vignettes before and after interacting in pairs with the simulated class.

Key Findings

  • Diagnostic scores improved significantly: Wilcoxon p = 0.014, effect size r = 0.79 (n = 11 paired)
  • Knowing-doing gap: During the simulation, participants asked predominantly uniform guiding questions — DPT vocabulary appeared in only 2 of 71 substantive teacher turns
  • Transfer to written work: Seven of thirteen POST sheets used DPT vocabulary readily — suggesting knowledge exists but isn't yet automated under live interaction pressure
  • Developmental trajectory visible: The simulation makes the responsiveness-to-student-ideas trajectory visible at transcript-level granularity, revealing where each participant stands
