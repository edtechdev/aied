A Systematic Review of Generative AI in Education: Human–AI Interaction Perspective

Created: 2026-08-07 | Tags: systematic-reviewgenerative-aihuman-ai-interactionai-literacyhigher-edefficacy-study
Liang, Yang, Sha, Gašević, Yan & Chen (2026) systematically review 56 empirical GenAI-in-education studies through the AIED-HCD framework, analyzing human–AI interaction modes along dimensions of human-ai-interaction. They find cautious adoption of high-AI-automation modes, but an emerging trend of high human control + high AI automation — calibrated human–AI complementarity rather than replacement.

Context

As generative-ai proliferates in education, dozens of empirical studies have appeared — but literature reviews have rarely analyzed effectiveness from a human–AI interaction lens. This systematic-review fills that gap using the AIED-HCD framework from learning-analytics research.

Framework: AIED-HCD Interaction Modes

Studies were classified along two dimensions: human control (teacher/learner oversight) and AI automation (autonomy of AI actions). Three modes emerged:

  • Low automation + high human control: AI as tool under explicit user direction — most common in current practice
  • Balanced: Shared agency between human and AI
  • High automation + high human control: AI acts autonomously but with strong human oversight — emerging trend

Key Findings

  • 56 empirical studies analyzed; efficacy-study evidence varies by interaction mode
  • Most practice remains conservative — low-AI-automation with teacher/learner in control
  • High-control + high-automation mode emerging as most promising design pattern
  • Sensitivity analysis confirms findings robust across contexts and educational levels
  • Educational contextual factors (domain, learner level) significantly moderate interaction mode effectiveness

Connections to Wiki

  • Directly informs ai-literacy by showing that effective AI use depends on interaction design, not just individual skill
  • Complements Idan & Anand (2026) on the productivity divide — interaction competence matters as much as access
  • The high-control + high-automation pattern mirrors teacher-role findings on AI as amplifier, not replacement
  • Connects to metacognition research through the emphasis on calibrated oversight

Citation

Liang, Z., Yang, K., Sha, L., Gašević, D., Yan, L., & Chen, G. (2026). A systematic review of generative AI in education: Empirical insights from a human–AI interaction perspective. British Journal of Educational Technology. doi:10.1111/bjet.70055.
