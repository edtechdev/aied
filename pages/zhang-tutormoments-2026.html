When Help is Unhelpful: Evaluating AI Tutors for Productive Struggle

Created: 2026-08-08 | Tags: intelligent-tutoringscaffoldingllmllm-evaluationk-12math-educationbenchmarkai-tutoringteacher-roleover-reliance
Zhang, Ross, Patel et al. (2026) introduce TutorMoments — a replay-based framework that evaluates whether LM intelligent-tutoring systems adapt their pedagogical actions to context. Testing frontier models against 462 teacher-annotated math-education transcripts from grades 2-7, they find LMs default to over-reliance-inducing over-scaffolding, rarely pushing for productive struggle — even when students are ready.

Context

AI tutors optimized for helpfulness may harm learning. Research on cognitive-offloading and genai-performance-vs-learning shows that AI assistance can improve short-term performance while reducing long-term learning. The TutorMoments framework directly tests whether frontier LMs navigate the "assistance dilemma" — knowing when to help versus when to let students struggle productively.

The TutorMoments Framework

TutorMoments evaluates AI tutors through situation-conditioned replay:

  1. Expert math-education teachers identify key pedagogical decision points in authentic tutoring transcripts
  2. Each moment is annotated as scaffolding-appropriate (student needs support) or rigor-appropriate (student ready for challenge)
  3. The framework replays these moments to test whether LM tutors select appropriate instructional moves
  4. This enables apples-to-apples comparison: different tutor actions evaluated under identical conditions

The dataset — TutorMoments-Preview — contains 462 transcripts from 198 students (grades 2-7) interacting with 173 human tutors, yielding 1,500+ teacher-annotated key moments.

Key Findings

  • Over-scaffolding default: Minimally prompted frontier LMs over-scaffold — aligning with the "helpful assistant" training objective at the expense of scaffolding that fades appropriately
  • Rigor gap: LMs rarely push for rigor even when students are demonstrably ready — missing opportunities for metacognition and deeper learning
  • Evaluation-aware prompting helps: Prompts that make LMs aware of the evaluation criteria substantially improve adaptability, but cross-model differences remain large
  • Narrow move repertoire: Tutor behavior concentrates into a small set of moves, lacking the strategic diversity of human teacher-role behavior
  • Grounding in theory: Framework operationalizes scaffolding, zone of proximal development, desirable difficulties, and productive struggle as evaluation criteria
