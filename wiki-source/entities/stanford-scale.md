# Stanford SCALE

> Stanford's **Center to Support Excellence in Teaching (CSET)** — more commonly referred to as **SCALE** — operates the **AI Hub for Education**, one of the most influential systematization efforts tracking AI research in K-12 and postsecondary settings.

## Overview

- **Home institution:** Stanford Graduate School of Education
- **Key initiative:** AI Hub for Education (research repository and evidence review)
- **Repository size:** 818 papers as of October 2025 (up from 28 in January 2023)
- **Update cadence:** Monthly additions to the research repository

## AI Hub for Education

The AI Hub curates and reviews papers on AI in education, with a focus on:

1. **Scope:** LLM-era AI/ML tools in educational settings
2. **Exclusions:** Pre-LLM rule-based intelligent tutoring systems (ITS)
3. **Sources:** 87% arXiv preprints
4. **Quality review:** Two-step process—LLM pre-screening followed by human review using *What Works Clearinghouse (2025)* standards
5. **Access:** [scale.stanford.edu/ai-hub](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai-hub) (public)

### Methodological Approach

- **Search terms:** "education" + "AI" or "artificial intelligence"
- **Content:** Focuses on impacts on students and educators; includes both descriptive and causal studies
- **Limitation:** Preprint-heavy repository may overrepresent early, uncorroborated findings

## Major Outputs

### The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A 2026 Review

- **Authors:** Stanford SCALE team
- **Key finding:** Only 20 of 818 papers meet strong causal inference standards
- **Impact:** Widely cited in policy discussions around AI in education procurement and regulation
- **See:** [[ai-k12-evidence-base]] for detailed findings

## Significance

SCALE's work is notable for applying rigorous evidence standards (WWC 2025) to a fast-moving domain dominated by preprints. Their framing of findings through established learning science principles (cognitive load, ZPD, metacognition, desirable difficulties) provides educators and policymakers a conceptual vocabulary for evaluating AI tools.

## Criticisms & Limitations

- ArXiv-heavy source base may miss peer-reviewed negative results
- Search terms limited to English-language "AI in education" papers; may miss domain-specific implementations (e.g., math AI, writing AI) that don't use those keywords
- Rapid field growth means quarterly reviews may already be outdated by publication
