Alex Liu, Min Sun, Lief Esbenshade, Victor Tian, Zachary Zhang, Kevin He (2026). Teacher-Authored Prompts for Configuring Student-AI Dialogue: K-12 Classroom Implementation. arXiv:2604.16738.

This study contributes to learning-at-scale and AI-in-education research by offering an empirical account of student-AI dialogue at classroom scale when dialogues are configured by teachers via prompt authoring. We deployed the Classroom Teaching Aide (TASD) system in 20 K-12 classrooms (39 unique settings, 1,479 student-AI conversations). Teachers authored two prompt layers: a teacher-to-AI setup prompt and a student-facing conversation starter. 71% of student-AI conversations were fully aligned with teacher-defined instructional goals, and fewer than 1% were substantially off-track. However, 38% of conversations under-reached the targeted Depth of Knowledge level, rising to nearly 50% for the highest cognitive demand. Adding explicit finish lines to prompts reduced the DOK gap by 0.22 levels (p < .001), and including 'no direct answers' guardrails reduced AI final-answer rates by 8.5 percentage points. Teacher-authored prompt layers emerge as a high-impact orchestration lever for scalable K-12 AI integration, with persistent gaps between intended and enacted cognitive demand requiring additional tooling and training.
