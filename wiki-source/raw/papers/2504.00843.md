# Benchmarking Large Language Models for Diagnosing Students' Cognitive Skills from Handwritten Math Work

**Authors:** Yoonsu Kim, Hyoungwook Jin, Hayeon Doh, Eunhye Kim, Dongyun Jung, Seungju Kim, Kiyoon Choi, Jinho Son, Juho Kim (KAIST, Univ. of Michigan, Ewha Womans Univ., AlgorithmLabs)

## Abstract

Students' handwritten math work offers rich diagnostic signals for cognitive skills, capturing intermediate reasoning beyond final answers. This paper systematically evaluates 18 large language models on MathCog, a novel expert-crafted benchmark comprising 3,036 diagnostic verdicts across 639 student responses to 110 math problems. Key findings: (1) all LLMs underperform (F1 < 0.5) regardless of capability; (2) performance degrades sharply under vague evidence; (3) models systematically over-attribute evidential strength, overthink minimal cues, and hallucinate nonexistent evidence.

## Dataset: MathCog

- Source: AI-Hub Korean middle-school handwritten math responses
- 12 topics, 110 problems, 639 student responses, 3,036 diagnostic verdicts
- 15 middle school teachers (avg 6.1 years experience), 3 teachers per response
- TIMSS 2019 cognitive domains: Knowing (Recall, Recognize, Classify/Order, Compute, Retrieve, Measure) and Applying (Determine, Represent/Model, Implement)
- Diagnostic dimensions: Correctness x Evidential Strength (Evident/Vague)

## Models Evaluated (18 total)

Reasoning: DeepSeek-R1, Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking, o1-Preview
Multimodal: Claude-3.5-Sonnet-img, Gemini-1.5-Flash-img, GPT-4o-img
Large text: Claude-3.5-Sonnet, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-405B
Medium: Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B
Small: Gemini-1.5-Flash, Gemini-1.5-Flash-8B, GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B

## Key Results

Maximum macro F1 < 0.5 (best: GPT-4o-img = 0.448, DeepSeek-R1 = 0.442)
Mean accuracy = 0.680 (SD = 0.050)
Evidence Over-Attribution: M = 0.580 (58% of vague cases misclassified as Evident)
Evidence False-Attribution: M = 0.585 (58.5% of incorrect diagnoses asserted Evident)
Multimodal models modestly outperformed; reasoning models showed no systematic advantage

## Error Patterns

1. Evidence source misidentification
2. Rubric misinterpretation
3. Over-inference from minimal cues
4. Consistency issues (contradictory verdicts)
5. Hallucinated evidence
