# Practitioner Beliefs and Behaviors in AI-Enhanced Education: DOT Framework Survey Evidence

**Authors:** David Gibson (Curtin University), M. Elizabeth Azukas (Georgia Institute of Technology), Gerald Knezek (University of North Texas)
**Submitted:** 27 May 2026
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2605.29041

## Abstract

This study reports findings from a cross-sectional survey (n = 72) of higher education practitioners examining beliefs, behaviors, and institutional conditions related to artificial intelligence (AI) integration in teaching and learning. Grounded in the DOT Framework, which integrates design thinking and open systems theory, the study investigates AI familiarity, usage patterns, design-oriented practices, and pedagogical beliefs. Exploratory factor analysis of 19 belief items identified a three-factor structure: AI Functional Capabilities, Oversight and Governance, and Instructor Collaboration and Planning (α = .90). Results indicate that practitioners hold favorable views of AI as a pedagogical support while maintaining strong commitments to human oversight and critical evaluation. Reported practices emphasize iterative prompting and content generation, with less consistent use of needs assessment and feedback loops. Institutional barriers including limited policy, training, and infrastructure were widely reported. These findings provide preliminary empirical support for the DOT Framework as a descriptive model of practitioner beliefs and practices, while also highlighting gaps between design-oriented theory and current implementation.

## Key Findings

- **Three-Factor Belief Structure**: EFA of 19 items → AI Functional Capabilities (α=.91), Oversight & Governance (α=.79), Instructor Collaboration & Planning (α=.85); overall α=.90
- **Favorable AI Views + Strong Oversight Commitment**: Practitioners see AI as pedagogical support but demand human oversight and critical evaluation
- **Practice Gaps**: Iterative prompting and content generation common; needs assessment and feedback loops underused
- **Institutional Barriers**: Widespread lack of policy, training, and infrastructure
- **DOT Framework Validation**: Preliminary empirical support for Design Thinking + Open Systems framework as descriptive model

## DOT Framework

Combines **Design Thinking** (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test) with **Open Systems Model** (Environment, Input, Process, Structure, Output, Feedback) to guide AI-enhanced teaching transformation at both micro (classroom) and macro (institution) levels.

### AI as Co-Intelligent Collaborator
Four operational tenets:
1. AI is fallible — always review outputs
2. AI supports, not replaces, higher-order thinking
3. Instructors should actively experiment to understand AI capabilities/limitations
4. Instructor maintains agency; AI is advisory and generative, not authoritative

## Method
- Cross-sectional survey, n=72 higher-ed practitioners
- 27 multi-item questions across six domains
- 19 Likert-scale belief items (1-5) with expert review
- Exploratory factor analysis
- Sample biased toward engaged AI users (54% full-time faculty, 25% advanced AI users)

## Contributions
1. Initial measurement structure for practitioner beliefs in AI-enhanced education
2. Empirical evidence for DOT Framework utility
3. Identified gaps for confirmatory validation and outcome-based research
