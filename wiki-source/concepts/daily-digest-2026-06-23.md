## Ingested Papers

- **Test-Driven, AI-Assisted Learning: Replacing Lectures with Weekly Closed-Book Tests** (Jin-Guo Liu, Shang-Qi Lu, Xin-Ran Shi, Long-Li Zheng, Wei Wang, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.23315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23315) — A lecture-free CS course with AI-assisted weekly closed-book tests maintained accountability and was scalable with a version-controlled AI agent workspace. [[test-driven-ai-assisted-learning]]
- **The Correct Answer Trap: Pedagogically-Grounded Detection and Feedback for Hidden Misconceptions** (Moiz Imran, Sahan Bulathwela, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.23205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23205) — Fine-tuned classifiers detect only 57% of hidden student misconceptions, while reasoning models reach 84% but at 8:1 false alarms — a detect-verify-escalate pipeline with diagnostic follow-up questions is proposed. [[correct-answer-trap-misconceptions]]
- **Do Gains from Generative AI-Enabled Adaptive Pretesting Persist? Evidence from a Retention Study** (Mahir Akgun, Sacip Toker, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.22328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22328) — Adaptive AI pretesting boosts initial learning, but long-term retention over 7 weeks depends on subsequent retrieval practice — learner-directed AI study underperforms structured retrieval. [[adaptive-pretesting-retention]]
- **Framing the 5% Problem: Teachers' Perspectives on Persistence in Educational Technology** (Conrad Borchers, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.22294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22294) — Teachers identify four dimensions of low persistence in adaptive math platforms (motivation, cognitive roadblocks, resilience, context) and prioritize diagnosis over aggregate metrics. [[framing-5-percent-problem-teachers-persistence]]
- **WIP: Bridging the Gap Between Instructional Design and Pedagogical Use: A Framework for Mathematics Educators** (Estefany Castillo Ventura, Javier Ulises Solis Lastra, Anarosa Alves Franco Brandão, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.20934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20934) — Proposes operationalizing learning theories as metadata dimensions for teacher-support systems in mathematics education, focusing on conceptual structure of content. [[bridging-instructional-design-framework-math]]
- **Analysis and Prediction of At-Risk Students Using Machine Learning Algorithms** (Soheila Gheisari, Hamid Salarian, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.20617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20617) — Logistic regression and linear SVM achieve highest accuracy for predicting student attrition using academic, demographic, and enrollment data. [[at-risk-students-ml-prediction]]
- **Estimating Learners' Skill Acquisition Without Temporal Information** (Ryosuke Nagai, Kyohei Atarashi, Koh Takeuchi, Jill-Jênn Vie, Hisashi Kashima, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.20611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20611) — A neural framework predicts next-skill acquisition from snapshot CDM data using pseudo-temporal ordering via inclusion relations among skill sets. [[skill-acquisition-without-temporal-info]]
- **CourseBlueprint: A Structured Pipeline for Adaptive Pedagogical Video Generation Grounded in Course Corpora** (Md Zabirul Islam, Md Motaleb Hossen Manik, Ge Wang, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.20608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20608) — Explicit typed instructional contracts (scaffolding, adaptive controller, engagement contract, slide override) drastically improve pedagogical video quality over fluent but PCK-absent text-to-video. [[courseblueprint-adaptive-video-generation]]
- **Why Machines Misread Pedagogical Quality: Human-Machine Alignment in LLM-Based Pretest Question Evaluation** (Pei-Yu Tseng, Mahir Akgun, Peng Liu, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.23629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23629) — Human-machine disagreements in AI pretest evaluation are systematic; rubric revision has a larger alignment effect than rationale-first evaluation, and the two are complementary. [[machines-misread-pedagogical-quality]]
- **Students' Perception Accuracy of Partners' AI Use and its Relation to Collaboration Performance** (Laura Graf, Ramona Beinstingel, Stephan Kusche, Oleksandra Poquet, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.23237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23237) — Misalignment in partners' beliefs about each other's AI use early in a project predicts lower final scores — especially for low-performing teams — and face-to-face sessions don't fix it. [[student-perception-ai-use-collaboration]]
- **Supporting Tutors in the Gig Economy with Automated Feedback: A Case Study on Ringle** (Yeon Su Park, Sieun Kim, Keighley Overbay, Seoyoung Kim, Sewook Wee, Daho Jung, Juho Kim, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.22609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22609) — Tutors perceived AI feedback more negatively than learner feedback but found it useful for self-monitoring; discrepancies between the two sources caused confusion. [[tutors-gig-economy-automated-feedback]]
- **What Changes When the Interlocutor Is an AI? Interactional Fluency and Linguistic Uptake in L2 Spoken Dialogue** (Russell Scheinberg, Ameeta Agrawal, Tetyana Sydorenko, Kalab Kahsay, Nina Vyatkina, Griet Boone, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.22225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22225) — AI dialogue in L2 practice resembles 'supported monologue' with fewer turns but greater short-term linguistic uptake; human dialogue is faster and more balanced. [[ai-interlocutor-l2-spoken-dialogue]]
- **Curiosity as Linguistic Intervention: Using LLM Tutoring Dialogues to Influence Exploratory Learning Behavior** (Gevindu Ganganath, Pasindu Bolonghege, Qianru Lyu, Pradeep Varakantham, Thivya Kandappu, 2026) — arXiv:[2606.22349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22349) — Curiosity-oriented LLM interventions (novelty, complexity, conflict, uncertainty) increased exploratory learner behaviors up to 2.4x — acting as a partially independent interaction-level mechanism. [[curiobot-llm-tutoring-exploratory-learning]]
