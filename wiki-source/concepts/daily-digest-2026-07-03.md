### Automated Grading of Linux/Bash Examinations Using Large Language Models
**Alonso-Carracedo et al. (2026)** — arXiv:2607.02432 — [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02432)

LLM-based grading achieves high agreement with human graders for Linux/bash command-line exams using a four-level cognitive taxonomy. Scalable approach addresses rising enrolment challenges in computing education.

### Data Comics for Education: Evaluating Effectiveness, Benefits, and the Ethics of AI-Assisted Creation
**Shan et al. (2026)** — arXiv:2607.02361 — [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02361)

AI-assisted data comics improve student engagement and visualization comprehension. Ethical considerations include authorship attribution, accuracy of AI-generated visuals, and potential for misleading representations in educational materials.

[[data-comics-for-education-evaluating-effectiveness-benefits-ethics]]

### From Answer Generators to Reasoning Facilitators: Designing AI Tutors for Mathematical Reasoning in High-Stakes Environments
**Feng, Tian & Zhao (2026)** — arXiv:2607.01692 — [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01692)

Presents a design framework for AI tutors that scaffold mathematical reasoning rather than providing direct answers, specifically for high-stakes exam environments. Usability study demonstrates improved learning outcomes when AI acts as reasoning facilitator.

[[from-answer-generators-to-reasoning-facilitators-ai-tutors]]

### Mind the Trust Gap: Identifying (Mis)alignments in Teacher-Student Views Toward Control and Agency in K-12 Classroom AI
**Nagashima et al. (2026)** — arXiv:2607.01506 — [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01506)

Identifies significant misalignments between teacher and student perceptions of control and agency in K-12 classroom AI systems. Students desire more autonomy while teachers prioritize oversight, creating a trust gap that must be addressed for effective AI integration.

[[mind-the-trust-gap-teacher-student-views-control-agency-k12-classroom-ai]]

### Constructing Epistemic AI Literacy: Detecting Epistemic Aims and Processes in Student-AI Co-Programming
**Wu (2026)** — arXiv:2607.00211 — [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00211)

Introduces a framework for detecting epistemic aims and processes in student-AI co-programming. Students engage in constructing questions, evaluating AI outputs, and integrating them into solutions — revealing the epistemic thinking patterns central to AI literacy.

[[constructing-epistemic-ai-literacy-student-ai-co-programming]]

### Evaluating Interactivity: Toward Automated Assessment of AI-Generated Explorable Explanations
**Wang, Wang & Wen (2026)** — arXiv:2606.31012 — [Full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31012)

Proposes an automated framework for assessing interaction quality in AI-generated explorable explanations. Addresses the gap between rapid LLM-based content generation and the need for pedagogically sound interactive learning materials.

[[evaluating-interactivity-automated-assessment-ai-generated-explorable-explanations]]
